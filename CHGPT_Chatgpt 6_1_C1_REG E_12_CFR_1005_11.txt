Business requirements: Regulation E error resolution
Card and ACH Disputes Platform | 12 CFR § 1005.11
Regulatory compliance
Functional requirements
US banking

1. Business objective
Implement a centralized Regulation E error-resolution capability within the bank's card and ACH disputes platform to ensure that all qualifying electronic fund transfer (EFT) errors are:
- Captured and acknowledged within the applicable regulatory requirements.
- Investigated within prescribed timeframes.
- Provisionally credited when required.
- Resolved with appropriate customer notifications.
- Corrected financially when an error is confirmed.
- Documented with a complete, auditable investigation history.
The platform must support debit card and ACH disputes, including unauthorized transactions, incorrect transaction amounts, duplicate transactions, missing credits, and other qualifying EFT errors.

Regulatory scope clarification: Regulation E applies to covered consumer EFT accounts and transactions. Debit card disputes and consumer ACH disputes may fall within scope. Credit card billing disputes generally fall under Regulation Z and are outside the scope of these requirements unless separately addressed.
2. Regulatory requirements and business rules
The following requirements establish the baseline against which the user stories and acceptance criteria should be tested.
Requirement	Regulatory expectation
Error notification	Consumer generally must notify the institution within 60 days after the institution sends the periodic statement first reflecting the error.
Investigation	Complete investigation within 10 business days of receiving an error notice.
Extended investigation	Generally, up to 45 calendar days, subject to applicable exceptions and provisional credit requirements.
New accounts	Initial investigation period may extend to 20 business days.
Extended investigation exceptions	Up to 90 calendar days for qualifying POS debit card, foreign-initiated EFT, and new-account cases.
Provisional credit	Generally required within 10 business days when the investigation cannot be completed, subject to the applicable new-account exception.
New-account provisional credit	Generally within 20 business days.
Provisional credit availability	Customer must have full use of provisionally credited funds during the investigation.
Final determination	Customer must receive the investigation outcome within applicable timeframes.
Error correction	Confirmed errors must be corrected within one business day after determining an error occurred.
Written explanation	If no error is found, provide a written explanation and advise the customer of the right to request supporting documentation.
Supporting documentation	Provide requested supporting documentation within the applicable regulatory timeframe.

Important implementation distinction: The platform must distinguish calendar days from business days. It must also distinguish the date the bank receives an error notice from the date a dispute is entered into the system. These dates are not necessarily identical.

The business rules should be validated against the current regulation and official interpretations by Legal and Compliance before production implementation.

3. User stories and Gherkin acceptance criteria
Epic 1: Dispute intake and regulatory classification
US-001: Capture and register a Regulation E error notice
User story

As a disputes operations user, I want the platform to capture and register consumer error notices for debit card and ACH transactions, so that the bank can initiate the Regulation E error-resolution process using the correct receipt date.

Business rules
- Accept error notices through supported channels, including telephone, branch, online banking, mobile banking, and written correspondence.
- Record the date and time the institution receives the notice.
- Preserve the original notice and supporting customer statements.
- An oral notice must be treated as a valid error notice; written submission must not be universally required.
Acceptance criteria
Feature: Register Regulation E error notices

  Scenario: Register an error notice received through online banking
    Given a consumer reports an unauthorized ACH transaction
    When the consumer submits the dispute through online banking
    Then the platform shall create a unique dispute reference
    And record the date and time of receipt
    And record the transaction details
    And classify the dispute as a Regulation E error notice
    And initiate the regulatory deadline calculation

  Scenario: Register an oral error notice
    Given a consumer contacts the bank by telephone
    And reports an incorrect debit card transaction amount
    When the representative records the error notice
    Then the platform shall register the dispute
    And capture the oral notice receipt date
    And record the communication channel
    And not reject the notice solely because it was not submitted in writing

  Scenario: Prevent duplicate dispute registration
    Given an existing dispute exists for the same consumer
    And the same transaction is reported again
    When the new notice is submitted
    Then the platform shall identify a potential duplicate
    And allow an authorized user to associate the notice
        with the existing dispute
    And preserve the original and subsequent notice timestamps


US-002: Classify the reported error under Regulation E
User story

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to classify reported transactions into Regulation E error categories, so that each dispute follows the appropriate investigation workflow.

Business rules

Supported error categories should include:
1. Unauthorized EFT.
2. Incorrect EFT amount.
3. Incorrect EFT date.
4. EFT not reflected in account statement.
5. Incorrect EFT to or from the consumer's account.
6. Consumer request for additional information or clarification.
7. Bookkeeping or computational error relating to an EFT.
8. Other qualifying EFT errors under § 1005.11(a).
Acceptance criteria
Feature: Classify Regulation E errors

  Scenario Outline: Classify an EFT error
    Given a consumer reports a "<reported_error>"
    When the dispute is submitted
    Then the platform shall assign the category "<error_category>"
    And apply the Regulation E investigation workflow
    And retain the original customer description

    Examples:
      | reported_error                  | error_category       |
      | Unauthorized ACH debit          | Unauthorized EFT     |
      | Debit card charged twice        | Duplicate EFT        |
      | Incorrect debit transaction     | Incorrect amount     |
      | Expected ACH credit not posted  | Missing EFT credit   |
      | Incorrect transaction date     | Incorrect date       |


US-003: Determine Regulation E applicability
User story

As a compliance operations user, I want the platform to determine whether a transaction and account are subject to Regulation E, so that covered disputes are processed using the appropriate regulatory workflow.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Determine Regulation E applicability

  Scenario: Consumer debit card transaction
    Given the transaction is a debit card EFT
    And the account is a covered consumer account
    When the dispute is submitted
    Then the platform shall identify the dispute as
         potentially subject to Regulation E

  Scenario: Consumer ACH transaction
    Given the transaction is a consumer ACH debit or credit
    And the account is a covered consumer account
    When the dispute is submitted
    Then the platform shall evaluate the dispute
         under Regulation E

  Scenario: Commercial account transaction
    Given the transaction is associated with a commercial account
    When the dispute is submitted
    Then the platform shall identify the account
         as outside the standard consumer Regulation E scope
    And route the case for the applicable commercial
        dispute process

  Scenario: Credit card transaction
    Given the reported transaction is a credit card transaction
    When the dispute is submitted
    Then the platform shall route the case
         to the applicable Regulation Z workflow
    And shall not automatically apply Regulation E deadlines


US-004: Calculate the 60-day consumer notification period
User story

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to calculate the consumer notification period based on the first periodic statement reflecting the alleged error, so that the bank can assess the timeliness of the consumer's notice.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Calculate consumer notification period

  Scenario: Notice received within 60 days
    Given the first periodic statement reflecting the error
          was sent to the consumer on January 5
    And the consumer reports the error on February 20
    When the platform calculates the notification period
    Then the platform shall identify the notice
         as received within 60 days

  Scenario: Notice received after 60 days
    Given the first periodic statement reflecting the error
          was sent to the consumer more than 60 days ago
    When the consumer reports the error
    Then the platform shall flag the notice
         as potentially outside the 60-day period
    And route the case for regulatory assessment
    And shall not automatically reject the dispute

  Scenario: Statement date is unavailable
    Given the consumer reports a potential EFT error
    And the first statement reflecting the error
        cannot be identified
    When the dispute is registered
    Then the platform shall flag the statement date
         as missing
    And create an operational follow-up task
    And shall not assume the consumer notification period expired


Important: The 60-day period is relevant to the consumer's potential liability for subsequent unauthorized transfers and certain error-resolution protections. It must not be implemented as an automatic denial rule for every late-reported dispute. Applicable liability rules under § 1005.6 and other requirements must be considered separately.

  Scenario: New account dispute

  Scenario: Regulatory deadline approaching

  Scenario: Deadline missed

    When the deadline monitoring process runs

Acceptance criteria

US-009: Issue provisional credit
User story

As a disputes operations user, I want the platform to initiate provisional credit for eligible disputes, so that the consumer has access to funds while the investigation continues.

Acceptance criteria

4. Dispute lifecycle and status model
The platform should implement a consistent status model across debit card and ACH disputes.
Regulation E dispute lifecycle
1. Dispute Received
   
   Consumer error notice captured; receipt date established.
2. Regulatory Assessment
   
   Account eligibility, transaction scope, error category, and notice timing evaluated.
3. Investigation Pending
   
   Investigator assigned; regulatory deadlines calculated.
4. Investigation in Progress
   
   Transaction evidence and supporting documentation collected.
5. Provisional Credit Pending / Issued
   
   Credit eligibility evaluated and applicable credit posted.
6. Investigation Completed
   
   Error confirmed or no-error determination recorded.
7. Final Correction / Reversal Pending
   
   Financial correction or provisional credit reversal processed as applicable.
8. Customer Notification Pending / Sent
   
   Final determination communicated to consumer.
9. Resolved / Closed
   
   Financial adjustments, communications, and required documentation completed.

Recommended additional exception statuses:
- Regulatory Deadline At Risk
- Regulatory Deadline Breached
- Provisional Credit Failed
- Investigation Escalated
- Written Confirmation Pending
- Supporting Documentation Pending
- Customer Notification Failed
- Financial Adjustment Failed
A dispute should not be considered fully resolved simply because an investigator has recorded a determination. Financial corrections, required customer communications, and outstanding regulatory obligations must also be considered.
5. Regulatory deadline configuration matrix
The following matrix should be implemented as configurable business rules rather than hardcoded workflow logic.
Activity	Standard case	Applicable exception
Initial investigation	10 business days	20 business days for qualifying new accounts
Extended investigation	45 calendar days	90 calendar days for qualifying cases
Provisional credit	Within 10 business days	Within 20 business days for qualifying new accounts
Provisional credit notification	Within 2 business days after credit	Same applicable requirement
Final investigation results	Within 3 business days after completion	Same applicable requirement
Error correction	Within 1 business day after determination	Same applicable requirement
No-error supporting documents	Within 3 business days of request	Same applicable requirement
Provisional credit reversal notice	At least 5 business days before debit	Subject to applicable regulatory conditions

Calendar and business-day calculation requirements
- Business days must exclude Saturdays, Sundays, and applicable federal holidays.
- Calendar-day deadlines must include weekends and holidays.
- The system must use the bank's approved business calendar.
- Each deadline must be calculated using the correct regulatory triggering event.
- Deadline calculations must be retained for audit purposes.
- Changes to regulatory parameters must be version-controlled and effective-dated.

11. Business analyst recommendation
For implementation, I would make one architectural decision particularly important: build a centralized, configurable Regulation E rules and deadline engine rather than embedding regulatory logic separately in the card and ACH dispute workflows.

This provides several benefits:
- One source of truth for regulatory deadline calculations.
- Consistent provisional credit eligibility across channels.
- Reduced risk of inconsistent handling of debit card and ACH disputes.
- Easier implementation of regulatory changes.
- Centralized compliance monitoring and reporting.
- Better auditability across the entire dispute lifecycle.
I would also make regulatory requirements independently testable through automated test scenarios, particularly for boundary conditions involving weekends, holidays, new accounts, oral notices, provisional credit, and 45-/90-day investigation periods.

Definition of Done: A Regulation E dispute is considered successfully implemented when the platform can demonstrate end-to-end processing from error notice receipt through investigation, provisional credit where required, final determination, financial correction or reversal, customer notification, and complete audit evidence.

These are proposed business requirements, not a legal opinion. Legal and Compliance should validate the detailed interpretations and operational rules before sign-off.
