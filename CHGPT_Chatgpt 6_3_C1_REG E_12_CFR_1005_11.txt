Regulation E Error Resolution – User Stories & Gherkin Acceptance Criteria
Business initiative: Implement Regulation E error resolution requirements in the Card and ACH Disputes Platform.

Regulatory reference: 12 CFR § 1005.11 – Procedures for resolving errors.

Business perspective: U.S. Bank – Consumer Payments, Debit Card Operations, ACH Operations, Disputes, and Regulatory Compliance.

Objective: Implement a consistent, auditable, and configurable error-resolution framework that enables the bank to receive, classify, investigate, resolve, and communicate consumer electronic funds transfer (EFT) disputes within Regulation E timelines.

The requirements below are written in a format suitable for:
- Product backlog and Agile delivery.
- Business requirements and functional specifications.
- Jira epics, features, and user stories.
- QA test scenarios and automation.
- Compliance and operational control validation.
1. Scope and regulatory considerations
The platform must support Regulation E error resolution for applicable consumer electronic fund transfers, including:
Transaction type	In scope
Debit card transactions	Yes
ATM transactions	Yes
ACH debit transactions	Yes
ACH credit transactions	Yes, where applicable
Recurring electronic transfers	Yes
Prepaid account EFTs	Where Regulation E applies
Credit card transactions	No – generally governed by Regulation Z

Important distinction: This specification addresses consumer accounts and applicable EFTs. Commercial accounts and business ACH disputes may be subject to different contractual and legal requirements.

The platform should distinguish between:
1. Error identification and intake.
2. Regulatory investigation timelines.
3. Provisional credit.
4. Investigation and resolution.
5. Consumer notification.
6. Final credit or debit adjustments.
7. Regulatory audit evidence and reporting.
The requirements should be validated by Legal and Compliance before production implementation, particularly for account eligibility, transaction types, and exceptions.
2. Regulatory rules translated into system requirements
The following are the principal regulatory requirements under 12 CFR § 1005.11.
Regulatory requirement	System requirement
Consumer reports an error	Capture and register the error notice
Notice generally within 60 days of statement transmittal	Calculate and monitor reporting eligibility
Oral or written notification	Support both intake methods
Written confirmation may be requested following oral notice	Track written confirmation and deadline
Investigation within 10 business days	Start and monitor investigation SLA
Provisional credit when investigation cannot be completed within 10 business days	Automatically initiate provisional credit, subject to permitted exceptions
Extended investigation period	Support 45-calendar-day general timeframe
New account exception	Support 20-business-day initial investigation period
Certain extended cases	Support 90-calendar-day investigation period
Provisional credit availability	Ensure consumer can use credited funds
Written notification of results	Generate and deliver investigation outcome
Correction of confirmed error	Correct within one business day after determining an error occurred
Investigation results	Provide results within three business days after completion

Critical timing distinction: The 10-, 20-, 45-, and 90-day periods are not interchangeable. The platform must select the appropriate regulatory timeline based on the circumstances of the EFT and account.

For extended investigations, the 45- or 90-day period is generally available only when the applicable provisional-credit requirements are satisfied. The special 20-business-day initial period applies to qualifying new accounts.
3. Proposed Agile epic structure
EPIC 1
Dispute intake and error classification

Capture consumer notices and identify qualifying Regulation E errors.


EPIC 2
Regulatory clock and SLA management

Calculate deadlines, identify exceptions, and prevent regulatory breaches.


EPIC 3
Provisional credit management

Calculate, issue, track, and reverse provisional credits where permitted.


EPIC 4
Investigation and error determination

Support investigation workflows and evidence-based decisions.


EPIC 5
Dispute resolution and account correction

Process confirmed errors and appropriate account adjustments.


EPIC 6
Consumer notifications

Generate compliant notices and delivery evidence.


EPIC 7
Audit, reporting, and compliance monitoring

Maintain an auditable history and identify potential violations.


4. Detailed user stories and Gherkin acceptance criteria
EPIC 1 – Dispute intake and error classification
US-001: Capture consumer error notification
User story

As a disputes operations associate, I want to capture a consumer's notification of an EFT error, so that the bank can initiate the Regulation E error-resolution process.

Business requirements
- Support disputes submitted through phone, branch, online banking, mobile banking, and written correspondence.
- Capture oral and written notices.
- Capture date and time the institution receives the notification.
- Capture consumer identification and affected account.
- Capture transaction date, amount, transaction type, and description of the alleged error.
- Assign a unique dispute reference number.
Acceptance criteria
Feature: Regulation E dispute intake

  Scenario: Consumer reports an unauthorized debit card transaction
    Given a consumer has an eligible deposit account
    And the consumer identifies an unauthorized debit card transaction
    When the disputes associate records the notification
    Then the system shall create a Regulation E dispute
    And capture the transaction amount and date
    And capture the notification receipt date
    And assign a unique dispute reference number
    And classify the dispute as an alleged unauthorized EFT

  Scenario: Consumer reports an ACH transaction error
    Given a consumer identifies an incorrect ACH debit
    When the dispute is submitted
    Then the system shall capture the ACH transaction details
    And classify the dispute as an ACH error
    And initiate the Regulation E error-resolution workflow

  Scenario: Consumer reports an error orally
    Given a consumer contacts the bank by telephone
    When the consumer provides the required information
    Then the system shall accept the oral notification
    And record the date of oral notification
    And identify the notification channel as oral
    And initiate applicable regulatory timelines

  Scenario: Consumer submits written notification
    Given a consumer submits a written error notification
    When the notification is received
    Then the system shall record the receipt date
    And identify the notification channel as written
    And initiate applicable regulatory timelines


US-002: Classify Regulation E error types
User story

As a disputes analyst, I want the system to classify alleged errors according to Regulation E, so that disputes are routed to the appropriate investigation process.

Error categories to support
1. Unauthorized EFT.
2. Incorrect EFT amount.
3. EFT not properly identified.
4. EFT not made to the recipient designated by the consumer.
5. Bookkeeping or computational error.
6. Omission of an EFT from a periodic statement.
7. Request for additional documentation or clarification.
8. Consumer request for information regarding preauthorized transfers.
Acceptance criteria
Feature: Regulation E error classification

  Scenario Outline: Classify qualifying error types
    Given a consumer reports an EFT issue
    When the analyst selects "<error_type>"
    Then the system shall classify the dispute as "<classification>"
    And associate the dispute with the Regulation E workflow

    Examples:
      | error_type                         | classification             |
      | Unauthorized EFT                   | Unauthorized transaction   |
      | Incorrect transaction amount       | Incorrect amount           |
      | Incorrect transaction recipient    | Incorrect recipient        |
      | Transaction omitted from statement | Omitted EFT                |
      | Incorrect account posting          | Bookkeeping error          |
      | Additional transaction information | Documentation request      |

  Scenario: Consumer requests transaction status only
    Given a consumer requests the status of a previously authorized EFT
    When the analyst records the inquiry
    Then the system shall classify the request as a general inquiry
    And shall not automatically classify it as a Regulation E error
    Unless additional information establishes an alleged error


US-003: Determine the 60-day notification period
User story

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to calculate the consumer's notification period based on the periodic statement date, so that the bank can identify disputes reported within the applicable Regulation E timeframe.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Regulation E notification period

  Scenario: Consumer reports an error within 60 days
    Given a periodic statement containing the disputed EFT was transmitted
    And the statement transmission date is recorded
    When the consumer reports the error within 60 days
    Then the system shall identify the notification as within the
         Regulation E notification period
    And continue the error-resolution workflow

  Scenario: Consumer reports an error after 60 days
    Given a periodic statement containing the disputed EFT was transmitted
    When the consumer reports the error more than 60 days later
    Then the system shall flag the notification for review
    And display the applicable statement transmission date
    And shall not automatically reject the dispute
    And shall route the case for an appropriate eligibility assessment

  Scenario: Multiple periodic statements exist
    Given the disputed EFT appears on a periodic statement
    When the system evaluates the notification period
    Then the system shall use the applicable statement transmission date
    And calculate the deadline using the regulatory counting rules


Important business rule: The 60-day period is generally measured from the date the institution transmits the periodic statement first reflecting the alleged error, not simply from the transaction date.

The system must not automatically reject late-reported claims without evaluating applicable exceptions and other legal obligations.
US-004: Request written confirmation of oral notification
User story

As a disputes operations associate, I want the system to request written confirmation when permitted following oral notification, so that the bank can satisfy its procedural requirements.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Written confirmation of oral notice

  Scenario: Written confirmation is requested
    Given a consumer reports an error orally
    And the bank elects to require written confirmation
    When the dispute is registered
    Then the system shall generate a written confirmation request
    And record the request date
    And calculate the 10-business-day confirmation deadline
    And notify the consumer of the confirmation requirement

  Scenario: Written confirmation is received within 10 business days
    Given written confirmation has been requested
    When the consumer submits written confirmation within
         10 business days
    Then the system shall record the confirmation receipt date
    And continue the normal investigation process

  Scenario: Written confirmation is not received
    Given written confirmation has been requested
    And the 10-business-day confirmation period has expired
    When no written confirmation has been received
    Then the system may withhold provisional credit where
         permitted by Regulation E
    And shall record the reason
    And shall not automatically terminate the investigation


Compliance note: The bank may require written confirmation within 10 business days following oral notice. If the consumer does not provide it, the bank may not be required to provide provisional credit, subject to the regulatory conditions. This does not eliminate the obligation to investigate.

EPIC 2 – Regulatory clock and SLA management
US-005: Calculate the initial 10-business-day investigation deadline
User story

As a disputes operations associate, I want the system to calculate the initial investigation deadline, so that all Regulation E disputes are investigated within the required timeframe.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Initial Regulation E investigation deadline

  Scenario: Calculate the 10-business-day deadline
    Given a valid Regulation E error notification is received
    When the dispute is created
    Then the system shall calculate the 10-business-day deadline
    And use the institution's applicable business-day calendar
    And exclude Saturdays, Sundays, and federal holidays
    And display the deadline on the dispute record

  Scenario: Investigation completed within 10 business days
    Given the dispute is under investigation
    When the bank determines the outcome within 10 business days
    Then the system shall record the investigation completion date
    And proceed to the applicable resolution workflow
    And shall not require provisional credit solely because
         an investigation extension was not needed

  Scenario: Investigation remains incomplete on the 10th business day
    Given the investigation is not completed within 10 business days
    And no applicable exception removes the provisional credit requirement
    When the 10-business-day deadline is reached
    Then the system shall trigger the provisional credit workflow
    And flag the dispute for regulatory SLA monitoring


US-006: Support the extended 45-calendar-day investigation period
User story

As a disputes manager, I want the system to support the extended investigation period, so that qualifying investigations can continue beyond the initial 10-business-day period without violating Regulation E.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Extended investigation period

  Scenario: Qualifying investigation requires additional time
    Given a Regulation E investigation cannot be completed
         within 10 business days
    And the bank qualifies for the extended investigation period
    And required provisional credit has been provided
    When the extension is applied
    Then the system shall establish the applicable
         45-calendar-day investigation deadline
    And record the extension reason
    And monitor the extended deadline

  Scenario: Extension is attempted without required provisional credit
    Given an investigation cannot be completed within 10 business days
    And provisional credit has not been provided
    And no applicable exception permits withholding provisional credit
    When an associate attempts to extend the investigation
    Then the system shall prevent the extension
    And display the outstanding provisional credit requirement

  Scenario: Investigation is approaching the extended deadline
    Given an investigation has an active extended deadline
    When the deadline is within the configured escalation threshold
    Then the system shall notify the assigned investigator
    And notify the disputes supervisor
    And flag the dispute for expedited resolution


US-007: Support the new-account 20-business-day initial period
User story

As a disputes operations associate, I want the system to identify qualifying new accounts and apply the appropriate initial investigation period, so that the bank applies the correct regulatory timeline.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: New account investigation period

  Scenario: Qualifying new account dispute
    Given the consumer account meets the Regulation E new-account criteria
    When the consumer reports an EFT error
    Then the system shall identify the account as qualifying
    And calculate the applicable 20-business-day initial
         investigation deadline
    And display the account qualification reason

  Scenario: Existing account dispute
    Given the consumer account does not meet new-account criteria
    When the dispute is created
    Then the system shall apply the standard 10-business-day
         initial investigation period

  Scenario: New account exception requires additional investigation
    Given the account qualifies for the new-account exception
    And the investigation cannot be completed within
         the applicable initial period
    When the investigation is extended
    Then the system shall evaluate eligibility for the
         applicable 90-calendar-day period
    And enforce required provisional credit conditions


US-008: Support the 90-calendar-day investigation period
User story

As a disputes manager, I want the system to apply the 90-calendar-day investigation period when the transaction qualifies for the specific Regulation E exceptions, so that extended investigations are handled correctly.

Relevant qualifying circumstances include:
- Certain EFTs occurring within 30 days after the first deposit to a new account.
- Certain point-of-sale debit card transactions.
- Certain foreign-initiated EFTs.
Acceptance criteria
Feature: Extended 90-day investigation period

  Scenario: Qualifying POS transaction
    Given the disputed EFT is a qualifying point-of-sale
         debit card transaction
    And the initial investigation cannot be completed
    When the extension eligibility is evaluated
    Then the system shall allow the applicable 90-calendar-day
         investigation period
    And apply the required provisional credit controls

  Scenario: Qualifying foreign-initiated EFT
    Given the disputed EFT was initiated outside the United States
    And the transaction meets the regulatory criteria
    When the dispute is evaluated for an extension
    Then the system shall apply the eligible 90-calendar-day
         investigation period
    And record the eligibility determination

  Scenario: Transaction does not qualify for 90 days
    Given the disputed EFT does not meet the regulatory criteria
    When the system evaluates the requested extension
    Then the system shall not apply the 90-day period
    And shall retain the applicable standard investigation deadline


Implementation note: The 90-day period is subject to the specific conditions in § 1005.11(c)(3), not a general extension available for every debit card or ACH dispute.

EPIC 3 – Provisional credit management
US-009: Issue provisional credit when required
User story

As a disputes operations associate, I want the system to automatically initiate provisional credit when the investigation cannot be completed within the initial regulatory period, so that consumers receive timely access to the disputed funds.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Provisional credit issuance

  Scenario: Provisional credit required after 10 business days
    Given the bank has not completed its investigation
         within the applicable initial period
    And the consumer meets provisional credit eligibility requirements
    When the initial investigation deadline is reached
    Then the system shall initiate provisional credit
    And credit the consumer's account for the amount of the alleged error
    And record the credit transaction reference
    And make the credited funds available for use

  Scenario: Provisional credit is issued
    Given provisional credit is required
    When the credit transaction is successfully posted
    Then the system shall update the dispute status to
         "Provisional Credit Issued"
    And record the posting date and amount
    And retain the transaction reference

  Scenario: Provisional credit posting fails
    Given provisional credit is required
    When the credit transaction fails
    Then the system shall create a high-priority exception
    And notify the responsible operations team
    And prevent the dispute from being marked compliant
         until the issue is resolved


US-010: Calculate the provisional credit amount
User story

As a disputes analyst, I want the system to calculate provisional credit based on the alleged error amount, so that the consumer receives the required amount.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Provisional credit calculation

  Scenario: Full disputed amount is provisionally credited
    Given a consumer disputes an EFT of $500
    And provisional credit is required
    When the credit amount is calculated
    Then the system shall calculate provisional credit of $500
    And shall not apply an unauthorized deductible
    And shall not reduce the credit based on the transaction type

  Scenario: Multiple transactions are disputed
    Given the consumer disputes multiple qualifying EFTs
    When provisional credit is calculated
    Then the system shall calculate the applicable aggregate
         disputed amount
    And maintain individual transaction-level credit references

  Scenario: Partial amount is disputed
    Given a consumer disputes only a portion of a transaction
    When provisional credit is calculated
    Then the system shall use the amount alleged to be erroneous
    And record the consumer's disputed amount


US-011: Notify consumer of provisional credit
User story

As a customer communications specialist, I want the system to notify consumers when provisional credit is issued, so that consumers understand the credit and its availability.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Provisional credit notification

  Scenario: Provisional credit notice generated
    Given provisional credit has been successfully posted
    When the credit transaction is confirmed
    Then the system shall generate a provisional credit notification
    And include the credited amount
    And include the account reference
    And include the dispute reference
    And explain that the credit is provisional

  Scenario: Consumer receives provisional credit
    Given provisional credit has been posted
    When the notice is generated
    Then the system shall initiate delivery within
         the applicable regulatory timeframe
    And record the notice delivery status


Regulatory requirement: When provisional credit is required, the consumer must be given full use of the credited funds during the investigation. The institution must notify the consumer within two business days after provisionally crediting the account.
US-012: Reverse provisional credit following a no-error determination
User story

As a disputes operations associate, I want the system to reverse provisional credit when the investigation establishes that no error occurred, so that the account can be adjusted appropriately.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Provisional credit reversal

  Scenario: No error found after provisional credit
    Given provisional credit was previously issued
    And the investigation determines that no error occurred
    When the final determination is approved
    Then the system shall initiate the applicable
         provisional credit reversal process
    And record the reversal amount
    And retain the original credit transaction reference
    And generate the required consumer notification

  Scenario: Consumer receives advance notice of debit
    Given provisional credit is to be reversed
    When the reversal is scheduled
    Then the system shall provide the required written notice
         at least 5 business days before the debit
    And explain the date and amount of the debit
    And inform the consumer that the institution may honor
         checks, drafts, or preauthorized transfers for 5 business
         days after the debit notwithstanding insufficient funds


Important: The five-business-day advance notice and honoring protection are important controls under § 1005.11(d)(2). The reversal must not be treated as an ordinary adjustment without these safeguards.

User story

As a disputes manager, I want disputes to be assigned to the appropriate investigation team, so that card and ACH errors are investigated by qualified personnel.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Dispute investigation assignment

    Given a dispute is classified as a debit card EFT error

  Scenario: ACH dispute routing

US-014: Capture investigation evidence
User story

As a disputes investigator, I want to capture all investigation activities and supporting evidence, so that the bank can demonstrate that a reasonable investigation was conducted.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Investigation evidence management

  Scenario: Investigator records investigation findings
    Given a dispute is under investigation
    When the investigator adds supporting evidence
    Then the system shall capture the evidence type
    And capture the evidence description
    And capture the upload or activity timestamp
    And associate the evidence with the dispute

  Scenario: ACH transaction investigation
    Given an ACH transaction is disputed
    When the investigator reviews the transaction
    Then the system shall support recording available ACH
         transaction details
    And record relevant authorization information
    And capture the investigator's findings

  Scenario: Debit card transaction investigation
    Given a debit card transaction is disputed
    When the investigator reviews the transaction
    Then the system shall support recording available card
         transaction information
    And capture relevant authorization and transaction evidence
    And record the investigator's findings

  Scenario: Evidence is updated
    Given investigation evidence already exists
    When an investigator updates the evidence
    Then the system shall retain the original evidence history
    And record the user and timestamp of the update


US-015: Determine whether an error occurred
User story

As a disputes investigator, I want to document whether an error occurred, so that the bank can complete the regulatory investigation and determine the appropriate account adjustment.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Error determination

  Scenario: Error is confirmed
    Given the investigation identifies an EFT error
    When the investigator records the determination
    Then the system shall classify the outcome as "Error Confirmed"
    And capture the investigation findings
    And initiate the account correction workflow

  Scenario: No error is identified
    Given the investigation determines that no error occurred
    When the investigator records the determination
    Then the system shall classify the outcome as "No Error"
    And require the investigator to document the reason
    And initiate the applicable consumer notification workflow

  Scenario: Investigation requires additional documentation
    Given the investigation is incomplete
    When additional documentation is required
    Then the system shall record the outstanding evidence
    And continue monitoring the regulatory deadline
    And shall not automatically suspend the regulatory clock


US-016: Provide investigation documentation upon request
User story

As a customer service representative, I want to retrieve relevant documentation supporting the investigation, so that the consumer can receive the information supporting the bank's determination.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Consumer investigation documentation

  Scenario: Consumer requests investigation documentation
    Given the bank has completed an investigation
    And the consumer requests the documents relied upon
    When the request is received
    Then the system shall create a documentation request
    And associate the request with the original dispute
    And retrieve available supporting documentation

  Scenario: No error determination
    Given the investigation determines that no error occurred
    When the final results are prepared
    Then the system shall provide a written explanation
    And inform the consumer that copies of documents
         relied upon in the investigation are available upon request



EPIC 5 – Dispute resolution and account correction
US-017: Correct confirmed EFT errors
User story

As a disputes operations associate, I want the system to correct confirmed EFT errors promptly, so that consumers receive the correct account balance.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Confirmed EFT error correction

  Scenario: Error confirmed without prior provisional credit
    Given the investigation determines that an error occurred
    And provisional credit has not been issued
    When the determination is approved
    Then the system shall initiate the appropriate account correction
    And complete the correction within one business day
         after determining that an error occurred
    And record the correction transaction reference

  Scenario: Error confirmed after provisional credit
    Given provisional credit has already been issued
    And the investigation confirms the error
    When the final determination is approved
    Then the system shall convert the provisional credit
         to a final credit as appropriate
    And ensure the consumer is not credited twice
    And record the final resolution

  Scenario: Confirmed partial error
    Given the investigation confirms only part of the
         disputed amount was erroneous
    When the final determination is approved
    Then the system shall correct the confirmed erroneous amount
    And process any remaining provisional credit under
         the applicable regulatory requirements


US-018: Close disputes only after regulatory requirements are fulfilled
User story

As a disputes manager, I want the system to enforce dispute closure requirements, so that cases cannot be closed before the necessary investigation, correction, and notification activities are completed.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Regulatory dispute closure

  Scenario: Dispute is eligible for closure
    Given the investigation has been completed
    And the final determination has been recorded
    And required account corrections have been processed
    And required consumer notifications have been generated
    When the associate closes the dispute
    Then the system shall permit closure
    And record the final disposition
    And retain the complete audit history

  Scenario: Dispute closure attempted before correction
    Given the investigation confirms an error occurred
    And the required account correction is incomplete
    When the associate attempts to close the dispute
    Then the system shall prevent closure
    And display the outstanding correction requirement

  Scenario: Dispute closure attempted before required notification
    Given the investigation is complete
    And required consumer notification has not been generated
    When the associate attempts to close the dispute
    Then the system shall prevent closure
    And display the outstanding notification requirement

EPIC 7 – Audit, reporting, and compliance monitoring
US-021: Maintain a complete regulatory audit trail
User story

As a compliance officer, I want the platform to maintain a complete and immutable audit trail of all Regulation E dispute activities, so that the bank can demonstrate compliance during internal audits and regulatory examinations.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Regulation E audit trail

  Scenario: Dispute activity is recorded
    Given a Regulation E dispute exists
    When any user performs an action on the dispute
    Then the system shall record the action
    And capture the user ID
    And capture the date and timestamp
    And capture the previous and updated values where applicable

  Scenario: Regulatory deadline changes
    Given a dispute has an established regulatory deadline
    When an authorized user updates a relevant case attribute
    Then the system shall preserve the original deadline
    And record the updated deadline
    And capture the reason for the change
    And record the user responsible

  Scenario: Dispute is closed
    Given a Regulation E dispute is closed
    When the audit record is generated
    Then the system shall retain the complete dispute lifecycle
    And include intake, investigation, credits, adjustments,
        notifications, and final determination

  Scenario: Unauthorized audit record modification
    Given an audit record has been created
    When a user attempts to modify or delete the record
    Then the system shall prevent unauthorized modification
    And record the attempted activity where supported

US-023: Escalate potential regulatory violations
User story

As a disputes supervisor, I want the system to automatically escalate cases approaching or exceeding regulatory deadlines, so that corrective action can be taken before a regulatory violation occurs.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Regulatory deadline escalation

  Scenario: Dispute approaches initial deadline
    Given a dispute is approaching its applicable initial deadline
    When the configured escalation threshold is reached
    Then the system shall notify the assigned investigator
    And notify the disputes supervisor

  Scenario: Dispute reaches regulatory deadline
    Given the dispute has reached its applicable deadline
    And the required action is incomplete
    When the deadline monitoring process executes
    Then the system shall flag the dispute as a regulatory exception
    And create an escalation record

  Scenario: Dispute exceeds regulatory deadline
    Given the dispute has exceeded its applicable deadline
    When the monitoring process executes
    Then the system shall classify the case as overdue
    And notify the appropriate management team
    And retain the exception until resolved

  Scenario: Regulatory exception is resolved
    Given a regulatory exception exists
    When the outstanding requirement is completed
    Then the system shall update the exception status
    And preserve the original breach or exception history
    And record the corrective action


5. Cross-functional business rules
These rules should be implemented as shared platform services rather than independently developed within card and ACH workflows.
Rule ID	Business rule	Expected system behavior

6. Recommended dispute lifecycle and status model
I recommend implementing a common status model across debit card and ACH disputes, with transaction-specific investigation workflows.
Regulation E dispute lifecycle
1. Dispute Received
   
   Consumer notice captured; receipt date established.
2. Eligibility Review
   
   Determine EFT applicability, statement date, and applicable regulatory period.
3. Investigation Initiated
   
   Assign investigator; initiate applicable 10- or 20-business-day clock.
4. Investigation in Progress
   
   Gather evidence and evaluate the alleged error.
5. Provisional Credit Pending / Issued
   
   Issue and verify provisional credit when required.
6. Extended Investigation
   
   Apply eligible 45- or 90-calendar-day timeframe.
7. Investigation Completed
   
   Determine error confirmed or no error.
8. Final Adjustment Pending / Completed
   
   Post corrections or process permitted provisional credit reversal.
9. Final Notification Pending / Sent
   
   Generate and deliver investigation results.
10. Closed
    
    Complete required actions and preserve the audit record.

Recommended exception statuses:
- Regulatory SLA at risk.
- Regulatory SLA breached.
- Provisional credit posting failure.
- Written confirmation pending.
- Consumer notification failure.
- Account adjustment failure.
- Supervisor review required.
These should be exception indicators, not substitutes for the primary dispute status.

13. Important regulatory interpretation decisions before development
There are several areas I would explicitly put through Legal and Compliance review before locking the requirements.

1. Regulation E versus network chargeback rules: Visa, Mastercard, and ACH network procedures must not replace the bank's Regulation E obligations. A network deadline cannot be used to extend a consumer's statutory protection.

2. ACH return processing versus error resolution: An ACH return, including an unauthorized debit return, is not necessarily equivalent to completing the Regulation E investigation. The platform should track the two processes independently.

3. Provisional credit: The system must not make provisional credit contingent on the bank completing its network dispute process.

4. Written confirmation: Failure to receive written confirmation after an oral notice must not automatically result in case rejection or termination of investigation.

5. Late notifications: A notification received after the 60-day statement period requires appropriate assessment. The system should not automatically deny the consumer's claim.

6. Error determination: The system must distinguish between determining whether an EFT error occurred and determining consumer liability under other applicable provisions, including § 1005.6.

7. Business-day calculations: The institution's business-day definition, processing cutoffs, holidays, and time zones need to be explicitly documented and validated.

8. Account types and EFT eligibility: ACH credits, wire transfers, prepaid accounts, and other payment types must be assessed against the Regulation E definition of EFT and applicable exclusions rather than included or excluded solely based on payment rail.

Primary regulatory source: 12 CFR § 1005.11 – Procedures for resolving errors.

Additional references:
- 12 CFR § 1005.6 – Liability of consumer for unauthorized transfers.
- 12 CFR § 1005.2 – Definitions.
My recommendation: Treat this as a single Regulation E compliance initiative with seven epics and a shared regulatory rules engine. Build the initial release around accurate deadline calculation, provisional credit, final account correction, and consumer notifications. These are the most consequential capabilities from a consumer protection and regulatory examination perspective.
