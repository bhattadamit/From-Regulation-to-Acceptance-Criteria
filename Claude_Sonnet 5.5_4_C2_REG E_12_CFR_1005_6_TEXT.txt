# Regulation E Consumer Liability for Unauthorized Transfers (12 CFR 1005.6): User Stories and Acceptance Criteria

**Scope:** The disputes platform determines the maximum consumer liability for an unauthorized electronic fund transfer (EFT), or a series of related unauthorized transfers. Every rule below comes only from the § 1005.6 text you provided.

**Personas:** Disputes Analyst (DA), Compliance Officer (CO)

**Assumptions and open items.** The text doesn't define these, so each story flags them as configuration or open questions rather than inventing rules:
1. "Business day" calendar and cutoff
2. The content of the § 1005.7(b)(1)–(3) disclosures (the platform only records whether they were provided)
3. What counts as an "accepted access device" and a "means to identify the consumer"
4. How the institution "establishes" that later transfers would not have occurred with timely notice
5. State-law and agreement caps (platform needs a data source)
6. What counts as "extenuating circumstances" and a "reasonable period"

---

## US-1: Check conditions for liability (§ 1005.6(a))

**As a** Disputes Analyst, **I want** the platform to confirm the conditions for consumer liability before any amount is assessed, **so that** no consumer is held liable when the institution hasn't met its prerequisites.

```gherkin
Feature: Conditions for consumer liability

  Background:
    Given an unauthorized EFT dispute exists for a consumer account

  Scenario: Liability blocked when required disclosures were not provided
    Given the institution has not provided all disclosures required by §1005.7(b)(1), (2) and (3)
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is $0
    And the determination reason is "Required disclosures not provided"

  Scenario: Liability permitted when disclosures were provided and no access device was involved
    Given the institution provided the disclosures required by §1005.7(b)(1), (2) and (3)
    And the unauthorized transfer did not involve an access device
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the case proceeds to limitation calculation under §1005.6(b)

  Scenario: Liability blocked when the access device was not an accepted access device
    Given the institution provided the required disclosures
    And the unauthorized transfer involved an access device
    And the access device is not recorded as an accepted access device
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is $0
    And the determination reason is "Access device not accepted"

  Scenario: Liability blocked when no means to identify the consumer was provided
    Given the institution provided the required disclosures
    And the unauthorized transfer involved an accepted access device
    And the institution did not provide a means to identify the consumer to whom the device was issued
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is $0
    And the determination reason is "No means of identification provided"

  Scenario: Liability permitted when all access device conditions are met
    Given the institution provided the required disclosures
    And the unauthorized transfer involved an accepted access device
    And the institution provided a means to identify the consumer to whom it was issued
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the case proceeds to limitation calculation under §1005.6(b)
```

## US-2: Apply $50 cap when notice is timely (§ 1005.6(b)(1))

**As a** Disputes Analyst, **I want** liability capped at the lesser of $50 or the unauthorized amount when the consumer gives timely notice of a lost or stolen access device, **so that** timely reporters are charged correctly.

```gherkin
Feature: Liability limit when timely notice is given

  Background:
    Given the §1005.6(a) conditions for liability are met
    And the unauthorized transfers involve a lost or stolen access device
    And the platform has recorded the date the consumer learned of the loss or theft

  Scenario Outline: Liability is the lesser of $50 or transfers before notice
    Given the consumer notified the institution within two business days after learning of the loss or theft
    And unauthorized transfers totaling <before_notice> occurred before notice
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is <liability>

    Examples:
      | before_notice | liability |
      | $20.00        | $20.00    |
      | $50.00        | $50.00    |
      | $200.00       | $50.00    |

  Scenario: Notice on the last day of the two-business-day window is timely
    Given the consumer notified the institution on the second business day after learning of the loss or theft
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the notice is classified as timely
```

## US-3: Apply $500 cap when notice is not timely (§ 1005.6(b)(2))

**As a** Disputes Analyst, **I want** the platform to compute liability for late notice as the lesser of $500 or the sum of two components, **so that** later transfers are only charged when the institution can establish they would have been prevented.

```gherkin
Feature: Liability limit when timely notice is not given

  Background:
    Given the §1005.6(a) conditions for liability are met
    And the unauthorized transfers involve a lost or stolen access device
    And the consumer did not notify the institution within two business days after learning of the loss or theft

  Scenario Outline: Liability is the lesser of $500 or the sum of components (i) and (ii)
    Given unauthorized transfers totaling <within_2_days> occurred within the two business days
    And unauthorized transfers totaling <after_2_days> occurred after the close of two business days and before notice
    And the institution <established> that the after-period transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified within two business days
    When the liability determination runs
    Then component (i) is <comp_i>
    And component (ii) is <comp_ii>
    And the consumer liability amount is <liability>

    Examples:
      | within_2_days | after_2_days | established     | comp_i | comp_ii  | liability |
      | $20.00        | $100.00      | has established | $20.00 | $100.00  | $120.00   |
      | $120.00       | $100.00      | has established | $50.00 | $100.00  | $150.00   |
      | $120.00       | $600.00      | has established | $50.00 | $600.00  | $500.00   |
      | $120.00       | $600.00      | has not established | $50.00 | $0.00 | $50.00    |
      | $30.00        | $600.00      | has not established | $30.00 | $0.00 | $30.00    |

  Scenario: Component (ii) requires a recorded institution finding
    Given the institution has not recorded a finding on whether after-period transfers would have been prevented by timely notice
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the case is held in "Pending institution establishment" status
    And no amount is assessed for component (ii)

  Scenario: Liability never exceeds $500
    Given components (i) and (ii) sum to more than $500.00
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is $500.00
```

## US-4: Apply the 60-day periodic statement rule (§ 1005.6(b)(3))

**As a** Disputes Analyst, **I want** the platform to evaluate whether the consumer reported an unauthorized transfer shown on a periodic statement within 60 days of transmittal, **so that** liability for subsequent transfers is determined correctly.

```gherkin
Feature: Periodic statement reporting window

  Background:
    Given the §1005.6(a) conditions for liability are met
    And an unauthorized EFT appears on a periodic statement
    And the platform has recorded the statement transmittal date

  Scenario: Consumer reports within 60 days of transmittal
    Given the consumer notified the institution within 60 days of the statement transmittal date
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer is not held liable under §1005.6(b)(3) for subsequent transfers

  Scenario: Consumer reports after 60 days and the institution establishes prevention
    Given the consumer did not notify the institution within 60 days of the statement transmittal date
    And unauthorized transfers totaling $400.00 occurred after the close of the 60 days and before notice
    And the institution established that those transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified within the 60-day period
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount for the transfers is $400.00

  Scenario: Consumer reports after 60 days and the institution does not establish prevention
    Given the consumer did not notify the institution within 60 days of the statement transmittal date
    And unauthorized transfers occurred after the close of the 60 days and before notice
    And the institution has not established that those transfers would have been prevented by timely notice
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount for those transfers is $0.00

  Scenario: Transfers within the 60 days are not charged under this rule
    Given the consumer did not notify the institution within 60 days of the statement transmittal date
    And unauthorized transfers occurred on or before the close of the 60 days
    When the liability determination runs
    Then those transfers are not assessed under §1005.6(b)(3)

  Scenario: Access device involved adds amounts under (b)(1) or (b)(2)
    Given the unauthorized transfer involved an access device
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the platform calculates the §1005.6(b)(3) amount
    And the platform also calculates the applicable §1005.6(b)(1) or (b)(2) amount
    And the determination shows each amount separately
```

> **Open item for CO/Product:** the text says the consumer "may be liable for other amounts" in (b)(1)/(b)(2) when an access device is involved. How the (b)(3) amount and the (b)(1)/(b)(2) amount combine, and whether any overall ceiling applies, is not stated in this text. The final scenario therefore shows the amounts separately and leaves the combination rule to be confirmed.

## US-5: Extend notice time limits for extenuating circumstances (§ 1005.6(b)(4))

**As a** Disputes Analyst, **I want** to record extenuating circumstances and extend the notice deadlines to a reasonable period, **so that** a consumer whose delay was justified is evaluated against the extended deadlines.

```gherkin
Feature: Extension of time limits

  Scenario: Analyst records extenuating circumstances and sets an extended period
    Given a dispute where the consumer's notice was later than a time limit in §1005.6(b)
    And the consumer's delay was due to extenuating circumstances
    When the analyst records the circumstances and an extended reasonable period
    Then the platform applies the extended period in place of the original time limit
    And the original and extended deadlines are both retained in the case record

  Scenario: Notice within the extended period is treated as timely
    Given an extended period has been recorded for the case
    And the consumer gave notice within the extended period
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the notice is classified as timely for the extended time limit

  Scenario: No extension applied without a recorded justification
    Given no extenuating circumstances have been recorded
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the original time limits are used

  Scenario: Extension decision is auditable
    When an extension is recorded
    Then the platform stores the analyst ID, timestamp, circumstances and extended period
```

## US-6: Determine when and how notice was given (§ 1005.6(b)(5))

**As a** Disputes Analyst, **I want** the platform to date notice according to the rules for how it was given, **so that** timeliness is measured from the correct point.

```gherkin
Feature: Notice to the financial institution

  Scenario Outline: Accepted notice channels
    When the consumer gives notice <channel>
    Then the platform accepts the notice as valid notice

    Examples:
      | channel      |
      | in person    |
      | by telephone |
      | in writing   |

  Scenario: Notice is given when the consumer takes reasonable steps
    Given the consumer took steps reasonably necessary to provide the pertinent information
    And no particular employee or agent of the institution actually received the information
    When the notice date is determined
    Then notice is treated as given on the date the consumer took those steps

  Scenario: Written notice dated at mailing or delivery for transmission
    Given the consumer notified the institution in writing
    When the notice date is determined
    Then the notice date is the date the consumer mailed it or delivered it for transmission by another usual means
    And not the date the institution received it

  Scenario: Constructive notice
    Given the institution became aware of circumstances leading to a reasonable belief that an unauthorized transfer to or from the consumer's account has been or may be made
    When the analyst records constructive notice
    Then the platform may set the notice date to the date the institution became aware
    And the analyst's basis for the belief is stored in the case record
```

## US-7: Apply a lower state-law or agreement cap (§ 1005.6(b)(6))

**As a** Compliance Officer, **I want** liability limited to the amount under any applicable state law or customer agreement when that amount is lower, **so that** the consumer never pays more than the lowest applicable limit.

```gherkin
Feature: Lower liability under state law or agreement

  Background:
    Given a liability amount has been calculated under §1005.6(b)(1) through (b)(3)

  Scenario Outline: Consumer liability is the lower of the Regulation E amount and the state-law or agreement amount
    Given the applicable state law or agreement imposes liability of <other_limit>
    And the Regulation E calculated liability is <reg_e_amount>
    When the final liability is determined
    Then the consumer liability amount is <final>

    Examples:
      | reg_e_amount | other_limit | final   |
      | $50.00       | $0.00       | $0.00   |
      | $500.00      | $200.00     | $200.00 |
      | $50.00       | $500.00     | $50.00  |

  Scenario: No state-law or agreement limit applies
    Given no state law or agreement imposes a different liability amount
    When the final liability is determined
    Then the consumer liability amount equals the Regulation E calculated amount

  Scenario: Source of the lower limit is recorded
    Given a state law or agreement limit is applied
    Then the determination records which source set the limit and the amount imposed
```

## US-8: Treat related unauthorized transfers as a series (§ 1005.6(b) lead-in)

**As a** Disputes Analyst, **I want** related unauthorized transfers grouped so one liability limit is applied to the series, **so that** the consumer is not charged a separate cap per transfer.

```gherkin
Feature: Series of related unauthorized transfers

  Scenario: One cap applies across a series
    Given three related unauthorized transfers of $100.00 each occurred before timely notice
    And the analyst has linked them as a series
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the §1005.6(b)(1) limit is applied once to the series
    And the consumer liability amount is $50.00

  Scenario: Unlinked transfers are assessed separately
    Given two unauthorized transfers have not been linked as related
    When the liability determination runs
    Then each is assessed as its own case

  Scenario: Determination shows the full calculation
    When a liability determination completes
    Then the output shows the transfers included, the notice classification, each component amount, any cap applied, any state-law or agreement limit, and the final liability
```
