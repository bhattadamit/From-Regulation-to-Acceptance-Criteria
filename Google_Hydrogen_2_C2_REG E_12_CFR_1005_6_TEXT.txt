### Epic: Regulation E Consumer Liability Calculation Engine (12 CFR § 1005.6)

---

### Story 1: Prerequisite Conditions for Consumer Liability

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Engine

**I want to** validate whether the statutory prerequisites for consumer liability are met before assessing any financial responsibility

**So that** the bank never holds a consumer liable when required disclosures, device acceptance, or consumer identification mechanisms were missing.

```gherkin
Feature: Validate Conditions for Consumer Liability (§ 1005.6(a))

  Scenario: Required disclosures under § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3) were not provided
    Given an unauthorized electronic fund transfer (EFT) dispute has been submitted
    And the bank did not provide the disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    When the liability engine evaluates the dispute
    Then the maximum consumer liability must be set to $0.00

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfer involves an unaccepted access device
    Given an unauthorized EFT dispute involves an access device
    And the bank provided the required disclosures under § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    And the access device is not an accepted access device
    When the liability engine evaluates the dispute
    Then the maximum consumer liability must be set to $0.00

  Scenario: Bank failed to provide a means to identify the consumer
    Given an unauthorized EFT dispute involves an accepted access device
    And the bank provided the required disclosures under § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    And the bank did not provide a means to identify the consumer to whom the access device was issued
    When the liability engine evaluates the dispute
    Then the maximum consumer liability must be set to $0.00

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfer does not involve an access device and disclosures were provided
    Given an unauthorized EFT dispute does not involve an access device
    And the bank provided the required disclosures under § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    When the liability engine evaluates the dispute
    Then the consumer is eligible for liability assessment under § 1005.6(b)

  Scenario: All conditions met for an access device transfer
    Given an unauthorized EFT dispute involves an accepted access device
    And the bank provided the required disclosures under § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    And the bank provided a means to identify the consumer to whom the device was issued
    When the liability engine evaluates the dispute
    Then the consumer is eligible for liability assessment under § 1005.6(b)

```

```gherkin

  Scenario Outline: Consumer provides notice via permitted channels

    And the consumer provides pertinent information via "<channel>"
    When the notice is recorded in the disputes platform
    Then the notice must be accepted as valid regardless of whether a specific employee or agent received it
    And the effective notice timestamp must be recorded as "<effective_timestamp>"

    Examples:
      | channel   | effective_timestamp                                                       |
      | In person | Date and time the consumer provided pertinent information in person       |
      | Telephone | Date and time the consumer provided pertinent information by telephone    |
      | Writing   | Date and time the consumer mailed or delivered the notice for transmission |

  Scenario: Constructive notice triggered by bank awareness
    Given the consumer has not yet contacted the bank to report an unauthorized EFT
    And the bank becomes aware of circumstances leading to the reasonable belief that an unauthorized transfer to or from the consumer's account has been or may be made
    When the constructive notice event is logged
    Then the effective notice timestamp must be set to the date and time the bank became aware of those circumstances

```

### Story 3: Apply Extenuating Circumstances Time Limit Extensions

**As a** Dispute Analyst

**I want to** apply an extenuating circumstances flag that extends the statutory reporting windows to a reasonable period

**So that** consumers who were prevented from timely reporting are not unfairly penalized under the 2-business-day or 60-day tiers.

```gherkin
Feature: Extension of Time Limits for Extenuating Circumstances (§ 1005.6(b)(4))

  Scenario: Extenuating circumstances extend the 2-business-day window
    Given a consumer learned of the loss or theft of an access device
    And the consumer notified the bank more than 2 business days after learning of the loss or theft
    And a Dispute Analyst documents that the delay was due to extenuating circumstances
    And the notice occurred within the extended reasonable period
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the notice must be treated as timely under the 2-business-day rule (§ 1005.6(b)(1))

  Scenario: Extenuating circumstances extend the 60-day periodic statement window
    Given an unauthorized EFT appeared on a periodic statement
    And the consumer notified the bank more than 60 days after transmittal of the statement
    And a Dispute Analyst documents that the delay was due to extenuating circumstances
    And the notice occurred within the extended reasonable period
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the notice must be treated as timely under the 60-day periodic statement rule (§ 1005.6(b)(3))

```

### Story 5: Calculate Liability for Untimely Notice of Lost/Stolen Access Device ($500 Tier)

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Engine

**I want to** compute tiered liability up to $500 when a consumer fails to notify the bank within two business days of learning of a lost or stolen access device

**So that** the consumer is only held liable for post-2-day transfers that the bank establishes would not have occurred had timely notice been given.

```gherkin
Feature: Untimely Notice of Lost or Stolen Access Device (§ 1005.6(b)(2))

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers occur both within and after 2 business days, and bank establishes preventability
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability conditions are met
    And the consumer failed to notify the bank within 2 business days after learning of the loss or theft of the access device
    And unauthorized transfers within the first 2 business days total $30.00
    And unauthorized transfers after the close of 2 business days and before notice total $300.00
    And the bank establishes that the $300.00 in post-2-day transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified the bank within the 2-day period
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the first-2-day component must be calculated as $30.00 (lesser of $50 or $30.00)
    And the total calculated Regulation E liability must be $330.00 (lesser of $500 or $30.00 + $300.00)

  Scenario: Combined liability exceeds the $500 statutory cap
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability conditions are met
    And the consumer failed to notify the bank within 2 business days after learning of the loss or theft of the access device
    And unauthorized transfers within the first 2 business days total $120.00
    And unauthorized transfers after the close of 2 business days and before notice total $600.00
    And the bank establishes that the $600.00 in post-2-day transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified the bank within the 2-day period
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the first-2-day component must be capped at $50.00
    And the sum before the overall cap is $650.00 ($50.00 + $600.00)
    And the total calculated Regulation E liability must be capped at $500.00

  Scenario: Bank fails to establish that post-2-day transfers would have been prevented
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability conditions are met
    And the consumer failed to notify the bank within 2 business days after learning of the loss or theft of the access device
    And unauthorized transfers within the first 2 business days total $80.00
    And unauthorized transfers after the close of 2 business days and before notice total $250.00
    And the bank cannot establish that the $250.00 in post-2-day transfers would not have occurred had notice been given within 2 business days
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the post-2-day preventable amount must be $0.00
    And the total calculated Regulation E liability must be $50.00 (lesser of $500 or $50.00 + $0.00)

```

### Story 6: Calculate Liability for Failure to Report Unauthorized Transfers on a Periodic Statement (60-Day Rule)

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Engine

**I want to** assess unlimited liability for subsequent preventable transfers occurring after 60 days from periodic statement transmittal—and combine it with access device liability when applicable

**So that** statements containing unauthorized transfers (including unauthorized EFTs not involving an access device, or unreported lost/stolen devices) are adjudicated accurately.

```gherkin
Feature: Periodic Statement 60-Day Reporting Rule (§ 1005.6(b)(3))

  Scenario: Consumer reports unauthorized transfer within 60 days of periodic statement transmittal (no access device involved)
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability conditions are met
    And an unauthorized EFT not involving an access device appeared on a periodic statement transmitted on "2026-09-01"
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-10-15" (within 60 days of transmittal)
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer liability for the reported transfer and any subsequent transfers must be $0.00

  Scenario: Consumer fails to report within 60 days and subsequent preventable transfers occur (no access device involved)
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability conditions are met
    And an unauthorized EFT not involving an access device of $200.00 appeared on a periodic statement transmitted on "2026-08-01"
    And the 60-day reporting window closed on "2026-09-30"
    And the consumer gave notice on "2026-10-20"
    And unauthorized transfers occurring after "2026-09-30" and before "2026-10-20" total $1,200.00
    And the bank establishes that the $1,200.00 in transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified the bank within the 60-day period
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer liability for the initial $200.00 transfer on the statement must be $0.00
    And the consumer liability for the post-60-day preventable transfers must be $1,200.00
    And the total calculated Regulation E liability must be $1,200.00

  Scenario: Bank cannot establish preventability of post-60-day transfers
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability conditions are met
    And the consumer failed to report an unauthorized EFT within 60 days of periodic statement transmittal
    And unauthorized transfers occurring after the close of 60 days and before notice total $900.00
    And the bank cannot establish that those transfers would not have occurred had notice been given within the 60-day period
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the total calculated Regulation E liability must be $0.00

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfer after 60 days also involves an access device
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability conditions are met
    And an unauthorized EFT involving an accepted access device appeared on a periodic statement
    And the consumer failed to report the unauthorized EFT within 60 days of statement transmittal
    And the consumer never learned of the loss or theft of the access device prior to giving notice
    And preventable unauthorized transfers occurring after the close of 60 days and before notice total $850.00
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the engine must evaluate liability under § 1005.6(b)(1) or (b)(2) for the access device (resulting in $0.00 since the consumer had not learned of the loss/theft)
    And add the post-60-day preventable transfer liability of $850.00 under § 1005.6(b)(3)
    And the total calculated Regulation E liability must be $850.00

  Scenario: Series of related unauthorized transfers spans both the 2-day access device rule and the 60-day statement rule
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability conditions are met
    And the consumer learned of the theft of an access device on Day 1 and failed to report it within 2 business days
    And $100.00 in unauthorized transfers occurred on Day 1 (within 2 business days)
    And $600.00 in preventable unauthorized transfers occurred on Day 10 (after 2 business days, before statement transmittal)
    And a periodic statement showing those transfers was transmitted on Day 15
    And the consumer gave notice on Day 80 (after the close of the 60-day statement period)
    And $400.00 in preventable unauthorized transfers occurred on Day 78 (after the close of 60 days)
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then liability for the transfers prior to the close of the 60-day period is capped at $500.00 under § 1005.6(b)(2)
    And liability for the preventable transfer after the close of the 60-day period is $400.00 under § 1005.6(b)(3)
    And the total calculated Regulation E liability must be $900.00

```
