# **Regulation E error resolution — user stories and Gherkin acceptance criteria**

**Regulatory basis:** 12 CFR § 1005.11, Procedures for resolving errors.

**Business context:** US bank card and ACH disputes platform.

The following backlog translates the supplied regulatory text into implementable user stories and testable Gherkin acceptance criteria. It covers error classification, notice intake, eligibility, investigation deadlines, provisional credit, consumer communications, resolution, and repeat disputes.

## **1. Scope and implementation principles**

The platform should support a common Regulation E error-resolution workflow for card and ACH disputes, with configurable product-specific processing where necessary.

The following principles govern the stories:

1. **Oral and written notices:** Both must be supported.
2. **Error classification:** Distinguish covered errors from excluded inquiries.
3. **Deadline calculation:** Calculate applicable business-day and calendar-day deadlines from the appropriate triggering event.
4. **Investigation paths:** Support standard 10-business-day resolution and extended investigation periods.
5. **Provisional credit:** Apply conditional crediting, notification, and consumer access requirements.
6. **Evidence and communications:** Retain investigation findings and provide required consumer notifications.
7. **Repeat disputes:** Distinguish repeat assertions from disputes arising after requested documentation or clarification.

**Important scope limitation:** Acceptance criteria below are based exclusively on the regulatory text supplied. They do not introduce additional requirements from other regulations, regulatory commentary, bank policies, or network rules. Where the regulation refers to another provision, such as § 1005.6(a), § 1005.9, § 1005.10(a), or § 1005.14, the platform must support the referenced condition without assuming its detailed requirements from this section alone.

# **Epic 1 — Error identification and classification**

## **US-001: Identify covered Regulation E errors**

**User story**

As a disputes operations analyst, I want the platform to identify whether a consumer inquiry qualifies as an error under Regulation E, so that covered notices enter the appropriate error-resolution workflow.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.11(a)(1)

### **Acceptance criteria**

**Scenario 1: Unauthorized electronic fund transfer**

```
Scenario: Consumer reports an unauthorized EFT
  Given a consumer reports an unauthorized electronic fund transfer
  When the platform classifies the inquiry
  Then the inquiry shall be classified as a Regulation E error
```

**Scenario 2: Incorrect electronic fund transfer**

```
Scenario: Consumer reports an incorrect EFT
  Given a consumer reports an incorrect electronic fund transfer
  To or from the consumer's account
  When the platform classifies the inquiry
  Then the inquiry shall be classified as a Regulation E error
```

**Scenario 3: Omitted electronic fund transfer**

```
Scenario: Consumer reports an omitted EFT
  Given a consumer reports that an electronic fund transfer
  Is missing from a periodic statement
  When the platform classifies the inquiry
  Then the inquiry shall be classified as a Regulation E error
```

**Scenario 4: Computational or bookkeeping error**

```
Scenario: Consumer reports an institutional calculation error
  Given a consumer reports a computational or bookkeeping error
  Made by the financial institution relating to an EFT
  When the platform classifies the inquiry
  Then the inquiry shall be classified as a Regulation E error
```

**Scenario 5: Incorrect amount received from an electronic terminal**

```
Scenario: Consumer reports an incorrect terminal disbursement
  Given a consumer reports receiving an incorrect amount of money
  From an electronic terminal
  When the platform classifies the inquiry
  Then the inquiry shall be classified as a Regulation E error
```

**Scenario 6: EFT not properly identified**

```
Scenario: Consumer reports an unidentified EFT
  Given a consumer reports an EFT not identified
  In accordance with section 1005.9 or 1005.10(a)
  When the platform classifies the inquiry
  Then the inquiry shall be classified as a Regulation E error
```

**Scenario 7: Request for documentation or clarification**

```
Scenario: Consumer requests EFT documentation or clarification
  Given a consumer requests documentation required by section 1005.9
  Or section 1005.10(a)
  Or requests additional information or clarification concerning an EFT
  When the platform classifies the inquiry
  Then the inquiry shall be classified as a Regulation E error
  And the inquiry shall be identified as a documentation
  Or clarification request
```

## **US-002: Exclude inquiries that do not constitute errors**

**User story**

As a disputes operations analyst, I want routine inquiries and specified information requests excluded from Regulation E error classification, so that the platform does not incorrectly initiate an error-resolution investigation.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.11(a)(2)

### **Acceptance criteria**

```
Scenario Outline: Exclude non-error inquiries
  Given a consumer submits an inquiry of type "<inquiry_type>"
  When the platform classifies the inquiry
  Then the inquiry shall not be classified as a Regulation E error

Examples:
  | inquiry_type                                  |
  | Routine account balance inquiry               |
  | Tax or other recordkeeping information request |
  | Request for duplicate documentation copies    |
```

### **Acceptance criteria**

```
Scenario Outline: Accept notice through supported communication channel
  Given a consumer submits a notice of error through "<channel>"
  When the notice is received by the institution
  Then the platform shall register the notice for eligibility assessment

Examples:
  | channel |
  | Oral    |
  | Written |
```

## **US-004: Validate notice timing and required information**

**User story**

As a disputes operations analyst, I want the platform to assess the timeliness and required information of a notice of error, so that the bank can determine whether the notice meets Regulation E intake requirements.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.11(b)(1)(i)-(iii)

### **Acceptance criteria**

**Scenario 1: Notice received within 60 days**

```
Scenario: Notice received within the applicable 60-day period
  Given the institution sent the periodic statement
  Or provided the required passbook documentation
  On which the alleged error is first reflected
  And the notice is received no later than 60 days afterward
  When the platform assesses notice eligibility
  Then the notice shall satisfy the timing requirement
```

**Scenario 2: Notice received after 60 days**

```
Scenario: Notice received after the applicable 60-day period
  Given the institution sent the periodic statement
  Or provided the required passbook documentation
  On which the alleged error is first reflected
  And more than 60 days have elapsed
  When the platform assesses notice eligibility
  Then the notice shall not satisfy the timing requirement
```

**Scenario 3: Consumer identity and account number**

```
Scenario: Notice identifies the consumer and account
  Given a consumer submits a notice of error
  When the platform validates the notice
  Then the notice shall identify the consumer's name
  And the consumer's account number
```

**Scenario 4: Reason for alleged error**

```
Scenario: Notice includes the reason for the alleged error
  Given a consumer submits a notice of error
  When the platform validates the notice
  Then the notice shall indicate why the consumer believes an error exists
```

**Scenario 5: Error details**

```
Scenario: Notice includes available error details
  Given a consumer submits a notice of error
  When the platform validates the notice
  Then the platform shall capture the type of error
  And the date of the error
  And the amount of the error
  To the extent possible
```

**Scenario 6: Documentation or clarification request exception**

```
Scenario: Documentation request does not require ordinary error details
  Given a consumer submits a request described in section 1005.11(a)(1)(vii)
  When the platform validates the notice
  Then the platform shall not require the type date and amount
  Of an alleged error as a condition of that request
```

## **US-005: Determine timeliness of errors asserted after documentation requests**

**User story**

As a disputes operations analyst, I want the platform to calculate the applicable notice period when a consumer asserts an error after receiving requested documentation or clarification, so that the correct regulatory starting point is used.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.11(b)(3)

### **Acceptance criteria**

```
Scenario: Error notice follows requested documentation
  Given the consumer requested documentation or clarification
  Under section 1005.11(a)(1)(vii)
  And the institution subsequently sent the requested information
  When the consumer submits a notice of error
  Then the platform shall assess timeliness
  Using the date the institution sent the requested information
  And the notice shall satisfy the timing requirement
  If received no later than 60 days after that date
```

## **US-006: Manage written confirmation following oral notice**

**User story**

As a consumer service representative, I want the platform to communicate and track written confirmation requirements for oral notices, so that any written confirmation required by the institution is managed within the regulatory timeframe.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.11(b)(2)

### **Acceptance criteria**

```
Scenario: Institution requires written confirmation
  Given a consumer provides an oral notice of error
  And the institution requires written confirmation
  When the oral notice is registered
  Then the consumer shall be informed of the written confirmation requirement
  And the address where confirmation must be sent shall be provided
```

```
Scenario: Written confirmation received within 10 business days
  Given the institution requires written confirmation of an oral notice
  And the consumer has been informed of the requirement
  When written confirmation is received within 10 business days
  Then the platform shall record the confirmation as received
```

```
Scenario: Written confirmation not received within 10 business days
  Given the institution requires written confirmation of an oral notice
  And the consumer has been informed of the requirement
  When written confirmation is not received within 10 business days
  Then the platform shall record that confirmation was not received
  And the case shall be eligible for the applicable provisional credit exception
```

# **Epic 3 — Investigation and regulatory deadlines**

## **US-007: Initiate and complete investigation within the standard timeframe**

**User story**

As a disputes operations analyst, I want the platform to track investigation deadlines from receipt of a notice of error, so that the institution determines whether an error occurred within the required timeframe.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.11(c)(1)

### **Acceptance criteria**

**Scenario 1: Standard investigation timeframe**

```
Scenario: Complete investigation within 10 business days
  Given the institution receives a notice of error
  And no extended investigation period applies
  When the investigation is conducted
  Then the institution shall determine whether an error occurred
  Within 10 business days of receiving the notice
```

**Scenario 2: Report investigation results**

```
Scenario: Report investigation results to the consumer
  Given the institution has completed its investigation
  When the investigation results are finalized
  Then the institution shall report the results to the consumer
  Within 3 business days after completing the investigation
```

**Scenario 3: Correct confirmed error**

```
Scenario: Correct an error identified through investigation
  Given the institution determines that an error occurred
  When the determination is recorded
  Then the institution shall correct the error
  Within 1 business day after the determination
```

## **US-008: Support extended investigation timeframe**

**User story**

As a disputes operations analyst, I want the platform to support an extended investigation process when the institution cannot complete an investigation within the initial period, so that the institution can continue investigating while meeting provisional credit and consumer notification requirements.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.11(c)(2)

### **Acceptance criteria**

**Scenario 1: Investigation cannot be completed within 10 business days**

```
Scenario: Enter extended investigation process
  Given the institution receives a notice of error
  And the institution is unable to complete its investigation
  Within 10 business days
  When the institution elects to continue the investigation
  Then the platform shall support an investigation period
  Of up to 45 days from receipt of the notice
  Subject to the applicable extension provisions
```

**Scenario 2: Provisional credit within 10 business days**

```
Scenario: Provide provisional credit during extended investigation
  Given the institution is unable to complete its investigation
  Within the applicable initial investigation period
  And the institution proceeds under the extended investigation process
  And no provisional credit exception applies
  When the applicable provisional credit deadline is reached
  Then the institution shall provisionally credit the consumer's account
  Within 10 business days of receiving the notice
  In the amount of the alleged error
  Including interest where applicable
```

**Scenario 3: Provisional credit amount for unauthorized EFT**

```
Scenario: Apply permitted withholding to provisional credit
  Given the alleged error concerns an unauthorized EFT
  And the institution has a reasonable basis for believing
  That an unauthorized EFT has occurred
  And the institution has satisfied section 1005.6(a)
  When the institution calculates provisional credit
  Then the institution may withhold an amount from the credit
  Not exceeding $50
```

**Scenario 4: Written confirmation exception**

```
Scenario: Do not require provisional credit when written confirmation is missing
  Given the institution requires written confirmation of an oral notice
  And the consumer does not provide written confirmation
  Within 10 business days of the oral notice
  When the institution assesses provisional credit eligibility
  Then the institution need not provisionally credit the account
```

**Scenario 5: Regulation T account exception**

```
Scenario: Apply provisional credit exception to Regulation T account
  Given the alleged error involves an account
  Subject to Regulation T of the Federal Reserve Board
  When the institution assesses provisional credit eligibility
  Then the institution need not provisionally credit the account
```

**Scenario 6: Notify consumer of provisional credit**

```
Scenario: Notify consumer after provisional credit
  Given the institution has provisionally credited the consumer's account
  When the provisional credit is posted
  Then the institution shall inform the consumer
  Of the amount of the provisional credit
  And the date of the provisional credit
  Within 2 business days after provisional crediting
```

**Scenario 7: Provide full use of provisionally credited funds**

```
Scenario: Consumer has full use of provisional credit
  Given provisional credit has been applied to the consumer's account
  When the investigation remains open
  Then the consumer shall have full use of the provisionally credited funds
  During the investigation
```

**Scenario 8: Correct confirmed error under extended investigation**

```
Scenario: Correct error following extended investigation
  Given the institution determines that an error occurred
  During an extended investigation
  When the determination is recorded
  Then the institution shall correct the error
  Within 1 business day after the determination
```

**Scenario 9: Report extended investigation results**

```
Scenario: Report results following extended investigation
  Given the institution completes its extended investigation
  When the investigation results are finalized
  Then the institution shall report the results to the consumer
  Within 3 business days after completing the investigation
```

**Scenario 10: Make provisional credit final**

```
Scenario: Notify consumer that provisional credit is final
  Given the investigation results establish that an error occurred
  And provisional credit was previously applied
  When the institution reports the investigation results
  Then the institution shall include notice
  That the provisional credit has been made final
  Where applicable
```

## **US-009: Calculate applicable extended regulatory timeframes**

**User story**

As a disputes operations analyst, I want the platform to calculate applicable extended deadlines based on the characteristics of the EFT and account, so that the correct investigation and provisional credit periods are applied.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.11(c)(3)

### **Acceptance criteria**

**Scenario 1: EFT within 30 days after the first deposit**

```
Scenario: Apply 20-business-day initial investigation period
  Given the notice of error involves an EFT to or from the account
  Within 30 days after the first deposit to the account was made
  When the platform calculates the applicable investigation deadline
  Then the applicable 10-business-day period
  Shall be replaced by 20 business days
```

**Scenario 2: EFT not initiated within a state**

```
Scenario: Apply 90-day investigation period for qualifying out-of-state EFT
  Given the notice of error involves an EFT
  That was not initiated within a state
  And an extended investigation is required
  When the platform calculates the investigation deadline
  Then the applicable 45-day period shall be replaced by 90 days
```

**Scenario 3: Point-of-sale debit card transaction**

```
Scenario: Apply 90-day investigation period for POS debit transaction
  Given the notice of error involves a point-of-sale debit card transaction
  And an extended investigation is required
  When the platform calculates the investigation deadline
  Then the applicable 45-day period shall be replaced by 90 days
```

**Scenario 4: EFT within 30 days after first deposit**

```
Scenario: Apply 90-day extended investigation period for qualifying new account EFT
  Given the notice of error involves an EFT
  That occurred within 30 days after the first deposit to the account
  And an extended investigation is required
  When the platform calculates the investigation deadline
  Then the applicable 45-day period shall be replaced by 90 days
```

**Scenario 5: Combine applicable new-account timeframes**

```
Scenario: Apply both new-account deadline provisions
  Given the notice of error involves an EFT
  To or from the account within 30 days after the first deposit
  And the institution cannot complete its investigation
  Within the applicable initial investigation period
  When the platform calculates the applicable deadlines
  Then the applicable initial 10-business-day period
  Shall be replaced by 20 business days
  And the applicable extended 45-day investigation period
  Shall be replaced by 90 days
```

## **US-010: Support investigation through review of institutional records**

**User story**

As a disputes operations analyst, I want the platform to support resolution based on the institution's own records when the regulatory conditions are met, so that the institution can use the permitted investigation method.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.11(c)(4)

### **Acceptance criteria**

```
Scenario: Institutional record review satisfies investigation requirements
  Given the alleged error concerns an EFT to or from a third party
  And there is no agreement between the institution and the third party
  For the type of EFT involved
  And the transfer is not covered by section 1005.14
  When the institution reviews its own records
  Then that review shall satisfy the investigation requirements
  Of section 1005.11
```

```
Scenario: Institutional record review does not qualify under third-party exception
  Given the alleged error concerns a transfer to or from a third party
  And an agreement exists between the institution and the third party
  For the type of EFT involved
  When the institution reviews its own records
  Then the platform shall not treat that review
  As satisfying the investigation requirements
  Under the exception in section 1005.11(c)(4)
```

## **US-012: Manage debit of provisional credit**

**User story**

As a disputes operations analyst, I want the platform to manage notification and payment protections when provisional credit is debited, so that the institution meets the requirements for handling the consumer's account following an adverse determination.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.11(d)(2)

### **Acceptance criteria**

**Scenario 1: Notify consumer of provisional credit debit**

```
Scenario: Notify consumer of provisional credit debit
  Given provisional credit has been applied to the consumer's account
  When the institution debits the provisionally credited amount
  Then the institution shall notify the consumer
  Of the date of the debiting
  And the amount of the debiting
```

**Scenario 2: Notify consumer of five-business-day protection**

```
Scenario: Notify consumer of continued payment protections
  Given the institution debits provisionally credited funds
  When the institution notifies the consumer of the debiting
  Then the institution shall notify the consumer
  That checks drafts or similar instruments payable to third parties
  And preauthorized transfers from the consumer's account
  Will be honored for 5 business days after notification
  Without charge to the consumer as a result of an overdraft
```

**Scenario 3: Honor qualifying checks and drafts**

```
Scenario: Honor qualifying third-party payment instruments
  Given the institution has notified the consumer
  Of the debit of provisional credit
  And the 5-business-day protection period is in effect
  When a check draft or similar instrument payable to a third party
  Is presented during that period
  Then the institution shall honor the item
  If it would have been paid had the provisionally credited funds
  Not been debited
```

**Scenario 4: Honor qualifying preauthorized transfers**

```
Scenario: Honor qualifying preauthorized transfers
  Given the institution has notified the consumer
  Of the debit of provisional credit
  And the 5-business-day protection period is in effect
  When a preauthorized transfer is presented during that period
  Then the institution shall honor the transfer
  If it would have been paid had the provisionally credited funds
  Not been debited
```

**Scenario 5: No overdraft charge resulting from provisional credit debit**

```
Scenario: Prevent overdraft charges resulting from provisional credit debit
  Given the institution has debited provisional credit
  And a qualifying payment is honored during the 5-business-day period
  When the payment results in an overdraft
  As a result of the provisional credit debit
  Then the institution shall not charge the consumer
  For that overdraft
```

# **Epic 5 — Repeat disputes and case management**

## **US-013: Handle consumer reassertion of previously resolved errors**

**User story**

As a disputes operations analyst, I want the platform to identify repeat assertions of previously resolved errors, so that the institution can distinguish cases that do not require further Regulation E error-resolution responsibilities from qualifying exceptions.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.11(e)

### **Acceptance criteria**

**Scenario 1: Same error reasserted after full compliance**

```
Scenario: Consumer reasserts an error previously resolved
  Given the institution has fully complied
  With the error-resolution requirements
  And the consumer later reasserts the same error
  When the platform evaluates the new assertion
  Then the platform shall identify the assertion
  As a previously resolved error
  And the institution shall have no further responsibilities
  Under section 1005.11
```

**Scenario 2: Error asserted after documentation or clarification**

```
Scenario: Consumer asserts an error after receiving requested information
  Given the consumer previously requested documentation
  Or clarification under section 1005.11(a)(1)(vii)
  And the institution provided the requested information
  When the consumer subsequently asserts an error
  Then the platform shall not automatically treat the assertion
  As an excluded repeat error under section 1005.11(e)
  And shall assess the assertion under the applicable requirements
```

# **3. Recommended dispute lifecycle**

The following represents the logical workflow supported by the supplied regulatory provisions.

## **Regulation E error resolution workflow**

1. **Notice received**

   Capture oral or written consumer notification.
2. **Error classification**

   Determine whether the inquiry meets the covered-error definition.
3. **Notice eligibility**

   Assess the applicable 60-day period, consumer identity, account number and required information.
4. **Investigation**

   Start the applicable 10- or 20-business-day investigation period.
5. **Investigation decision**

   **Investigation completed**
   - Error confirmed: correct within one business day.
   - No error or different error: provide written explanation and document-request rights.

   **Investigation not completed**
   - Enter extended investigation.
   - Apply provisional credit, unless an exception applies.
   - Notify consumer within two business days of crediting.
   - Continue investigation through applicable 45- or 90-day deadline.
6. **Final determination and reporting**

   Report results within three business days after completing the investigation.
7. **Provisional credit disposition**

   If debited, provide required notification and five-business-day payment protections.
8. **Case closure and reassertion handling**

   Apply the repeat-error provision where the institution has fully complied.

# **4. Key business rules for platform implementation**

These are the principal rules that should be reflected in the platform's workflow and configuration.

| Rule ID | Business rule                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| BR-001  | An error can be reported orally or in writing.                                                                                                                  |
| BR-002  | The standard notice period is 60 days after the institution sends the statement or provides passbook documentation on which the alleged error first appears.    |
| BR-003  | Documentation or clarification requests have a separate 60-day notice period measured from when the requested information is sent.                              |
| BR-004  | Written confirmation may be required for oral notices, provided the consumer is informed of the requirement and submission address when giving the oral notice. |
| BR-005  | The standard investigation period is 10 business days.                                                                                                          |
| BR-006  | The initial investigation period becomes 20 business days for qualifying EFTs within 30 days after the first account deposit.                                   |
| BR-007  | Extended investigations may take up to 45 days, or 90 days where the specified conditions apply.                                                                |
| BR-008  | Provisional credit is required for extended investigations unless an applicable exception applies.                                                              |
| BR-009  | Permitted withholding for qualifying unauthorized EFT provisional credits is capped at $50.                                                                     |
| BR-010  | Consumers must have full use of provisionally credited funds during the investigation.                                                                          |
| BR-011  | Confirmed errors must be corrected within one business day after determination.                                                                                 |
| BR-012  | Investigation results must be reported within three business days after completion.                                                                             |
| BR-013  | No-error and different-error determinations require a written explanation and notice of document-request rights.                                                |
| BR-014  | Documents relied upon must be provided promptly upon consumer request.                                                                                          |
| BR-015  | Debited provisional credits trigger notification and five-business-day payment protections.                                                                     |
| BR-016  | Fully resolved errors generally do not require further action upon reassertion, subject to the documentation/clarification exception.                           |

# **5. Important design considerations and regulatory interpretation boundaries**

The following items should be addressed during solution design. They are not additional regulatory acceptance criteria.

**A. Card and ACH applicability**

The platform should classify the underlying transfer rather than relying solely on the dispute product label. For example, the POS debit card extension is expressly stated in § 1005.11(c)(3)(ii)(B), whereas ACH is not separately identified as a category receiving that extension.

**B. Calendar-day versus business-day calculations**

The supplied text distinguishes business days from days:

- 10, 20, 2, 3, and 5-day periods explicitly use business days.
- 45- and 90-day investigation periods use days, without the business-day qualifier.

The deadline engine should preserve this distinction. The supplied text does not establish the bank's applicable holiday calendar or detailed day-counting conventions.

**C. Provisional credit exceptions**

The platform should independently evaluate:

- Missing written confirmation when required and not received within 10 business days of an oral notice.
- Accounts subject to Regulation T.
- The permitted maximum $50 withholding for qualifying unauthorized EFTs.

**D. Different-error outcomes**

The platform should distinguish at least three investigation outcomes:

1. Error occurred as alleged.
2. No error occurred.
3. An error occurred, but in a different manner or amount.

The third outcome must not be incorrectly treated as a no-error outcome for purposes of the investigation reporting requirements.

**E. Documentation requests**

Documentation and clarification requests require their own case classification and date tracking. They are particularly important because the regulation expressly provides an exception to ordinary repeat-dispute treatment.

**F. Referenced regulatory provisions**

Before production implementation, the relevant requirements of §§ 1005.6(a), 1005.9, 1005.10(a), and 1005.14 should be separately assessed. The supplied text references these provisions but does not reproduce their substantive requirements.
