# Reg E Consumer Liability for Unauthorized EFTs (12 CFR 1005.6): User Stories and Acceptance Criteria

**Scope:** The criteria below rely only on the § 1005.6 text you provided. Where that text is silent or ambiguous, I list the gap under *Open Questions* instead of filling it in.

**Personas:** Disputes Analyst (DA), Compliance Officer (CO), Consumer (via channel intake)

---

## Story 1: Preconditions for holding a consumer liable (§ 1005.6(a))

**As a** Disputes Analyst
**I want** the platform to check the regulatory preconditions before it assigns any liability to the consumer
**So that** we never hold a consumer liable when the bank has not met its obligations.

```gherkin
Feature: Conditions for consumer liability

  Background:
    Given an unauthorized electronic fund transfer has been logged against a consumer's account

  Scenario: Required disclosures were not provided
    Given the bank has not provided the disclosures required by 1005.7(b)(1), (b)(2) and (b)(3)
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is $0.00
    And the rationale "Required disclosures not provided" is recorded

  Scenario: Transfer involved an access device that was not accepted
    Given the bank has provided the disclosures required by 1005.7(b)(1), (b)(2) and (b)(3)
    And the transfer involved an access device
    And the access device is not flagged as accepted
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is $0.00
    And the rationale "Access device not accepted" is recorded

  Scenario: Bank provided no means to identify the consumer to whom the device was issued
    Given the bank has provided the required disclosures
    And the transfer involved an accepted access device
    And no means of identifying the consumer to whom the device was issued was provided
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is $0.00
    And the rationale "No means of identification provided" is recorded

  Scenario: All preconditions are met for an access device transfer
    Given the bank has provided the required disclosures
    And the transfer involved an accepted access device
    And the bank provided a means to identify the consumer to whom it was issued
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the determination proceeds to the limitation-of-liability rules in 1005.6(b)

  Scenario: All preconditions are met for a transfer not involving an access device
    Given the bank has provided the required disclosures
    And the transfer did not involve an access device
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the determination proceeds to the limitation-of-liability rules in 1005.6(b)
```

## Story 2: Timely notice of loss or theft, maximum $50 (§ 1005.6(b)(1))

**As a** Disputes Analyst
**I want** the platform to cap liability at the lesser of $50 or the unauthorized amount before notice when the consumer gave timely notice
**So that** liability is calculated correctly and consistently.

```gherkin
Feature: Liability cap when timely notice is given

  Scenario Outline: Consumer notifies within two business days of learning of loss or theft
    Given the preconditions in 1005.6(a) are met
    And the transfer involved the loss or theft of an access device
    And the consumer gave notice within two business days after learning of the loss or theft
    And unauthorized transfers occurring before notice total <before_notice>
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is <liability>

    Examples:
      | before_notice | liability |
      | $30.00        | $30.00    |
      | $50.00        | $50.00    |
      | $200.00       | $50.00    |
      | $0.00         | $0.00     |

  Scenario: Related transfers are treated as one series
    Given the preconditions in 1005.6(a) are met
    And timely notice was given
    And three unauthorized transfers of $40.00, $40.00 and $40.00 are linked as a series of related unauthorized transfers
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the limit is applied once to the series
    And the consumer liability amount is $50.00
```

## Story 3: Timely notice not given, maximum $500 (§ 1005.6(b)(2))

**As a** Disputes Analyst
**I want** the platform to calculate the higher liability tier when the consumer did not give notice within two business days
**So that** the bank recovers only what the regulation allows.

```gherkin
Feature: Liability when timely notice of loss or theft is not given

  Scenario Outline: Consumer fails to notify within two business days
    Given the preconditions in 1005.6(a) are met
    And the transfer involved the loss or theft of an access device
    And the consumer did not give notice within two business days after learning of the loss or theft
    And unauthorized transfers within the two business days total <within_2bd>
    And unauthorized transfers after the close of two business days and before notice total <after_2bd>
    And the bank <established> that the after-two-day transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified within the two-day period
    When the liability determination runs
    Then component (i) is the lesser of $50.00 or <within_2bd>
    And component (ii) is <component_ii>
    And the consumer liability amount is the lesser of $500.00 or the sum of components (i) and (ii), which is <liability>

    Examples:
      | within_2bd | after_2bd | established            | component_ii | liability |
      | $20.00     | $100.00   | established            | $100.00      | $120.00   |
      | $200.00    | $100.00   | established            | $100.00      | $150.00   |
      | $200.00    | $600.00   | established            | $600.00      | $500.00   |
      | $200.00    | $400.00   | did not establish      | $0.00        | $50.00    |
      | $30.00     | $400.00   | did not establish      | $0.00        | $30.00    |

  Scenario: Bank has not recorded evidence that later transfers were preventable
    Given the preconditions in 1005.6(a) are met
    And the consumer did not give timely notice
    And no "would not have occurred" evidence is recorded for transfers after the two business days
    When the liability determination runs
    Then transfers after the close of two business days are excluded from the liability amount
    And the result shows that the bank's burden for component (ii) was not met

  Scenario: Analyst records evidence supporting component (ii)
    Given a determination is open for an untimely-notice case
    When the Disputes Analyst records the basis for "would not have occurred had the consumer notified within two days"
    Then the basis, author and timestamp are stored on the case
    And transfers after the two business days are included in component (ii)
```

## Story 4: Periodic statement reporting window (§ 1005.6(b)(3))

**As a** Disputes Analyst
**I want** the platform to apply the 60-day statement rule
**So that** consumers who report within 60 days avoid liability for subsequent transfers, and later reports are assessed correctly.

```gherkin
Feature: Liability for unauthorized transfers appearing on a periodic statement

  Scenario: Consumer reports within 60 days of statement transmittal
    Given the preconditions in 1005.6(a) are met
    And an unauthorized transfer appears on a periodic statement transmitted on a known date
    And the consumer gave notice within 60 days of that transmittal date
    When the liability determination runs
    Then no liability is assessed under 1005.6(b)(3) for subsequent transfers

  Scenario: Consumer does not report within 60 days
    Given the preconditions in 1005.6(a) are met
    And an unauthorized transfer appears on a periodic statement transmitted on a known date
    And the consumer did not give notice within 60 days of that transmittal date
    And unauthorized transfers after the close of the 60 days and before notice total $300.00
    And the bank established that those transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified within the 60-day period
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount under 1005.6(b)(3) is $300.00

  Scenario: Consumer does not report within 60 days and the bank cannot establish preventability
    Given the consumer did not give notice within 60 days of statement transmittal
    And the bank has not established that the later transfers would not have occurred
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the amount under 1005.6(b)(3) is $0.00

  Scenario: Access device involved and 60-day window missed
    Given the preconditions in 1005.6(a) are met
    And the unauthorized transfer involved an access device
    And the consumer did not report within 60 days of statement transmittal
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the platform calculates the 1005.6(b)(3) amount
    And the platform also calculates the applicable 1005.6(b)(1) or 1005.6(b)(2) amount
    And both amounts and the basis for each are displayed to the Disputes Analyst

  Scenario: 60-day clock starts at transmittal
    Given a periodic statement has a recorded transmittal date
    When the platform computes the reporting deadline
    Then the deadline is 60 days after the financial institution's transmittal of the statement
```

## Story 5: Extension of time limits for extenuating circumstances (§ 1005.6(b)(4))

**As a** Disputes Analyst
**I want** to extend the notice deadlines when the consumer's delay was due to extenuating circumstances
**So that** the bank applies a reasonable period as the regulation requires.

```gherkin
Feature: Extension of notice time limits

  Scenario: Analyst grants an extension
    Given a consumer's notice was outside the two-business-day or 60-day limit
    And the Disputes Analyst records that the delay was due to extenuating circumstances
    When the analyst enters a reasonable extended period and a justification
    Then the platform recalculates the applicable deadline using the extended period
    And the original deadline, extended deadline, justification, author and timestamp are retained in the audit trail
    And the liability determination is re-run using the extended deadline

  Scenario: Extension requires justification
    Given the Disputes Analyst selects "Extend time limit"
    When the analyst submits without describing the extenuating circumstances
    Then the platform blocks the extension
    And an error message states that a description is required

  Scenario: Extension changes the outcome
    Given a case was assessed as untimely under 1005.6(b)(2)
    When an extension makes the consumer's notice timely
    Then the platform re-assesses the case under 1005.6(b)(1)
    And the prior and revised liability amounts are both shown
```

## Story 6: Determining when and how notice was given (§ 1005.6(b)(5))

**As a** Disputes Analyst
**I want** the platform to record notice using the regulation's definition of when notice is given
**So that** timeliness is measured from the correct date.

```gherkin
Feature: Notice to the financial institution

  Scenario Outline: Notice channel is accepted
    Given a consumer provides pertinent information about an unauthorized transfer
    When notice is logged through <channel>
    Then the platform accepts it as notice to the institution

    Examples:
      | channel      |
      | in person    |
      | telephone    |
      | in writing   |

  Scenario: Notice is effective even if no particular employee received it
    Given the consumer took steps reasonably necessary to provide pertinent information to the institution
    And no specific employee or agent actually received the information
    When the notice date is determined
    Then notice is treated as given on the date the consumer took those steps

  Scenario: Written notice by mail
    Given the consumer sent written notice by mail
    When the notice date is determined
    Then the notice date is the date the consumer mailed the notice
    And not the date the bank received it

  Scenario: Written notice by another usual means of transmission
    Given the consumer delivered written notice for transmission by another usual means
    When the notice date is determined
    Then the notice date is the date of delivery for transmission

  Scenario: Constructive notice
    Given the institution became aware of circumstances leading to a reasonable belief that an unauthorized transfer has been or may be made
    When the Disputes Analyst records the constructive notice event and its date
    Then the platform may treat notice as constructively given on that date
    And the supporting circumstances are stored on the case

  Scenario: Notice date determines timeliness
    Given a notice date has been determined
    When the platform tests timeliness
    Then the notice date is the date compared against the two-business-day and 60-day limits
```

## Story 7: Lower liability under state law or agreement (§ 1005.6(b)(6))

**As a** Compliance Officer
**I want** the platform to cap liability at any lower amount imposed by state law or the customer agreement
**So that** consumers are never charged more than the most protective applicable limit.

```gherkin
Feature: Lower liability under state law or agreement

  Scenario Outline: Applicable limit is lower than the Reg E result
    Given the Reg E calculation under 1005.6(b) is <reg_e_amount>
    And a state law or agreement applicable to the account imposes a maximum liability of <other_limit>
    When the final liability is determined
    Then the final consumer liability is <final>

    Examples:
      | reg_e_amount | other_limit | final   |
      | $500.00      | $100.00     | $100.00 |
      | $50.00       | $0.00       | $0.00   |
      | $50.00       | $200.00     | $50.00  |

  Scenario: No state law or agreement limit applies
    Given no state law or agreement limit is configured for the account
    When the final liability is determined
    Then the final liability equals the Reg E calculation

  Scenario: Source of limit is shown
    Given a lower limit from state law or agreement was applied
    Then the case displays which source was applied and the amount
```

## Story 8: Audit trail and decision transparency

**As a** Compliance Officer
**I want** every liability determination to show its inputs, rule path and result
**So that** decisions can be reviewed and explained.

```gherkin
Feature: Liability determination audit trail

  Scenario: Determination is stored with all inputs
    When a liability determination completes
    Then the case stores the precondition results, notice date, statement transmittal date, transfer amounts and dates, rule path applied, extensions, any state or agreement limit, and the final amount

  Scenario: Re-determination preserves history
    Given an input on the case changes after a determination

    Then the prior result remains viewable with its inputs
    And the new result is linked to the change that triggered it
```

## Open Questions (the provided text does not resolve these)

1. **"Business day":** Not defined in the text. The two-business-day calculation needs a configured calendar and definition, which must come from the full regulation or Compliance.
2. **Date consumer "learned" of the loss or theft:** The text keys the two-day window to this date, but does not say how to capture or evidence it. Stories 2 and 3 assume it is captured as a case field.
3. **Combined cap in § 1005.6(b)(3), access-device case:** The text says the consumer "may be liable for other amounts" under (b)(1) or (b)(2) in addition to the 60-day amount. It does not say whether the $500 cap applies to the total. Story 4 shows both amounts without summing them, pending Compliance direction.
4. **Content of § 1005.7(b)(1)–(3) disclosures:** The text only references them, so Story 1 treats them as a Boolean "provided" status.
5. **"Series of related unauthorized transfers":** The text does not say how transfers are linked. A grouping rule is needed.
6. **"Reasonable period" for extensions:** Story 5 leaves the length to analyst judgment, with no system default.
7. **Burden of proof:** The text puts the burden on the institution for post-notice-window transfers in (b)(2)(ii) and (b)(3). The stories exclude those amounts unless evidence is recorded.
