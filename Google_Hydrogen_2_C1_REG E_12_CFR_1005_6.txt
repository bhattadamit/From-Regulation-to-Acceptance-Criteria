Here is a comprehensive set of user stories and Gherkin acceptance criteria covering the consumer liability tiers under Regulation E (12 CFR § 1005.6).

---

### Epic: Regulation E Consumer Liability Calculation Engine (12 CFR § 1005.6)

**Business Objective:** Automatically calculate the maximum dollar amount of consumer liability for unauthorized Electronic Fund Transfers (EFTs) based on the type of transfer (Access Device vs. Non-Access Device), the date the consumer learned of the loss/theft, the periodic statement transmittal date, and the dispute reporting date.

---

### Story 1: Liability Calculation with Lost or Stolen Access Device (2-Business-Day & 60-Day Rules)

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Specialist,

**I want** the system to automatically calculate consumer liability when an unauthorized EFT involves a lost or stolen access device (e.g., physical debit card),

**So that** the consumer is only held liable up to the statutory limits ($50 or $500) based on when they notified the bank.

#### Acceptance Criteria (Gherkin)

```gherkin
Feature: Reg E Liability - Lost or Stolen Access Device (12 CFR 1005.6(b)(1)-(2))

  Background:
    Given a Reg E dispute claim is opened for an unauthorized EFT
    And the unauthorized EFT involved a lost or stolen access device
    And the bank has met all initial disclosure requirements under 12 CFR 1005.7

  Scenario: Consumer reports lost/stolen card within 2 business days of learning of loss
    Given the consumer learned of the loss or theft on "2026-10-01" (Thursday)
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-10-05" (Monday, 2 business days later)
    And unauthorized transfers totaling "$800.00" occurred before notification
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the maximum consumer liability is capped at the lesser of "$50.00" or the amount of unauthorized transfers ("$50.00")
    And the consumer has "$0.00" liability for any unauthorized transfers occurring after notification

  Scenario: Consumer reports lost/stolen card after 2 business days, but within 60 days of periodic statement
    Given the consumer learned of the loss or theft on "2026-10-01" (Thursday)
    And unauthorized transfers of "$100.00" occurred within the first 2 business days ("2026-10-01" to "2026-10-05")
    And unauthorized transfers of "$600.00" occurred after the 2-business-day window ("2026-10-06" to "2026-10-10")
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-10-10" (more than 2 business days after learning of loss, before statement close)
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the liability for the first 2 business days is capped at "$50.00"
    And the remaining liability cap up to "$500.00" ("$450.00") is applied to the post-2-day transfers
    And the total maximum consumer liability is "$500.00"

  Scenario: Consumer reports lost/stolen card after 2 business days, with total loss under $500
    Given the consumer learned of the loss or theft on "2026-10-01"
    And unauthorized transfers of "$30.00" occurred within the first 2 business days
    And unauthorized transfers of "$200.00" occurred after the 2-business-day window
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-10-12"
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the liability for the first 2 business days is "$30.00" (lesser of $50 or actual loss)
    And the liability for the post-2-day transfers is "$200.00"
    And the total maximum consumer liability is "$230.00"

```

### Story 2: Liability Calculation Without an Access Device (60-Day Statement Rule)

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Specialist,

**I want** the system to apply the 60-day periodic statement rule when an unauthorized EFT does not involve a lost or stolen access device (e.g., unauthorized ACH debit or card-not-present transaction using compromised credentials),

**So that** the consumer is protected from the $50/$500 tiers and only bears liability for transfers occurring after the 60-day reporting window.

#### Acceptance Criteria (Gherkin)

```gherkin
Feature: Reg E Liability - No Access Device Involved (12 CFR 1005.6(b)(3))

  Background:
    Given a Reg E dispute claim is opened for an unauthorized EFT
    And the unauthorized EFT did NOT involve a lost or stolen access device

  Scenario: Consumer reports unauthorized EFT within 60 days of periodic statement transmittal
    Given the periodic statement showing the first unauthorized EFT was transmitted on "2026-09-01"
    And unauthorized transfers totaling "$2,500.00" occurred between "2026-08-15" and "2026-10-25"
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-10-25" (54 days after statement transmittal)
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer liability is "$0.00"

  Scenario: Consumer fails to report unauthorized EFT within 60 days of periodic statement transmittal
    Given the periodic statement showing the first unauthorized EFT was transmitted on "2026-09-01"
    And the 60-day reporting window expired on "2026-10-31"
    And unauthorized transfers of "$400.00" occurred on or before "2026-10-31"
    And unauthorized transfers of "$1,200.00" occurred after "2026-10-31" before notification
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-11-15"
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer liability for transfers on or before "2026-10-31" is "$0.00"
    And the consumer liability for transfers after "2026-10-31" is "$1,200.00" (unlimited liability for post-60-day transfers)
    And the total consumer liability is "$1,200.00"

```

**So that** the system extends the 2-business-day and 60-day reporting deadlines to a reasonable period in compliance with Regulation E.

```gherkin

  Scenario: Adjudicator applies an extenuating circumstances extension

    And the consumer provided valid documentation of hospitalization from "2026-09-28" to "2026-10-20"
    When the specialist selects "Extenuating Circumstances Approved"
    And sets the extended reasonable reporting deadline to "2026-10-25"
    And the consumer reported the unauthorized EFT on "2026-10-23"

    And the system logs the specialist's User ID, timestamp, and reason code in the audit trail

```

### Story 5: Bank Prerequisite & State Law / Network Zero-Liability Overrides

**As a** Compliance Officer,

**I want** the system to enforce bank disclosure prerequisites (12 CFR 1005.6(a)) and apply stricter state laws or card network zero-liability rules (12 CFR 1005.6(b)(6)),

**So that** the bank never holds a consumer liable when legally prohibited or when network rules offer greater protection.

#### Acceptance Criteria (Gherkin)

```gherkin
Feature: Reg E Liability - Prerequisites and Lesser Liability Overrides (12 CFR 1005.6(a) & (b)(6))

  Scenario: Bank failed to provide required initial disclosures or notice mechanism
    Given a Reg E dispute claim is opened for an unauthorized EFT
    When the system checks account onboarding records and finds initial disclosures (12 CFR 1005.7) were not delivered
    Then the consumer liability is automatically set to "$0.00" regardless of reporting timelines

  Scenario: Card network Zero Liability policy applies (Lesser Liability Rule)
    Given a Reg E dispute claim involves a Visa or Mastercard branded debit card
    And the card network's Zero Liability policy applies to the disputed transactions
    And the statutory Reg E calculation yields "$50.00" in consumer liability
    When the final liability determination runs
    Then the system applies the lesser liability under 12 CFR 1005.6(b)(6)
    And sets final consumer liability to "$0.00"
    And displays both "Statutory Reg E Liability: $50.00" and "Applied Customer Liability: $0.00 (Network Rule)" on the claim summary

```

### Story 6: Notice to Bank & Mailbox Rule Timestamping (12 CFR 1005.6(b)(5))

**As a** Disputes Intake Agent,

**I want** the system to capture the exact notification channel and timestamp (including postmark/mailing date if notified by mail),

**So that** liability cutoffs are determined accurately even if the consumer notifies a branch or mails a letter rather than calling the dedicated dispute line.

#### Acceptance Criteria (Gherkin)

```gherkin
Feature: Reg E Liability - Notice to Financial Institution (12 CFR 1005.6(b)(5))

  Scenario: Consumer notifies the bank via mail
    Given a consumer mails a written notice of unauthorized EFTs postmarked on "2026-10-03"
    And the bank receives and logs the mail on "2026-10-07"
    When the intake agent enters "Mail" as the notification channel with a postmark date of "2026-10-03"
    Then the system uses "2026-10-03" as the official notification date for all Reg E liability cutoff calculations

  Scenario: Consumer notifies the bank in person at a local branch
    Given a consumer informs a teller at a retail branch of a stolen debit card on "2026-10-02" at "14:15 EST"
    And the formal dispute ticket is not opened until "2026-10-04"
    When the agent logs the branch notification timestamp as "2026-10-02 14:15 EST"
    Then the system uses "2026-10-02 14:15 EST" as the official notification timestamp
    And any unauthorized transfers occurring after "2026-10-02 14:15 EST" are assigned "$0.00" consumer liability

```

### Key Implementation Notes for Engineering

* **Business Day Definition (12 CFR 1005.2(d)):** Ensure the engine uses the bank's official business calendar (excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and federal holidays) when calculating the **2-business-day** rule, while using **calendar days** for the **60-day** periodic statement rule.
* **Negligence Prohibition:** Under Official Staff Commentary to § 1005.6(b), consumer negligence (e.g., writing a PIN on a card) **cannot** be used to increase liability beyond the statutory limits. Do not include negligence inputs in the calculation engine.
