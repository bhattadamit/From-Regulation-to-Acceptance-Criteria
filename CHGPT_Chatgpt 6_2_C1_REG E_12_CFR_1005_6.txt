# **Regulation E – Consumer Liability for Unauthorized Electronic Fund Transfers**

**Business requirements and user stories for a US bank disputes platform**

Regulatory: 12 CFR § 1005.6

Product: Disputes platform

Business analysis specification

## **1. Business objective**

Implement the consumer liability requirements of Regulation E, 12 CFR § 1005.6, within the bank's disputes platform to ensure that consumer liability for unauthorized electronic fund transfers (EFTs) is determined accurately, consistently, and in accordance with applicable regulatory requirements.

The platform must:

1. Capture when a consumer first becomes aware of an unauthorized transfer or loss/theft of an access device.
2. Capture when the consumer notifies the bank.
3. Calculate applicable reporting deadlines.
4. Determine the appropriate consumer liability threshold.
5. Identify potentially unlimited liability arising from delayed notification, where legally applicable.
6. Apply regulatory exceptions and consumer protections.
7. Maintain an auditable record of the liability determination.
8. Support downstream dispute processing, provisional credit, adjustments, and customer communications without conflating Regulation E liability with investigation requirements under § 1005.11.

## **2. Regulatory interpretation and business rules**

The following rules provide the foundation for the user stories.

### **2.1 Consumer liability thresholds**

Under 12 CFR § 1005.6(b), consumer liability depends on the circumstances and timing of notification.

**Scenario 1 – Notification within two business days**

# **$50**

Maximum potential consumer liability for unauthorized transfers resulting from an access device being lost or stolen, provided the consumer notifies the financial institution within two business days after learning of the loss or theft.

**Scenario 2 – Notification after two business days but within 60 calendar days**

# **$500**

Maximum potential consumer liability, provided the financial institution establishes that the unauthorized transfers would have been prevented had notification occurred within two business days.

**Scenario 3 – Notification more than 60 calendar days after statement transmission**

# **Potentially unlimited**

The consumer may be liable for unauthorized transfers occurring after the 60-day period and before notification, if the financial institution establishes that those transfers would have been prevented by timely notification.

**Important distinctions:**

- The $50 and $500 limits are maximum potential liability, not automatic charges to the consumer.
- The 60-day period is measured from transmission of the periodic statement reflecting the unauthorized EFT, not necessarily from the transaction date.
- Liability for transfers occurring after the 60-day period is limited to those occurring after that period and before notification, subject to the bank's burden of proof.
- A consumer generally has no liability for unauthorized transfers occurring after notification.
- Additional statutory exceptions may eliminate or limit liability.

### **2.2 Regulatory business rules**

| Rule ID | Business rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| REG-001 | If the consumer notifies the bank within two business days after learning of the loss or theft of an access device, maximum potential liability is $50.                                                                                |
| REG-002 | If notification occurs after two business days, the maximum potential liability may be $500, subject to the bank establishing preventability.                                                                                          |
| REG-003 | If notification occurs more than 60 calendar days after transmission of the statement reflecting the unauthorized EFT, liability may extend to subsequent unauthorized transfers before notification, subject to statutory conditions. |
| REG-004 | The bank must establish that the consumer's failure to notify within the applicable period resulted in additional preventable unauthorized transfers before imposing the applicable extended liability.                                |
| REG-005 | No liability applies to unauthorized transfers occurring after the bank has been notified of the loss or theft of the access device.                                                                                                   |
| REG-006 | No liability applies where the access device was not accepted, or where circumstances beyond the consumer's control prevented timely notification and notification was made as soon as reasonably possible.                            |
| REG-007 | The platform must distinguish between loss/theft reporting and the 60-day periodic statement notification requirement.                                                                                                                 |
| REG-008 | Consumer liability must not exceed the applicable statutory limits.                                                                                                                                                                    |
| REG-009 | Liability must be evaluated against the relevant unauthorized transfers, not simply assigned as a flat amount to the entire dispute.                                                                                                   |

*Implementation note:* The legal and compliance teams should validate the treatment of access-device versus non-access-device transactions, the precise applicability of each liability tier, and any institution-specific consumer protections before production deployment.

## **3. Functional user stories and Gherkin acceptance criteria**

The stories below are organized by functional capability and can be translated into Jira epics and stories.

### **Epic 1 – Capture notification and transaction information**

#### **US-001: Capture consumer notification date and time**

**As a** disputes analyst,\
**I want** the platform to capture the date and time the consumer first notified the bank of a lost or stolen access device or unauthorized EFT,\
**So that** the platform can determine the applicable Regulation E liability period.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Capture consumer notification

  Scenario: Consumer reports a lost or stolen access device
    Given a consumer contacts the bank to report a lost or stolen access device
    When the disputes analyst creates a case
    Then the platform must capture the date and time of notification
    And capture the notification channel
    And capture the date and time the consumer learned of the loss or theft
    And retain the original notification timestamp
    And associate the notification with the affected account and access device

  Scenario: Consumer reports an unauthorized EFT
    Given a consumer reports an unauthorized electronic fund transfer
    When the disputes analyst creates a dispute
    Then the platform must capture the date and time of notification
    And associate the notification with the disputed transaction
    And identify whether the access device was lost, stolen, or otherwise compromised

  Scenario: Notification date is earlier than case creation
    Given the consumer previously notified the bank through another channel
    When the disputes analyst creates a dispute
    Then the platform must use the original notification date
    And must not automatically substitute the case creation date
```

**Key data attributes**

- Original notification date/time
- Notification channel (telephone, branch, online, written, other)
- Consumer awareness date/time
- Access device status
- Account identifier
- Dispute reference
- Date/time of case creation

#### **US-002: Capture periodic statement information**

**As a** disputes analyst,\
**I want** the platform to identify the periodic statement transmission date associated with an unauthorized EFT,\
**So that** the 60-calendar-day reporting requirement can be evaluated correctly.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Capture periodic statement information

  Scenario: Unauthorized EFT appears on a periodic statement
    Given an unauthorized EFT has been identified
    When the platform retrieves the associated periodic statement
    Then it must capture the statement transmission date
    And associate the statement with the disputed transaction
    And calculate the applicable 60-calendar-day notification deadline

  Scenario: Multiple transactions appear on different statements
    Given a dispute contains multiple unauthorized EFTs
    And the EFTs appear on different periodic statements
    When the platform evaluates consumer liability
    Then it must evaluate the applicable statement transmission date
    And the corresponding 60-day deadline for each relevant EFT

  Scenario: Statement transmission date is unavailable
    Given an unauthorized EFT has been identified
    And the statement transmission date is unavailable
    When the platform evaluates consumer liability
    Then it must flag the liability determination for manual review
    And must not assume the transaction date is the statement transmission date
```

#### **US-003: Capture unauthorized transaction details**

**As a** disputes analyst,\
**I want** the platform to capture all unauthorized EFTs associated with a consumer dispute,\
**So that** consumer liability can be calculated using the correct transactions and dates.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Capture unauthorized EFT details

  Scenario: Multiple unauthorized transfers are reported
    Given a consumer reports multiple unauthorized EFTs
    When the transactions are added to the dispute
    Then the platform must capture each transaction separately
    And record its transaction date
    And record its transaction amount
    And record its posting date
    And record its transaction classification
    And associate it with the relevant access device or account

  Scenario: Transaction occurs after consumer notification
    Given a consumer has already notified the bank of a lost or stolen access device
    When an additional unauthorized EFT occurs
    Then the platform must identify the transaction as occurring after notification
    And assign zero consumer liability to that transaction under the applicable Regulation E liability rule

  Scenario: Transaction classification is not eligible for Regulation E
    Given a transaction is submitted for dispute
    When the transaction is identified as outside the scope of an electronic fund transfer covered by Regulation E
    Then the platform must not automatically apply the Regulation E liability calculation
    And route the case to the appropriate dispute process
```

### **Epic 2 – Determine notification timeliness**

#### **US-004: Calculate the two-business-day reporting period**

**As a** disputes analyst,\
**I want** the platform to calculate whether notification occurred within two business days after the consumer learned of the loss or theft,\
**So that** the applicable $50 liability threshold can be evaluated.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Calculate two-business-day notification period

  Scenario: Consumer reports within two business days
    Given the consumer learned of the loss or theft of an access device
    And the consumer notified the bank within two business days
    When the platform evaluates liability
    Then the platform must classify notification as timely
    And identify the applicable maximum liability threshold as $50

  Scenario: Consumer reports after two business days
    Given the consumer learned of the loss or theft of an access device
    And notification occurred more than two business days later
    When the platform evaluates liability
    Then the platform must classify notification as delayed
    And identify the potential applicability of the $500 liability tier
    And require evaluation of additional preventable unauthorized transfers

  Scenario: Notification occurs on a weekend or bank holiday
    Given the consumer learns of the loss or theft before a weekend or bank holiday
    When the platform calculates the two-business-day reporting period
    Then it must exclude Saturdays, Sundays, and applicable legal public holidays
    And use the configured Regulation E business-day calendar

  Scenario: Consumer notification predates the dispute case
    Given the consumer previously notified the bank
    When a dispute is subsequently created
    Then the original notification timestamp must be used
    And the reporting period must not be recalculated from the case creation date
```

**Business rule:** The business-day calendar must be centrally configurable and aligned with the Regulation E definition of business day and the bank's applicable business-day determination. Do not substitute a calendar-day calculation.

#### **US-005: Calculate the 60-calendar-day reporting period**

**As a** disputes analyst,\
**I want** the platform to calculate whether the consumer notified the bank within 60 calendar days after transmission of the statement reflecting the unauthorized EFT,\
**So that** the platform can determine whether extended liability may apply.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Calculate 60-calendar-day notification period

  Scenario: Consumer reports within 60 calendar days
    Given an unauthorized EFT appears on a periodic statement
    And the consumer notifies the bank within 60 calendar days after statement transmission
    When the platform evaluates liability
    Then the platform must classify the notification as within the 60-day period
    And must not apply extended liability under the 60-day reporting provision

  Scenario: Consumer reports after 60 calendar days
    Given an unauthorized EFT appears on a periodic statement
    And the consumer notifies the bank more than 60 calendar days after statement transmission
    When the platform evaluates liability
    Then the platform must classify the notification as outside the 60-day period
    And identify potential extended liability
    And identify unauthorized EFTs occurring after the 60-day period and before notification

  Scenario: Consumer reports on the 60th calendar day
    Given the statement transmission date is known
    When the consumer notifies the bank on the 60th calendar day
    Then the platform must classify notification as within the 60-day period

  Scenario: Different statements have different transmission dates
    Given multiple unauthorized EFTs appear on different periodic statements
    When the platform evaluates the dispute
    Then it must calculate the 60-day deadline separately for each relevant statement
    And must not apply a single statement deadline indiscriminately to all transactions
```

### **Epic 3 – Calculate consumer liability**

#### **US-006: Apply the $50 consumer liability threshold**

**As a** disputes analyst,\
**I want** the platform to apply the $50 maximum liability threshold when the consumer provides timely notification,\
**So that** the bank does not assess liability exceeding the statutory limit.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Apply $50 liability threshold

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers total more than $50
    Given the consumer notifies the bank within two business days of learning of the loss or theft
    And unauthorized transfers occurred before notification
    And the total value of those transfers exceeds $50
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then maximum potential consumer liability must not exceed $50

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers total less than $50
    Given the consumer notifies the bank within two business days
    And the total value of eligible unauthorized transfers is $30
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then maximum potential consumer liability must not exceed $30

  Scenario: No unauthorized transfers occur before notification
    Given the consumer notifies the bank before any unauthorized transfer occurs
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then consumer liability must equal $0

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers occur after notification
    Given the consumer has notified the bank of the loss or theft
    And additional unauthorized transfers occur after notification
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then those subsequent transfers must not be included in consumer liability
```

#### **US-007: Apply the $500 consumer liability threshold**

**As a** disputes analyst,\
**I want** the platform to evaluate the $500 liability threshold when the consumer fails to report a lost or stolen access device within two business days,\
**So that** any additional liability is assessed in accordance with Regulation E.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Apply $500 liability threshold

  Scenario: Consumer reports after two business days
    Given the consumer did not notify the bank within two business days
    And unauthorized EFTs occurred during the applicable delayed reporting period
    When the platform evaluates consumer liability
    Then it must identify the potentially applicable $500 liability limit
    And calculate the value of eligible unauthorized transfers
    And require the bank to establish that timely notification would have prevented those transfers

  Scenario: Bank establishes preventability
    Given notification occurred after two business days
    And the bank establishes that the unauthorized transfers would have been prevented by timely notification
    When the platform calculates liability
    Then consumer liability must not exceed the lesser of $500 or the value of eligible unauthorized transfers

  Scenario: Bank cannot establish preventability
    Given notification occurred after two business days
    And the bank cannot establish that the unauthorized transfers would have been prevented by timely notification
    When the platform calculates liability
    Then the platform must not impose extended liability based solely on delayed notification

  Scenario: Eligible unauthorized transfers total less than $500
    Given the bank establishes preventability
    And eligible unauthorized transfers total $300
    When the platform calculates liability
    Then maximum potential consumer liability must not exceed $300
```

**Important implementation distinction:** The $500 tier is not a blanket $500 deductible. The calculation must reflect the statutory lesser-of limitation and the qualifying unauthorized transfers during the applicable period.

#### **US-008: Apply extended liability after the 60-day period**

**As a** disputes analyst,\
**I want** the platform to identify unauthorized transfers occurring after the 60-day notification period and before consumer notification,\
**So that** potentially extended consumer liability is calculated in accordance with § 1005.6(b)(3).

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Apply extended liability after 60 days

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers occur after the 60-day deadline
    Given the consumer did not notify the bank within 60 calendar days after statement transmission
    And additional unauthorized transfers occurred after the 60-day deadline
    And those transfers occurred before consumer notification
    When the platform evaluates liability
    Then it must identify those transfers as potentially subject to extended liability
    And calculate their aggregate value separately

  Scenario: Bank establishes that additional transfers were preventable
    Given unauthorized transfers occurred after the 60-day deadline
    And the bank establishes that timely notification would have prevented those transfers
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the platform may assign liability for those qualifying transfers
    And must exclude transfers occurring after notification

  Scenario: Bank cannot establish preventability
    Given the consumer notified the bank after the 60-day period
    And the bank cannot establish that additional transfers would have been prevented
    When the platform evaluates liability
    Then it must not automatically assign extended liability

  Scenario: No additional transfers occurred after the 60-day deadline
    Given the consumer notified the bank after the 60-day period
    And no unauthorized transfers occurred after the deadline and before notification
    When the platform calculates liability
    Then extended liability for subsequent transfers must equal $0

  Scenario: Transfers occur after notification
    Given the consumer notified the bank
    And additional unauthorized transfers occur after notification
    When the platform calculates liability
    Then those transfers must be excluded from consumer liability
```

**Business rule:** The platform must distinguish between the initial liability calculation and additional liability arising from unauthorized transfers after the 60-day period. It must not treat the entire disputed amount as automatically subject to unlimited liability.

### **Epic 4 – Apply regulatory exceptions and consumer protections**

**As a** disputes analyst,\
**I want** the platform to identify circumstances that prevented the consumer from notifying the bank within the required timeframe,\

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Apply circumstances beyond consumer control

  Scenario: Consumer was unable to notify the bank due to circumstances beyond their control
```

## **4. Consolidated liability decision matrix**

The following matrix should be used as the core decision logic for the platform.

| Condition                                                                     | Potential consumer liability                                                                                                                                     |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| No unauthorized EFT before notification                                       | $0                                                                                                                                                               |
| Notification within two business days of learning of loss/theft               | Lesser of $50 or qualifying unauthorized transfers before notification                                                                                           |
| Notification after two business days, but within the applicable 60-day period | Lesser of $500 or qualifying unauthorized transfers during the statutory delayed-notification window, if preventability is established                           |
| Notification after 60 calendar days from statement transmission               | Potential additional liability for qualifying unauthorized transfers occurring after the 60-day period and before notification, if preventability is established |
| Unauthorized EFT after notification                                           | $0                                                                                                                                                               |
| Qualifying circumstances beyond consumer control                              | Apply statutory exception                                                                                                                                        |
| Access device not accepted                                                    | Apply statutory no-liability protection                                                                                                                          |

**Critical calculation distinction**

The platform should implement the following as separate components rather than one simple liability cap:

1. Initial liability under § 1005.6(b)(1): Lesser of $50 or qualifying unauthorized transfers before notification.
2. Additional liability under § 1005.6(b)(2): Lesser of $500 or qualifying unauthorized transfers occurring during the statutory period beginning two business days after the consumer learns of the loss/theft and ending before notification, where the institution establishes preventability.
3. Additional liability under § 1005.6(b)(3): Qualifying unauthorized transfers occurring after expiration of the 60-day period and before notification, where the institution establishes preventability.

This distinction is important because the $500 provision is not a replacement flat cap on all disputed transactions, and the post-60-day provision can create additional liability beyond the initial statutory amount.

## **7. Suggested end-to-end process flow**

**1. Consumer reports unauthorized EFT**

Capture notification, awareness and transaction details

**2. Validate Regulation E applicability**

Determine covered EFT, access-device status and applicable protections

**3. Calculate notification deadlines**

Two-business-day period and 60-calendar-day period

**4. Determine liability tier**

Initial $50 liability, additional $500 liability, or extended liability

**5. Evaluate preventability and exceptions**

Validate evidence and statutory conditions

**6. Calculate final consumer liability**

Apply transaction-level calculations and applicable statutory limitations

**7. Approve and record determination**

Audit history, downstream processing and reporting

## **11. Senior business analyst recommendation**

I recommend structuring the implementation into four development capabilities rather than embedding all the logic directly into the disputes workflow.

| Capability                      | Responsibility                                                          |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Notification & deadline engine  | Calculate two-business-day and 60-calendar-day deadlines                |
| Regulation E liability engine   | Apply statutory thresholds and transaction-level liability calculations |
| Evidence & exception management | Capture preventability, exceptions and approvals                        |
| Audit & downstream integration  | Preserve decisions and communicate approved amounts to other systems    |

Recommended design

**Build the Regulation E liability calculation as a centralized, reusable rules engine.**

This is preferable to hardcoding liability rules into individual dispute types. It provides consistent treatment across debit cards, ATM transactions, ACH transactions and other covered EFTs, while allowing transaction-specific applicability rules.

One particularly important design decision: the platform should maintain three separate amounts:

- Total unauthorized transaction amount.
- Total potentially consumer-liable amount under Regulation E.
- Final approved consumer liability amount.

These amounts should never be treated as interchangeable.

This specification is a proposed business requirements baseline, not a legal opinion. Before implementation, Legal and Compliance should validate the precise application of § 1005.6(b)(1)–(3), especially transaction-level windows, access-device applicability, and the institution's burden of establishing preventability.
