# Reg E Consumer Liability for Unauthorized EFTs (12 CFR 1005.6): Disputes Platform Backlog

## 1. Scope, assumptions, and definitions

**In scope:** Determining and recording the maximum consumer liability for a transfer already classified as unauthorized under 1005.2(m). The platform calculates the cap and the liability amount, and it records the evidence.

**Out of scope (separate epics):** the investigation, provisional credit, and timelines in 1005.11, and the network or Zelle/ACH dispute rails.

**Assumptions (validate with Compliance and Legal before build):**
- I wrote this from my knowledge of the regulation and have not checked it against the current eCFR text or Official Interpretations. Compliance should confirm the citations.
- Business day follows the bank's disclosed definition under 1005.2(d). The calendar service owns that definition.
- Statutory amounts ($50 and $500) and time limits (2 business days and 60 days) are configurable parameters with effective dates, not hard-coded values.
- Anything more consumer-favorable than Reg E (card network zero-liability rules, state law, the account agreement) always wins.

| Term | Meaning in platform |
|---|---|
| Learning date | Date the consumer learned of the loss or theft of the access device (captured at intake) |
| Notice timestamp | When the consumer, or a constructive-notice event, first put the bank on notice |
| Access device involved | Flag: the unauthorized transfer used a card, PIN, token, or similar accepted access device |
| Statement transmittal date | Date the first periodic statement reflecting the unauthorized transfer was sent |

## 2. Rule summary driving the stories

| Scenario | Maximum liability |
|---|---|
| Conditions in 1005.6(a) not met | $0 |
| Device lost or stolen, notice within 2 business days of learning | Lesser of $50 or the unauthorized transfers before notice |
| Device lost or stolen, notice after 2 business days | Lesser of $500 or the sum of (i) the lesser of $50 or the transfers within the 2 days, and (ii) later transfers before notice that the bank establishes would not have occurred with timely notice |
| Unauthorized transfers on a periodic statement, not reported within 60 days of transmittal | Transfers after day 60 and before notice that the bank establishes would not have occurred with timely notice. If a device was involved, add the amounts from the two rows above |
| Extenuating circumstances (travel, hospitalization) | Time limits extended to a reasonable period |
| State law or agreement imposes less liability | The lesser liability governs |
| Any dispute | The bank bears the burden of proof |

## 3. Epic: Reg E consumer liability determination

### US-01: Evaluate conditions of liability (1005.6(a))

**As a** disputes analyst, **I want** the platform to confirm the legal preconditions for holding a consumer liable, **so that** we never assess liability we are not entitled to.

**Acceptance criteria**

```gherkin
Feature: Conditions for consumer liability

  Background:
    Given a dispute is classified as an unauthorized electronic fund transfer

  Scenario: All conditions satisfied
    Given the access device is recorded as an accepted access device
    And the bank provided a means to identify the consumer to whom the device was issued
    And the required 1005.7(b)(1), (b)(2) and (b)(3) disclosures were delivered before the transfer
    When the liability engine evaluates the dispute
    Then the liability eligibility status is "ELIGIBLE"
    And the engine proceeds to liability-cap calculation

  Scenario Outline: A failed condition caps liability at zero
    Given the condition "<condition>" is not satisfied
    When the liability engine evaluates the dispute
    Then the liability eligibility status is "NOT_ELIGIBLE"
    And the maximum consumer liability is $0.00
    And the reason "<condition>" is recorded on the dispute

    Examples:
      | condition                                        |
      | Accepted access device                           |
      | Means to identify consumer                       |
      | Required disclosures delivered (1005.7(b))       |

  Scenario: Missing disclosure evidence is treated as not satisfied
    Given no disclosure delivery record exists for the account at the transfer date
    When the liability engine evaluates the dispute
    Then the eligibility status is "NOT_ELIGIBLE"
    And the dispute is flagged "Evidence missing - disclosure"

  Scenario: Consumer negligence does not increase liability
    Given the consumer wrote the PIN on the card
    When the liability engine evaluates the dispute
    Then the engine applies the same caps as it would without the negligence
    And no negligence-based surcharge field is available to the analyst
```

### US-02: Capture and calculate notice timing

**As a** disputes analyst, **I want** accurate learning, notice, and business-day calculations, **so that** the correct liability tier is applied.

**Acceptance criteria**

```gherkin
Feature: Notice timing

  Scenario Outline: Notice channels are all effective
    Given the consumer contacts the bank by "<channel>"
    When the contact includes information reasonably necessary to identify the account and the unauthorized activity
    Then the notice timestamp is recorded as the time of that contact
    And it is recorded whether or not a specific employee received it

    Examples:
      | channel                          |
      | in person                        |
      | telephone                        |
      | written mail (time of mailing)   |
      | secure message / app             |

  Scenario: Constructive notice
    Given the bank becomes aware of circumstances leading to a reasonable belief that an unauthorized transfer has been or may be made
    When an analyst records a constructive-notice event with its source and time
    Then the notice timestamp is set to that event time
    And the notice type is "CONSTRUCTIVE"

  Scenario: The day of learning is not counted
    Given the consumer learned of the card loss on Monday 2026-11-02
    When the platform calculates the two-business-day deadline
    Then the deadline is end of day Wednesday 2026-11-04

  Scenario: Federal holidays are skipped
    Given the consumer learned of the card loss on Monday 2026-11-09
    And Wednesday 2026-11-11 is a bank non-business day
    When the platform calculates the two-business-day deadline
    Then the deadline is end of day Thursday 2026-11-12
    And notice given on 2026-11-12 is "TIMELY"
    And notice given on 2026-11-13 is "NOT_TIMELY"

  Scenario: Learning date is unknown
    Given the consumer cannot state when they learned of the loss
    When the analyst saves intake
    Then the analyst must record a learning date with a basis note
    And the platform does not default the learning date to the transfer date
```

### US-03: Apply the $50 cap (timely notice)

**As a** disputes analyst, **I want** liability capped at $50 when the consumer reports a lost or stolen device on time, **so that** we comply with 1005.6(b)(1).

**Acceptance criteria**

```gherkin
Feature: Tier 1 liability - timely notice

  Scenario Outline: Lesser of $50 or unauthorized transfers before notice
    Given an eligible dispute involving a lost or stolen access device
    And notice was given within two business days of learning
    And unauthorized transfers before notice total <before_notice>
    When the liability engine calculates liability
    Then the consumer liability is <liability>
    And the applied rule is "REG_E_6B1_TIMELY"

    Examples:
      | before_notice | liability |
      | $120.00       | $50.00    |
      | $50.00        | $50.00    |
      | $30.00        | $30.00    |

  Scenario: Transfers after notice carry no consumer liability
    Given notice was given at 2026-11-03 10:00
    And an unauthorized transfer of $200.00 posted at 2026-11-03 15:00
    When the liability engine calculates liability
    Then that $200.00 transfer is excluded from consumer liability
```

### US-04: Apply the $500 cap (late notice)

**As a** disputes analyst, **I want** the platform to compute late-notice liability and require evidence that later transfers were preventable, **so that** we comply with 1005.6(b)(2).

**Acceptance criteria**

```gherkin
Feature: Tier 2 liability - notice after two business days

  Scenario Outline: Late-notice formula
    Given an eligible dispute involving a lost or stolen access device
    And notice was NOT given within two business days of learning
    And unauthorized transfers within the two business days total <within_2bd>
    And unauthorized transfers after the two days and before notice total <after_2bd>
    And the bank evidence flag "would not have occurred with timely notice" is <evidenced>
    When the liability engine calculates liability
    Then the consumer liability is <liability>
    And the applied rule is "REG_E_6B2_LATE"

    Examples:
      | within_2bd | after_2bd | evidenced | liability |
      | $40.00     | $300.00   | true      | $340.00   |
      | $200.00    | $900.00   | true      | $500.00   |
      | $200.00    | $900.00   | false     | $50.00    |
      | $20.00     | $0.00     | true      | $20.00    |

  Scenario: Preventability evidence is mandatory for post-deadline transfers
    Given post-deadline transfers exist
    When the analyst attempts to include them in liability
    Then the platform requires an evidence entry with a rationale and source
    And the transfers cannot be included without it

  Scenario: Transfers after notice are never included
    Given a transfer posted after the notice timestamp
    When the liability engine calculates liability
    Then the transfer is excluded from consumer liability

  Scenario: Cap applies across tiers
    Given the computed sum exceeds $500.00
    When the liability engine calculates liability
    Then the liability is reduced to $500.00
```

### US-05: Apply the 60-day periodic statement rule

**As a** disputes analyst, **I want** the 60-day statement window evaluated, including unauthorized transfers that did not use an access device, **so that** we comply with 1005.6(b)(3).

**Acceptance criteria**

```gherkin
Feature: Periodic statement 60-day rule

  Scenario: Reported within 60 days
    Given the first statement reflecting the unauthorized transfer was transmitted on 2026-10-01
    And notice was given on 2026-11-20
    When the liability engine evaluates the 60-day window
    Then the 60-day deadline is 2026-11-30
    And the window status is "WITHIN_60_DAYS"
    And no statement-based liability is assessed

  Scenario: Reported after 60 days, no access device involved
    Given the first statement reflecting the unauthorized transfer was transmitted on 2026-10-01
    And the transfer was an ACH debit made without an access device
    And notice was given on 2026-12-15
    And unauthorized transfers after 2026-11-30 and before notice total $400.00
    And the bank evidence flag "would not have occurred with timely notice" is true
    When the liability engine calculates liability
    Then the consumer liability is $400.00
    And the applied rule is "REG_E_6B3_STATEMENT"
    And the $50 and $500 device tiers are not applied

  Scenario: Reported after 60 days, access device involved
    Given the transfer involved an access device
    And the 60-day window was missed
    When the liability engine calculates liability
    Then statement-based liability is calculated under REG_E_6B3_STATEMENT
    And the device-tier amount under 6(b)(1) or 6(b)(2) is added
    And the combined result and its components are displayed to the analyst

  Scenario: Transfers inside the window are not charged
    Given an unauthorized transfer that posted on or before 2026-11-30
    When the 60-day window was missed
    Then that transfer is not included in statement-based liability
    And it remains subject only to device-tier rules where applicable

  Scenario: Statement transmittal date missing
    Given no statement transmittal record exists
    When the liability engine evaluates the 60-day window
    Then the status is "INDETERMINATE"
    And the analyst must resolve it before a liability decision is finalized
```

### US-06: Extend time limits for extenuating circumstances

**As a** disputes analyst, **I want** to extend the notice deadlines when the consumer was prevented from reporting, **so that** the consumer is not held liable for a delay outside their control (1005.6(b)(4)).

**Acceptance criteria**

```gherkin
Feature: Extension of time limits

  Scenario: Extension applied with documented reason
    Given the consumer states they were hospitalized from 2026-11-03 to 2026-11-14
    When the analyst records an extenuating circumstance with category "Hospitalization" and supporting notes
    And a supervisor approves the extension
    Then the notice deadlines are recalculated to a reasonable extended date
    And the original and extended deadlines are both retained in the audit trail

  Scenario: Extension requires reason and approval
    When the analyst saves an extension without a reason category or supervisor approval
    Then the platform rejects the save

  Scenario: Recalculation changes the tier
    Given notice was previously classified "NOT_TIMELY"
    When an approved extension makes the notice timely
    Then liability is recalculated using the timely-notice tier
    And the prior calculation is retained as superseded

  Scenario: Extension categories
    Then the category list includes "Extended travel", "Hospitalization" and "Other (free text, supervisor review required)"
```

### US-08: Burden of proof and evidence record

**As a** compliance officer, **I want** every liability decision backed by retained evidence, **so that** the bank can show the transfer was authorized or that the liability conditions were met.

**Acceptance criteria**

```gherkin
Feature: Evidence and audit trail

  Scenario: Evidence package is assembled for every liability decision
    When the engine produces a liability amount greater than $0.00
    Then the dispute contains links to disclosure delivery records
    And access device issuance and acceptance records
    And the notice record with channel and timestamp
    And the statement transmittal record
    And the preventability evidence for any post-deadline transfers

  Scenario: Liability above $0 is blocked without a complete evidence package
    Given any required evidence item is missing
    When the analyst attempts to finalize liability above $0.00
    Then the platform blocks finalization
    And lists the missing items

  Scenario: Authorized-versus-unauthorized determination is recorded
    Given the dispute outcome is that the transfer was authorized
    Then the platform records the investigation basis supporting that finding
    And no Reg E liability calculation is run

  Scenario: Immutable audit log
    When any liability input, rule version, override or recalculation occurs
    Then the platform records user, timestamp, before and after values, and rule version
    And audit records cannot be edited or deleted by any user role
    And records are retained for at least 2 years
```

*(Reg E requires at least a 2-year retention period. If bank policy is longer, use the longer period.)*

### US-09: Decision output and downstream integration

**As a** disputes analyst, **I want** a clear liability decision and calculation breakdown, **so that** the consumer is correctly charged or credited and communications are accurate.

**Acceptance criteria**

```gherkin
Feature: Liability decision output

  Scenario: Calculation breakdown displayed
    When a liability calculation completes
    Then the screen shows eligibility status, notice timeliness, tier applied, each transfer included or excluded with reason, caps applied, governing source and final amount

  Scenario: Final liability is published to ledger and customer communication
    Given the analyst finalizes the liability decision
    Then the final amount is sent to the account ledger service
    And the consumer-notice generator receives the amount and the reason codes
    And the decision is not published if a calculation is in "INDETERMINATE" status

  Scenario: Manual override
    When an analyst overrides the calculated amount
    Then a reason and second-person approval are required
    And an override that increases liability above the calculated amount is rejected
    And an override that decreases it is permitted

  Scenario: Recalculation after new information
    Given a finalized decision exists
    When a new fact changes any input
    Then the engine recalculates and displays the difference
    And any resulting refund or debit requires approval before it is posted
```

## 4. Non-functional requirements

- **Configurability:** the cap amounts, time limits, business-day calendar, and rule effective dates are managed in a rules table with versioning and Compliance approval.
- **Determinism:** the same inputs and rule version always produce the same output, and the rule version is stored with every calculation.
- **Testing:** unit and regression suites built from every Examples table above, plus Compliance-reviewed test cases from the Official Interpretations.
- **Performance and availability:** a calculation returns in under 2 seconds at the 95th percentile, and the audit log is write-once.

## 5. Open questions for Compliance and Legal

1. **Aggregation:** when the 60-day rule and the device tiers both apply, confirm how the combined amount is capped, and whether the $500 limit applies to the total.
2. **Fraudulently induced transfers (scams):** confirm the bank's classification policy under 1005.2(m) and current CFPB guidance, because it determines whether those transfers ever reach this engine.
3. **Learning-date evidence:** confirm what level of proof we require, and how we treat a vague or unverifiable learning date.
4. **Business-day definition:** confirm the one in our disclosures, including how it treats Saturday posting and weekend cutoffs.
5. **Preventability evidence:** define what the bank accepts as showing a transfer "would not have occurred" (for example, card-block logs or velocity data).
6. **Network and agreement rules:** confirm the current zero-liability terms for each card program and for debit versus prepaid.
7. **Commercial and non-consumer accounts:** confirm these are excluded from the engine, since Reg E covers consumer accounts only.
