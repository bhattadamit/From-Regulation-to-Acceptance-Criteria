### US-01: Prerequisite Conditions for Consumer Liability (§ 1005.6(a))

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Engine

**I want to** validate whether all regulatory prerequisites for consumer liability are met prior to assessing any financial loss to the consumer

**So that** the bank does not hold a consumer liable when mandatory disclosures, device acceptance, or consumer identification mechanisms were not satisfied.

#### Acceptance Criteria

```gherkin
Feature: Prerequisite Conditions for Consumer Liability (12 CFR 1005.6(a))

  Scenario: Initial disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1)-(3) were not provided
    Given a dispute claim is filed for an unauthorized electronic fund transfer (EFT)
    And the bank cannot verify that disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3) were provided to the consumer
    When the disputes platform evaluates consumer liability
    Then the maximum consumer liability shall be calculated as $0.00

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfer involves an unaccepted access device
    Given a dispute claim is filed for an unauthorized EFT involving an access device
    And the bank has provided the disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    And the access device is not an accepted access device
    When the disputes platform evaluates consumer liability
    Then the maximum consumer liability shall be calculated as $0.00

  Scenario: Bank failed to provide a means to identify the consumer for the access device
    Given a dispute claim is filed for an unauthorized EFT involving an accepted access device
    And the bank has provided the disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    And the bank did not provide a means to identify the consumer to whom the device was issued
    When the disputes platform evaluates consumer liability
    Then the maximum consumer liability shall be calculated as $0.00

  Scenario: All prerequisite conditions for liability are satisfied (Access Device)
    Given a dispute claim is filed for an unauthorized EFT involving an accepted access device
    And the bank provided the disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    And the bank provided a means to identify the consumer to whom the device was issued
    When the disputes platform evaluates consumer liability
    Then the platform shall proceed to calculate liability limits under § 1005.6(b)

  Scenario: All prerequisite conditions for liability are satisfied (No Access Device)
    Given a dispute claim is filed for an unauthorized EFT that did not involve an access device
    And the bank provided the disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    When the disputes platform evaluates consumer liability
    Then the platform shall proceed to calculate liability limits under § 1005.6(b)

```

### US-02: Notice Receipt and Effective Notice Timestamp (§ 1005.6(b)(5))

**As a** Disputes Intake Specialist / System

**I want to** capture the legally effective notice date and time based on the channel and manner of notification

**So that** the 2-business-day and 60-day regulatory clocks are measured accurately against the moment notice was legally given.

#### Acceptance Criteria

```gherkin
Feature: Determination of Notice to Financial Institution (12 CFR 1005.6(b)(5))

  Scenario: Consumer provides notice in person or by telephone
    Given a consumer takes steps reasonably necessary to provide pertinent information regarding an unauthorized EFT or lost/stolen access device
    And the consumer notifies the bank in person or by telephone
    When the intake record is logged in the disputes platform
    Then the effective notice timestamp shall be set to the date and time the consumer provided the information, regardless of whether a specific employee or agent received it

  Scenario: Consumer provides written notice
    Given a consumer takes steps reasonably necessary to provide pertinent information in writing
    When the intake record is logged in the disputes platform
    Then the effective notice timestamp shall be set to the date and time the consumer mailed the notice or delivered it for transmission by any other usual means

  Scenario: Constructive notice triggered by bank awareness
    Given the consumer has not yet contacted the bank
    When the bank becomes aware of circumstances leading to the reasonable belief that an unauthorized transfer to or from the consumer's account has been or may be made
    Then the disputes platform shall set the effective notice timestamp to the date and time the bank became aware of those circumstances

```

### US-03: Two-Business-Day Rule for Lost or Stolen Access Devices (§ 1005.6(b)(1) & (2))

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Engine

**I want to** calculate consumer liability limits based on whether the consumer notified the bank within two business days of learning of a lost or stolen access device

**So that** liability is accurately capped at either the $50 tier or the $500 tier.

#### Acceptance Criteria

```gherkin
Feature: Access Device Loss or Theft Liability Tiers (12 CFR 1005.6(b)(1)-(2))

  Background:
    Given all prerequisite conditions under § 1005.6(a) are met
    And the unauthorized EFTs involve a lost or stolen access device

  Scenario: Timely notice given within 2 business days (Transfers >= $50)
    Given the consumer notifies the bank within 2 business days after learning of the loss or theft of the access device
    And unauthorized transfers totaling $120.00 occurred before notice was given to the bank
    When the disputes platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer's maximum liability shall be $50.00

  Scenario: Timely notice given within 2 business days (Transfers < $50)
    Given the consumer notifies the bank within 2 business days after learning of the loss or theft of the access device
    And unauthorized transfers totaling $35.00 occurred before notice was given to the bank
    When the disputes platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer's maximum liability shall be $35.00

  Scenario: Untimely notice after 2 business days (Both periods exceed minimums, capped at $500)
    Given the consumer notifies the bank more than 2 business days after learning of the loss or theft
    And $100.00 in unauthorized transfers occurred within the first 2 business days (Tier 1 capped at $50.00)
    And $600.00 in unauthorized transfers occurred after the close of 2 business days and before notice
    And the bank establishes that the $600.00 in transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified the bank within the 2-day period
    When the disputes platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer's maximum liability shall be $500.00

  Scenario: Untimely notice after 2 business days (Sum is less than $500)
    Given the consumer notifies the bank more than 2 business days after learning of the loss or theft
    And $30.00 in unauthorized transfers occurred within the first 2 business days
    And $200.00 in unauthorized transfers occurred after the close of 2 business days and before notice
    And the bank establishes that the $200.00 in transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified the bank within the 2-day period
    When the disputes platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer's maximum liability shall be $230.00

  Scenario: Untimely notice after 2 business days (Bank fails to establish preventability)
    Given the consumer notifies the bank more than 2 business days after learning of the loss or theft
    And $100.00 in unauthorized transfers occurred within the first 2 business days
    And $400.00 in unauthorized transfers occurred after the close of 2 business days and before notice
    And the bank fails to establish that the $400.00 in transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified the bank within the 2-day period
    When the disputes platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the liability for transfers after the close of 2 business days shall be $0.00
    And the consumer's maximum total liability shall be $50.00

```

### US-04: 60-Day Periodic Statement Reporting Rule (§ 1005.6(b)(3))

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Engine

**I want to** evaluate whether an unauthorized transfer appeared on a periodic statement and whether the consumer reported it within 60 days of statement transmittal

**So that** unlimited liability for subsequent preventable transfers is only applied when the 60-day reporting window is missed.

#### Acceptance Criteria

```gherkin
Feature: Periodic Statement 60-Day Reporting Window (12 CFR 1005.6(b)(3))

  Background:
    Given all prerequisite conditions under § 1005.6(a) are met

  Scenario: Consumer reports unauthorized transfer within 60 days of statement transmittal (No access device)
    Given an unauthorized EFT (not involving an access device) appears on a periodic statement
    And the consumer reports the unauthorized EFT within 60 days of the bank's transmittal of the statement
    When the disputes platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer's liability for the reported transfer and any subsequent transfers shall be $0.00

  Scenario: Consumer fails to report within 60 days and bank establishes preventability (No access device)
    Given an unauthorized EFT (not involving an access device) for $150.00 appears on a periodic statement
    And the consumer fails to report the transfer within 60 days of statement transmittal
    And $800.00 in subsequent unauthorized transfers occur after the close of the 60 days and before notice to the bank
    And the bank establishes that the $800.00 in subsequent transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified the bank within the 60-day period
    When the disputes platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer's liability for the initial $150.00 transfer shall be $0.00
    And the consumer's liability for the subsequent transfers shall be $800.00

  Scenario: Consumer fails to report within 60 days but bank cannot establish preventability
    Given an unauthorized EFT for $150.00 appears on a periodic statement
    And the consumer fails to report the transfer within 60 days of statement transmittal
    And $800.00 in subsequent unauthorized transfers occur after the close of the 60 days and before notice to the bank
    And the bank fails to establish that the $800.00 in subsequent transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified the bank within the 60-day period
    When the disputes platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer's liability for the subsequent transfers shall be $0.00

  Scenario: Combined liability when an access device is also involved after 60 days
    Given an unauthorized EFT involving a lost/stolen access device occurs and appears on a periodic statement
    And the consumer failed to report the loss/theft within 2 business days AND failed to report the statement error within 60 days of transmittal
    And $1,200.00 in unauthorized transfers occurred after the close of the 60-day period and before notice
    And the bank establishes that all post-2-day and post-60-day transfers would not have occurred with timely notice
    When the disputes platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the platform shall calculate up to $500.00 liability under § 1005.6(b)(2) for transfers within the 60-day window
    And the platform shall add $1,200.00 liability under § 1005.6(b)(3) for transfers occurring after the close of the 60-day window
    And the consumer's total maximum liability shall be $1,700.00

```

### US-05: Extension of Time Limits for Extenuating Circumstances (§ 1005.6(b)(4))

**As a** Disputes Investigator

**I want to** record extenuating circumstances that delayed a consumer's notification and extend the applicable 2-business-day or 60-day deadlines to a reasonable period

**So that** consumers are not penalized with higher liability tiers when delays were justified.

#### Acceptance Criteria

```gherkin
Feature: Extension of Time Limits for Extenuating Circumstances (12 CFR 1005.6(b)(4))

  Scenario: Extenuating circumstances extend the 2-business-day notice deadline
    Given a consumer notified the bank more than 2 business days after learning of a lost or stolen access device
    And the investigator documents that the delay was due to extenuating circumstances
    And the investigator sets an extended deadline representing a reasonable period
    When the consumer's notice timestamp falls on or before the extended deadline
    Then the disputes platform shall treat the notice as timely under § 1005.6(b)(1)
    And cap liability at the lesser of $50.00 or the unauthorized transfers prior to notice

  Scenario: Extenuating circumstances extend the 60-day periodic statement deadline
    Given a consumer reported an unauthorized transfer more than 60 days after periodic statement transmittal
    And the investigator documents that the delay was due to extenuating circumstances
    And the investigator sets an extended deadline representing a reasonable period
    When the consumer's notice timestamp falls on or before the extended deadline
    Then the disputes platform shall treat the notice as timely under § 1005.6(b)(3)
    And assess $0.00 liability for transfers occurring after day 60

```

### US-06: Lower Liability Override under State Law or Account Agreement (§ 1005.6(b)(6))

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Engine

**I want to** compare the calculated federal Regulation E liability against applicable state law and the consumer's account agreement

**So that** the final liability assessed to the consumer never exceeds the lowest cap imposed by federal law, state law, or contract.

#### Acceptance Criteria

```gherkin
Feature: Application of Lower State Law or Agreement Liability Cap (12 CFR 1005.6(b)(6))

  Scenario: Account agreement imposes zero liability (lower than Reg E)
    Given the disputes platform calculates a federal Regulation E liability of $50.00
    And the consumer's account agreement imposes a maximum liability of $0.00 for unauthorized transfers
    When the platform finalizes the consumer liability amount
    Then the final consumer liability shall be capped at $0.00

  Scenario: State law imposes a lower liability cap than Reg E
    Given the disputes platform calculates a federal Regulation E liability of $500.00
    And the applicable state law for the consumer's account imposes a maximum liability of $50.00
    And the consumer's account agreement imposes a maximum liability of $500.00
    When the platform finalizes the consumer liability amount
    Then the final consumer liability shall be capped at $50.00

  Scenario: Reg E imposes lower liability than state law or agreement
    Given the disputes platform calculates a federal Regulation E liability of $50.00
    And applicable state law imposes a maximum liability of $100.00
    And the consumer's account agreement imposes a maximum liability of $500.00
    When the platform finalizes the consumer liability amount
    Then the final consumer liability shall be capped at $50.00

```
