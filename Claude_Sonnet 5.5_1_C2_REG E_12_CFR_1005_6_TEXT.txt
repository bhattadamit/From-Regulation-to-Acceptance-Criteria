# Reg E Consumer Liability for Unauthorized Transfers (12 CFR 1005.6): User Stories and Acceptance Criteria

## Scope and assumptions

- Acceptance criteria are based only on the § 1005.6 text you provided. Anything it doesn't define is listed as an open item at the end rather than invented.
- The disputes platform is the system of record for the claim. It holds the unauthorized transfers (amount, date), the notice date, the date the consumer learned of the loss or theft, statement transmittal dates, and the disclosure and access-device flags.
- "Series of related unauthorized transfers" is handled as one claim group, per the lead-in to (b).
- All dollar examples are illustrative test data.

---

## Epic: Determine consumer liability for unauthorized EFTs

### US-1: Confirm conditions for liability (§ 1005.6(a))

**As a** disputes analyst
**I want** the platform to check the preconditions before assigning any liability to the consumer
**So that** we never hold a consumer liable when the regulatory conditions aren't met.

```gherkin
Feature: Conditions for consumer liability

  Background:
    Given an unauthorized EFT claim exists for a consumer account

  Scenario: Required disclosures not provided
    Given the institution has not provided all disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is $0
    And the determination reason is "Required disclosures not provided"

  Scenario: All required disclosures provided and no access device involved
    Given the institution has provided the disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    And the unauthorized transfer did not involve an access device
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the platform proceeds to the limitation rules in § 1005.6(b)

  Scenario: Access device was not an accepted access device
    Given the institution has provided all required disclosures
    And the unauthorized transfer involved an access device
    And the access device is not flagged as accepted
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is $0
    And the determination reason is "Access device not accepted"

  Scenario: Institution provided no means to identify the consumer
    Given the institution has provided all required disclosures
    And the unauthorized transfer involved an accepted access device
    And the institution did not provide a means to identify the consumer to whom it was issued
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is $0
    And the determination reason is "No means of identifying consumer provided"

  Scenario: Access device conditions fully met
    Given the institution has provided all required disclosures
    And the unauthorized transfer involved an accepted access device
    And the institution provided a means to identify the consumer to whom it was issued
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the platform proceeds to the limitation rules in § 1005.6(b)
```

### US-2: Cap liability when timely notice is given (§ 1005.6(b)(1))

**As a** disputes analyst
**I want** liability capped at the lesser of $50 or the unauthorized amount when the consumer gives notice within two business days of learning of the loss or theft
**So that** timely reporters are charged no more than the regulatory maximum.

```gherkin
Feature: Liability cap with timely notice

  Background:
    Given the conditions for liability in § 1005.6(a) are met
    And the unauthorized transfer involved loss or theft of an access device
    And the consumer notified the institution within two business days after learning of the loss or theft

  Scenario Outline: Liability is the lesser of $50 or unauthorized transfers before notice
    Given unauthorized transfers totaling <amount_before_notice> occurred before notice to the institution
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is <liability>

    Examples:
      | amount_before_notice | liability |
      | $0.00                | $0.00     |
      | $30.00               | $30.00    |
      | $50.00               | $50.00    |
      | $300.00              | $50.00    |

  Scenario: Transfers after notice are not charged to the consumer
    Given unauthorized transfers totaling $100.00 occurred before notice
    And unauthorized transfers totaling $75.00 occurred after notice
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is $50.00
    And the $75.00 after notice is excluded from consumer liability
```

### US-3: Cap liability when timely notice is not given (§ 1005.6(b)(2))

**As a** disputes analyst
**I want** liability calculated under the two-part formula when notice is late
**So that** the consumer's exposure is capped at $500 and the later-transfer component is charged only when the institution can establish causation.

```gherkin
Feature: Liability cap without timely notice (access device loss or theft)

  Background:
    Given the conditions for liability in § 1005.6(a) are met
    And the unauthorized transfer involved loss or theft of an access device
    And the consumer did not notify the institution within two business days after learning of the loss or theft

  Scenario Outline: Liability is the lesser of $500 or the sum of components (i) and (ii)
    Given unauthorized transfers within the two business days total <within_2bd>
    And unauthorized transfers after the close of two business days and before notice total <after_2bd>
    And the institution <establishes> that the after-period transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified within two business days
    When the liability determination runs
    Then component (i) is <comp_i>
    And component (ii) is <comp_ii>
    And the consumer liability amount is <liability>

    Examples:
      | within_2bd | after_2bd | establishes     | comp_i | comp_ii  | liability |
      | $40.00     | $0.00     | establishes     | $40.00 | $0.00    | $40.00    |
      | $200.00    | $100.00   | establishes     | $50.00 | $100.00  | $150.00   |
      | $200.00    | $100.00   | does not establish | $50.00 | $0.00 | $50.00    |
      | $200.00    | $600.00   | establishes     | $50.00 | $600.00  | $500.00   |

  Scenario: Causation evidence is required to include after-period transfers
    Given unauthorized transfers occurred after the close of two business days and before notice
    And no causation determination has been recorded by the institution
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the after-period transfers are excluded from consumer liability
    And the determination flags "Institution causation not established"

  Scenario: Analyst records causation basis
    When the analyst marks after-period transfers as "would not have occurred had notice been timely"
    Then the platform requires a written basis for the determination
    And the basis is stored with the claim
```

### US-4: Apply the 60-day periodic statement rule (§ 1005.6(b)(3), first two sentences)

**As a** disputes analyst
**I want** the platform to check whether the consumer reported an unauthorized transfer shown on a periodic statement within 60 days of the statement's transmittal
**So that** liability for subsequent transfers is applied correctly.

```gherkin
Feature: Periodic statement 60-day reporting rule

  Background:
    Given the conditions for liability in § 1005.6(a) are met
    And an unauthorized transfer appears on a periodic statement
    And the institution recorded the statement transmittal date

  Scenario: Consumer reports within 60 days of transmittal
    Given the consumer notified the institution within 60 days of the statement transmittal date
    When the liability determination runs
    Then no liability is assessed under § 1005.6(b)(3) for subsequent transfers

  Scenario Outline: Consumer reports after 60 days
    Given the 60-day period closed on the statement transmittal date plus 60 days
    And the consumer notified the institution <days_after_transmittal> days after transmittal
    And unauthorized transfers after the close of the 60 days and before notice total <after_60>
    And the institution <establishes> that those transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified within the 60-day period
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the § 1005.6(b)(3) liability amount is <liability>

    Examples:
      | days_after_transmittal | after_60 | establishes        | liability |
      | 70                     | $400.00  | establishes        | $400.00   |
      | 70                     | $400.00  | does not establish | $0.00     |
      | 90                     | $0.00    | establishes        | $0.00     |

  Scenario: Transfers within the 60 days are not charged under this paragraph
    Given the consumer notified the institution after the 60-day period
    And unauthorized transfers of $250.00 occurred within the 60 days
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the $250.00 is excluded from the § 1005.6(b)(3) liability amount

  Scenario: Statement transmittal date missing
    Given no statement transmittal date is recorded for the statement
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the platform blocks the § 1005.6(b)(3) calculation
    And routes the claim to a manual review queue with reason "Missing transmittal date"
```

### US-5: Combine periodic statement liability with access device limits (§ 1005.6(b)(3), last sentence)

**As a** disputes analyst
**I want** access-device claims to also evaluate the (b)(1) or (b)(2) amounts alongside the statement rule
**So that** all applicable liability under § 1005.6(b) is considered.

```gherkin
Feature: Access device claims with periodic statement reporting failure

  Background:
    Given the conditions for liability in § 1005.6(a) are met
    And the unauthorized transfer involved an accepted access device
    And the consumer failed to report within 60 days of the statement transmittal date

  Scenario: Access device involved: evaluate (b)(3) and the applicable (b)(1) or (b)(2) amount
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the platform calculates the § 1005.6(b)(3) amount
    And the platform calculates the applicable § 1005.6(b)(1) or (b)(2) amount based on the consumer's notice timing
    And the determination displays each component separately

  Scenario: No access device involved: only (b)(3) applies
    Given the unauthorized transfer did not involve an access device
    When the liability determination runs
    Then only the § 1005.6(b)(3) amount is calculated
    And no § 1005.6(b)(1) or (b)(2) amount is assessed
```

### US-6: Extend notice time limits for extenuating circumstances (§ 1005.6(b)(4))

**As a** disputes analyst
**I want** to extend the notice deadlines when the consumer's delay was due to extenuating circumstances
**So that** the consumer is evaluated against a reasonable period rather than the standard limits.

```gherkin
Feature: Extension of time limits

  Scenario: Analyst records extenuating circumstances
    Given a claim where the consumer's notice was late under the two-business-day or 60-day limit
    When the analyst selects "Delay due to extenuating circumstances"
    Then the platform requires a description of the circumstances
    And the platform requires a revised deadline representing a reasonable period
    And the platform recalculates liability using the revised deadline in place of the standard limit

  Scenario: Notice falls within the extended period
    Given an extension was granted with a revised deadline of 2026-11-25
    And the consumer notified the institution on 2026-11-24
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the notice is treated as timely under the applicable paragraph
    And the extension rationale and revised deadline are shown on the determination

  Scenario: Extension cannot be saved without a revised deadline
    When the analyst saves an extension without a revised deadline
    Then the platform rejects the save
    And displays "A reasonable-period deadline is required"

  Scenario: Both time limits can be extended
    Given extenuating circumstances apply
    Then the analyst can extend the two-business-day limit, the 60-day limit, or both
```

### US-7: Record when notice is given (§ 1005.6(b)(5))

**As a** disputes analyst
**I want** the platform to capture the notice date and channel according to the regulatory definition of notice
**So that** timeliness is measured from the correct date.

```gherkin
Feature: Notice to the financial institution

  Scenario Outline: Supported notice channels
    When the consumer provides notice by <channel>
    Then the platform accepts the notice as valid in form

    Examples:
      | channel      |
      | in person    |
      | telephone    |
      | in writing   |

  Scenario: Notice given when consumer takes reasonable steps
    Given the consumer took steps reasonably necessary to provide the pertinent information to the institution
    And no specific employee or agent actually received the information
    When the analyst records the notice
    Then the notice date is the date the consumer took those steps
    And the claim is not rejected for lack of receipt by a particular employee or agent

  Scenario: Written notice by mail
    Given the consumer sent written notice by mail
    When the analyst records the notice
    Then the notice date is the date the consumer mailed the notice
    And not the date the institution received it

  Scenario: Written notice delivered by another usual means
    Given the consumer delivered written notice for transmission by another usual means
    When the analyst records the notice
    Then the notice date is the date the consumer delivered it for transmission

  Scenario: Constructive notice
    Given the institution became aware of circumstances leading to the reasonable belief that an unauthorized transfer to or from the consumer's account has been or may be made
    When the analyst records constructive notice
    Then the platform requires the date the institution became aware and a description of the circumstances
    And the notice date used for timeliness may be set to that date

  Scenario: Notice date required before determination
    Given a claim has no recorded notice date
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the platform blocks the determination
    And displays "Notice date required"
```

### US-8: Apply lower liability from state law or agreement (§ 1005.6(b)(6))

**As a** disputes analyst
**I want** the platform to cap liability at the amount imposed by state law or the account agreement when that amount is lower
**So that** the consumer is never charged more than the most favorable applicable limit.

```gherkin
Feature: Liability under state law or agreement

  Scenario Outline: Lower state law or agreement limit applies
    Given the liability calculated under § 1005.6(b) is <calculated>
    And state law or the consumer agreement imposes liability of <other_limit>
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the final consumer liability amount is <final>

    Examples:
      | calculated | other_limit | final   |
      | $150.00    | $0.00       | $0.00   |
      | $500.00    | $100.00     | $100.00 |
      | $50.00     | $200.00     | $50.00  |

  Scenario: No state law or agreement limit on file
    Given no state law or agreement limit is configured for the account
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the final consumer liability equals the amount calculated under § 1005.6(b)

  Scenario: Source of the applied limit is shown
    Given a state law or agreement limit reduced the liability
    When the determination is displayed
    Then it shows the calculated amount, the limiting source, and the final amount
```

### US-9: Treat related transfers as a single series (§ 1005.6(b) lead-in)

**As a** disputes analyst
**I want** related unauthorized transfers grouped so one liability determination covers the series
**So that** caps are applied once per series rather than once per transfer.

```gherkin
Feature: Series of related unauthorized transfers

  Scenario: Caps applied to the series, not each transfer
    Given three related unauthorized transfers of $40.00 each occurred before timely notice
    When the analyst groups them as a related series
    And the liability determination runs
    Then the $50.00 cap is applied once to the series total of $120.00
    And the consumer liability amount is $50.00

  Scenario: Unrelated transfers are determined separately
    Given two unauthorized transfers are not grouped as related
    When the liability determination runs
    Then each is determined as a separate series
```

## Open items for Compliance/Legal confirmation

1. **Business day definition:** "Two business days" is used but not defined in the text provided. Make the business-day calendar configurable and have Compliance confirm the rule.
2. **Date the consumer learned of the loss or theft:** The platform needs a captured field. The text doesn't specify how to evidence it.
3. **Combining (b)(3) with (b)(1) or (b)(2):** The last sentence of (b)(3) says the consumer "may be liable for other amounts," but doesn't say how to aggregate them. US-5 shows components separately. Confirm the aggregation rule.
4. **§ 1005.7(b)(1)–(3) disclosure content:** Referenced in (a) but not provided. It is modeled as a yes/no flag.
5. **"Accepted access device" and "means to identify":** Modeled as flags. Definitions are not in the text provided.
6. **"Reasonable period" for extensions:** Left to analyst judgment with a mandatory rationale.
7. **Out of scope:** Error resolution timelines and provisional credit are not in this text, so no criteria were written for them.
