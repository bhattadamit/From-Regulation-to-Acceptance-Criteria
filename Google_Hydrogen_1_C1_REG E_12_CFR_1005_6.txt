Implementing Regulation E consumer liability rules (**12 CFR 1005.6**) in a disputes platform requires breaking down the liability tiers based on two primary variables: **whether an access device (e.g., debit card) was lost or stolen**, and **when the consumer notified the bank** relative to the event or statement transmittal.

These user stories are structured for an agile development team building the automated liability calculation engine within a banking disputes platform.

---

### Epic: Regulation E Consumer Liability Calculation Engine (12 CFR 1005.6)

#### Story 1: Tier 1 Liability — Timely Notice of Lost or Stolen Access Device (2 Business Days)

**As a** Disputes Operations Analyst,

**I want** the system to cap consumer liability at the lesser of $50 or the amount of unauthorized transfers when a lost or stolen access device is reported within two business days of learning of the loss or theft,

**So that** the bank complies with 12 CFR 1005.6(b)(1) and accurately calculates provisional credit and final settlement amounts.

**Acceptance Criteria:**

```gherkin
Feature: Reg E Tier 1 Liability Calculation (Lost/Stolen Device <= 2 Business Days)

  Background:
    Given a dispute claim is filed for an unauthorized electronic fund transfer (EFT)
    And the unauthorized EFT involved a lost or stolen access device (e.g., debit card)
    And the bank has fulfilled all initial disclosure requirements under 12 CFR 1005.7

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers exceed $50 and notice is given within 2 business days
    Given the consumer learned of the loss or theft on "2026-10-05" (Monday)
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-10-07" (Wednesday)
    And the total amount of unauthorized transfers prior to notice is "$400.00"
    When the liability calculation engine runs
    Then the maximum consumer liability must be calculated as "$50.00"
    And the bank's liability (reimbursement amount) must be "$350.00"

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers are less than $50 and notice is given within 2 business days
    Given the consumer learned of the loss or theft on "2026-10-05" (Monday)
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-10-06" (Tuesday)
    And the total amount of unauthorized transfers prior to notice is "$35.00"
    When the liability calculation engine runs
    Then the maximum consumer liability must be calculated as "$35.00"
    And the bank's liability (reimbursement amount) must be "$0.00"

  Scenario: Weekend and federal holiday exclusion in 2-business-day calculation
    Given the consumer learned of the loss or theft on "2026-10-09" (Friday)
    And "2026-10-12" (Monday) is a federal holiday (Columbus Day / Indigenous Peoples' Day)
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-10-14" (Wednesday)
    And the total amount of unauthorized transfers prior to notice is "$200.00"
    When the liability calculation engine runs
    Then the elapsed business days must be calculated as "2"
    And the maximum consumer liability must be capped at "$50.00"

```

#### Story 2: Tier 2 Liability — Delayed Notice of Lost or Stolen Access Device (Beyond 2 Business Days, Within 60 Days of Statement)

**As a** Disputes Operations Analyst,

**I want** the system to apply the tiered $500 liability cap when a consumer fails to report a lost or stolen access device within two business days,

**So that** the consumer is only held liable for transfers that occurred after the two-business-day window and could have been prevented had timely notice been given, per 12 CFR 1005.6(b)(2).

**Acceptance Criteria:**

```gherkin
Feature: Reg E Tier 2 Liability Calculation (Lost/Stolen Device > 2 Business Days)

  Background:
    Given a dispute claim is filed for unauthorized EFTs involving a lost or stolen access device
    And the consumer notified the bank more than 2 business days after learning of the loss/theft
    And the consumer notified the bank within 60 calendar days of the periodic statement transmittal

  Scenario: Transfers occur both within and after the 2-business-day window
    Given the consumer learned of the card theft on "2026-10-01" (Thursday)
    And the 2-business-day window expired at the end of "2026-10-05" (Monday)
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-10-08" (Thursday)
    And an unauthorized transfer of "$100.00" occurred on "2026-10-02" (within 2 business days)
    And an unauthorized transfer of "$600.00" occurred on "2026-10-07" (after 2 business days, before notice)
    When the liability calculation engine runs
    Then Tier 1 liability for the "2026-10-02" transfer is calculated as "$50.00" (lesser of $50 or $100)
    And Tier 2 liability for the "2026-10-07" transfer is calculated as "$450.00" ($500 overall cap minus $50 Tier 1)
    And the total maximum consumer liability must be "$500.00"
    And the bank's liability must be "$200.00" ($700 total unauthorized minus $500 consumer liability)

  Scenario: Preventability check — Transfers occur after 2 business days, but total is under $500
    Given the consumer learned of the card theft on "2026-10-01" (Thursday)
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-10-08" (Thursday)
    And an unauthorized transfer of "$30.00" occurred on "2026-10-02" (within 2 business days)
    And an unauthorized transfer of "$150.00" occurred on "2026-10-07" (after 2 business days, before notice)
    When the liability calculation engine runs
    Then Tier 1 liability is calculated as "$30.00" (lesser of $50 or $30)
    And Tier 2 liability is calculated as "$150.00" (amount that occurred after 2 business days)
    And the total maximum consumer liability must be "$180.00"
    And the bank's liability must be "$0.00"

  Scenario: All unauthorized transfers occurred within the first 2 business days despite late reporting
    Given the consumer learned of the card theft on "2026-10-01" (Thursday)
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-10-08" (Thursday)
    And an unauthorized transfer of "$300.00" occurred on "2026-10-02" (within 2 business days)
    And "$0.00" in unauthorized transfers occurred after "2026-10-05"
    When the liability calculation engine runs
    Then Tier 1 liability is calculated as "$50.00"
    And Tier 2 liability is calculated as "$0.00" (no transfers occurred after the 2-day window that could have been prevented)
    And the total maximum consumer liability must be "$50.00"
    And the bank's liability must be "$250.00"

```

**I want** the system to assign unlimited consumer liability for unauthorized transfers occurring after the 60-calendar-day statement window expires,

**Acceptance Criteria:**

```gherkin

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers occur both inside and outside the 60-day statement window (No lost/stolen device)
    Given the unauthorized EFTs did NOT involve a lost or stolen access device (e.g., ACH debit or card-not-present skimming)
    And the periodic statement showing the first unauthorized EFT was transmitted on "2026-08-01"
    And the 60-calendar-day window expired on "2026-09-30"
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-10-15"
    And unauthorized transfer A for "$300.00" occurred on "2026-07-25" (reflected on the Aug 1 statement)
    And unauthorized transfer B for "$500.00" occurred on "2026-09-15" (within 60 days of Aug 1 statement)
    And unauthorized transfer C for "$1,200.00" occurred on "2026-10-05" (after 60-day window expired, prior to notice)
    When the liability calculation engine runs
    Then consumer liability for Transfer A ("$300.00") must be "$0.00" (reported within 60 days, no lost/stolen device)
    And consumer liability for Transfer B ("$500.00") must be "$0.00" (occurred before 60-day window expired)
    And consumer liability for Transfer C ("$1,200.00") must be "$1,200.00" (unlimited liability after 60 days)
    And the bank's total liability must be "$800.00"

  Scenario: Combined Tier 1/2 and Tier 3 (Lost/Stolen card unreported past 60 days)
    Given the consumer's debit card was stolen on "2026-07-20" and the consumer was unaware of the theft
    And the periodic statement showing a "$200.00" unauthorized transfer was transmitted on "2026-08-01"
    And the 60-calendar-day window expired on "2026-09-30"
    And a second unauthorized transfer of "$900.00" occurred on "2026-10-05"
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-10-10"
    When the liability calculation engine runs
    Then consumer liability for the "$200.00" transfer (within 60 days) is capped at "$50.00" (Tier 1 applies as consumer had not yet learned of theft prior to statement)
    And consumer liability for the "$900.00" transfer (after 60 days) is "$900.00" (Tier 3 unlimited liability)
    And the total consumer liability must be "$950.00"

```

**I want** the system to apply $0 consumer liability for unauthorized transfers that do not involve a lost or stolen access device when reported within 60 calendar days of the periodic statement,

**So that** consumers are not penalized under Tier 1 or Tier 2 rules for unauthorized ACH debits or compromised card numbers where the physical card remains in their possession (12 CFR 1005.6(b)(3)).

**Acceptance Criteria:**

```gherkin

  Scenario: Unauthorized ACH or Card-Not-Present (skimming) reported within 60 days of statement
    Given a dispute claim is filed for unauthorized EFTs totaling "$1,500.00"
    And the dispute intake flag "Access_Device_Lost_Or_Stolen" is set to "FALSE"
    And the periodic statement reflecting the first unauthorized EFT was transmitted on "2026-10-01"
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-11-15" (45 calendar days after statement transmittal)
    When the liability calculation engine runs
    Then Tier 1 ($50) and Tier 2 ($500) caps must NOT be applied
    And the maximum consumer liability must be "$0.00"
    And the bank's liability must be "$1,500.00"

```

#### Story 5: Extenuating Circumstances Extension Override

**As a** Senior Disputes Investigator,

**I want** the ability to manually override the notification deadlines due to documented extenuating circumstances,

**So that** the system recalculates consumer liability using a reasonable extended timeframe in compliance with 12 CFR 1005.6(b)(4).

**Acceptance Criteria:**

```gherkin
Feature: Reg E Extenuating Circumstances Extension

  Scenario: Investigator applies extenuating circumstances for extended hospitalization
    Given a dispute claim initially calculated "$500.00" in Tier 2 consumer liability because notice took 10 business days
    And the consumer provided documentation of emergency hospitalization during that period
    When the Senior Disputes Investigator selects "Extenuating Circumstances Approved"
    And enters an "Adjusted Reasonable Notification Deadline" of "2026-10-15"
    And enters a mandatory audit justification note
    Then the liability calculation engine must re-evaluate the claim using "2026-10-15" as the timely notice threshold
    And if the consumer notified the bank on or before "2026-10-15", recalculate consumer liability to Tier 1 (max "$50.00")
    And log the override user ID, timestamp, previous liability, new liability, and justification in the compliance audit trail

```

    When the liability calculation engine runs

### Key Technical & Compliance Notes for Engineering

* **Notification Definition (12 CFR 1005.6(b)(5)):** Notice is considered given when the consumer takes steps reasonably necessary to provide the bank with pertinent information, *whether or not a particular employee actually receives it*. If notice is mailed, it is effective upon **receipt** by the bank (or when the bank *should* have received it in the normal course of mail), whereas phone/in-app/branch notice is effective immediately upon submission.
* **Negligence Prohibition (Official Interpretation 1005.6(b)-2):** Do not build fields that increase consumer liability for "consumer negligence" (e.g., writing a PIN on the card). Under Reg E, negligence cannot be used to impose greater liability than the statutory tiers.
* **State Law & Card Network Rules:** Ensure the liability engine sits *before* a secondary "Card Network Zero Liability" (Visa/Mastercard) or "State Law Override" ruleset. Reg E sets the *maximum* consumer liability; network rules often reduce the consumer's out-of-pocket share to $0 even when Reg E allows $50 or $500.
