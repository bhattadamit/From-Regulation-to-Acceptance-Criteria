# Regulation E §1005.6: Consumer Liability for Unauthorized EFTs
**Disputes Platform: Epic with User Stories and Gherkin Acceptance Criteria**

## Scope and basis

Acceptance criteria are drawn only from the §1005.6(a)–(b) text provided. Where the text refers to something it doesn't define, I treat it as an input or flag it in the open questions at the end rather than inventing rules.

**Epic:** As a Disputes Operations Manager, I want the platform to determine consumer liability for unauthorized EFTs according to §1005.6, so that we never assess more liability than the regulation allows and can evidence every determination.

**Common Background** (applies to all features below):

```gherkin
Background:
  Given a dispute case exists for an unauthorized electronic fund transfer
        or a series of related unauthorized transfers on a consumer account
  And each transfer in the case has a date, an amount, and a link to the account
```

## US-01: Liability eligibility gate (§1005.6(a))

**As a** disputes analyst, **I want** the platform to confirm the bank's preconditions before any liability is calculated, **so that** we never hold a consumer liable when the regulation doesn't allow it.

```gherkin
Feature: Conditions for consumer liability

  Scenario: Required disclosures were not provided
    Given the bank has not provided the disclosures required by §1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is $0.00
    And the case records the reason "Required disclosures not provided"

  Scenario: Required disclosures were provided and no access device is involved
    Given the bank has provided the disclosures required by §1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    And the unauthorized transfer did not involve an access device
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the case proceeds to the limitation rules in §1005.6(b)

  Scenario: Access device is not an accepted access device
    Given the bank has provided the required disclosures
    And the unauthorized transfer involved an access device
    And the access device is not an accepted access device
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is $0.00
    And the case records the reason "Access device not accepted"

  Scenario: No means to identify the consumer to whom the device was issued
    Given the bank has provided the required disclosures
    And the unauthorized transfer involved an accepted access device
    And the bank did not provide a means to identify the consumer to whom it was issued
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is $0.00
    And the case records the reason "No means of identification provided"

  Scenario: All access device conditions are met
    Given the bank has provided the required disclosures
    And the unauthorized transfer involved an accepted access device
    And the bank provided a means to identify the consumer to whom it was issued
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the case proceeds to the limitation rules in §1005.6(b)
```

## US-02: Timely notice of lost or stolen access device (§1005.6(b)(1))

**As a** disputes analyst, **I want** liability capped at the lesser of $50 or pre-notice unauthorized transfers when the consumer gave timely notice, **so that** timely reporters are protected.

```gherkin
Feature: Timely notice cap

  Scenario Outline: Consumer notifies within two business days of learning of the loss or theft
    Given the consumer notified the bank within two business days after learning of the loss or theft of the access device
    And unauthorized transfers totaling <before_notice> occurred before notice to the bank
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is <liability>

    Examples:
      | before_notice | liability |
      | $30.00        | $30.00    |
      | $50.00        | $50.00    |
      | $200.00       | $50.00    |

  Scenario: Transfers after notice are excluded
    Given the consumer gave timely notice
    And an unauthorized transfer occurred after notice to the bank
    When the liability determination runs
    Then that transfer is excluded from consumer liability
```

*Note: the "transfers after notice" scenario is derived from the "before notice" wording in (b)(1). Compliance to confirm.*

## US-03: Timely notice not given for access device loss or theft (§1005.6(b)(2))

**As a** disputes analyst, **I want** liability computed as the lesser of $500 or the sum of two components when notice was late, **so that** late notice is handled correctly and the bank's burden of proof is enforced.

```gherkin
Feature: Late notice cap

  Scenario Outline: Late notice, component calculation
    Given the consumer failed to notify the bank within two business days after learning of the loss or theft of the access device
    And unauthorized transfers totaling <within_2bd> occurred within the two business days
    And unauthorized transfers totaling <after_2bd> occurred after the close of two business days and before notice
    And the bank <established> that the after-two-day transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified within the two-day period
    When the liability determination runs
    Then component (i) is <comp_i>
    And component (ii) is <comp_ii>
    And the consumer liability amount is <liability>

    Examples:
      | within_2bd | after_2bd | established     | comp_i | comp_ii  | liability |
      | $20.00     | $600.00   | established     | $20.00 | $600.00  | $500.00   |
      | $80.00     | $100.00   | established     | $50.00 | $100.00  | $150.00   |
      | $80.00     | $100.00   | not established | $50.00 | $0.00    | $50.00    |
      | $30.00     | $0.00     | not applicable  | $30.00 | $0.00    | $30.00    |

  Scenario: Component (ii) requires the bank to establish causation
    Given the case has unauthorized transfers after the close of two business days and before notice
    And no bank determination is recorded that these transfers would not have occurred with timely notice
    When the liability determination runs
    Then those transfers are excluded from consumer liability

  Scenario: Establishment evidence is recorded per transfer
    Given the bank has established that specific after-two-day transfers would not have occurred
    When the analyst saves the determination
    Then the platform stores the supporting rationale and the transfers it covers
```

## US-04: Periodic statement reporting window (§1005.6(b)(3))

**As a** disputes analyst, **I want** the platform to apply the 60-day statement rule, **so that** liability for later transfers is assessed only when the consumer failed to report in time.

```gherkin
Feature: Periodic statement 60-day rule

  Scenario: Consumer reports within 60 days of statement transmittal
    Given an unauthorized transfer appears on a periodic statement
    And the consumer reported it within 60 days of the bank's transmittal of that statement
    When the liability determination runs
    Then no liability is assessed under §1005.6(b)(3) for subsequent transfers

  Scenario: Consumer does not report within 60 days, no access device involved
    Given an unauthorized transfer appears on a periodic statement
    And the consumer did not report it within 60 days of the statement transmittal
    And unauthorized transfers totaling $300.00 occurred after the close of the 60 days and before notice
    And the bank established that these transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified within the 60-day period
    And the unauthorized transfers did not involve an access device
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is $300.00

  Scenario: Post-60-day transfers not established as preventable
    Given the consumer did not report within 60 days of statement transmittal
    And the bank has not established that the post-60-day transfers would not have occurred with timely notice
    When the liability determination runs
    Then those transfers are excluded from consumer liability

  Scenario: Transfers within the 60 days are not charged under this paragraph
    Given the consumer did not report within 60 days of statement transmittal
    And unauthorized transfers occurred before the close of the 60 days
    And no access device was involved
    When the liability determination runs
    Then those transfers are excluded from consumer liability

  Scenario: Access device involved, other amounts also apply
    Given the unauthorized transfer involved an access device
    And the consumer did not report within 60 days of statement transmittal
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the platform adds any applicable amount from §1005.6(b)(1) or (b)(2) to the (b)(3) amount
    And the case shows each component separately
```

## US-05: Extension of time limits (§1005.6(b)(4))

**As a** disputes analyst, **I want** to record extenuating circumstances and extend notice deadlines, **so that** delayed notice caused by such circumstances doesn't increase liability.

```gherkin
Feature: Extension for extenuating circumstances

  Scenario: Analyst records extenuating circumstances
    Given the consumer's delay in notifying the bank was due to extenuating circumstances
    When the analyst records the circumstances and a reasonable extended period
    Then the platform replaces the two-business-day and 60-day limits for that case with the extended period
    And the original limits and extended limits are both retained in the case history

  Scenario: Notice falls inside the extended period
    Given an extension has been recorded for the case
    And the consumer's notice was given within the extended period
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the notice is treated as timely for the extended limit

  Scenario: No extension recorded
    Given no extenuating circumstances are recorded
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the standard two-business-day and 60-day limits apply

  Scenario: Extension requires analyst rationale
    When the analyst attempts to save an extension without a rationale
    Then the platform blocks the save and requests a rationale
```

## US-07: Lower liability under state law or agreement (§1005.6(b)(6))

**As a** compliance officer, **I want** liability limited to any lower amount in state law or the account agreement, **so that** we honor the more consumer-favorable limit.

```gherkin
Feature: State law or agreement limit

  Scenario: Agreement imposes less liability than §1005.6
    Given the calculated liability under §1005.6 is $150.00
    And the consumer agreement limits liability to $50.00 for this transfer type
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the final consumer liability amount is $50.00

  Scenario: State law imposes less liability
    Given the calculated liability under §1005.6 is $500.00
    And applicable state law limits liability to $100.00
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the final consumer liability amount is $100.00

  Scenario: State law or agreement imposes equal or greater liability
    Given the calculated liability under §1005.6 is $50.00
    And the agreement would impose $200.00
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the final consumer liability amount is $50.00

  Scenario: Final amount is the minimum across all limits
    Given more than one lower limit applies
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the final consumer liability amount is the lowest applicable amount
    And the case shows which limit was applied
```

## US-08: Series of related transfers and determination audit trail (§1005.6(b) lead-in)

**As a** compliance officer, **I want** related unauthorized transfers evaluated as one series with a complete audit record, **so that** the cap is applied once and the determination can be defended.

```gherkin
Feature: Series of related unauthorized transfers

  Scenario: Related transfers share one liability cap
    Given three unauthorized transfers are flagged as related
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the §1005.6(b) limits are applied once to the series
    And not separately to each transfer

  Scenario: Unrelated transfers are determined separately
    Given two unauthorized transfers are not flagged as related
    When the liability determination runs
    Then each is evaluated under its own liability determination

  Scenario: Determination record
    When a liability determination completes
    Then the platform stores the inputs: disclosure status, access device status, notice date, learning date, statement transmittal date, extension, and applicable state or agreement limit
    And it stores the paragraph applied, each component amount, and the final amount
    And it stores the user, timestamp, and any rationale entered

  Scenario: Recalculation after inputs change
    Given a stored determination exists
    When an input such as the notice date is corrected
    Then the platform recalculates the liability
    And it retains the prior determination in the history
```

## Open questions for Compliance and Legal

1. **§1005.7(b)(1)–(3) content:** not provided, so US-01 treats disclosure status as a recorded yes/no input.
2. **"Business day" and "learning" date:** the text doesn't define business days or how the date of learning is evidenced. We need a bank policy and a source for this date.
3. **(b)(3) with an access device:** the text says the consumer "may be liable for other amounts" under (b)(1) or (b)(2). Please confirm whether those amounts simply add to the (b)(3) amount, as US-04 assumes, and whether any overall cap applies.
4. **Extension length:** (b)(4) requires only "a reasonable period." Who sets the length, and under what policy guidance?
5. **"Series of related" criteria:** the text doesn't define relatedness, so we need rules for the related flag in US-08.
6. **Which state laws and agreement terms apply:** US-07 needs a maintained reference source for the applicable limit per account.
7. **Out of scope on this text:** investigation, provisional credit, and error-resolution timelines are not covered by the provided text and are excluded.
