# Regulation E Consumer Liability for Unauthorized EFTs (12 CFR 1005.6): User Stories

## 1. Scope, assumptions and open questions

**In scope:** Rules in the disputes platform that decide whether a consumer is liable for an unauthorized EFT and, if so, how much. This follows the liability limits in §1005.6 and the definition of "unauthorized electronic fund transfer" in §1005.2(m).

**Out of scope (integration points only):** Error-resolution timelines, provisional credit and investigation (§1005.11), the content of §1005.7 disclosures, and claims involving transfers the consumer initiated (scams).

**Assumptions to confirm with Compliance/Legal:**

| # | Assumption / question |
|---|---|
| A1 | Day counts use the bank's business-day calendar (§1005.2(d)). The day the consumer learns of the loss is not counted, so the two-business-day window starts the next business day. Please confirm against Official Interpretations §1005.6(b). |
| A2 | If a transfer involved no access device (e.g., ACH debit using only an account number), only the 60-day rule in §1005.6(b)(3) applies, not the $50/$500 tiers. Please confirm. |
| A3 | Our deposit agreement or card-network zero-liability program may impose lower liability than Reg E, and §1005.6(b)(6) says the lesser liability governs. This is modeled as configuration. |
| A4 | The platform receives a system-of-record flag for each account/product on whether the §1005.7(b) disclosures were provided and when. |
| A5 | Transfers fraudulently induced by a third party who obtained the access device or credentials from the consumer are treated as unauthorized per Official Interpretation §1005.2(m). |
| A6 | Citation sub-paragraph labels and commentary references should be verified by Compliance before they go into the rules catalog. |

**Glossary:** *UEFT* = unauthorized EFT. *Timely notice* = notice within two business days of learning of the loss or theft. *Notice date* = the date the consumer took steps reasonably necessary to inform the bank (oral or written, any channel).

## 2. Liability decision flow

1. **Is it an unauthorized EFT?** (US-01)
2. **Are the §1005.6(a) conditions met?** Required disclosures given, accepted access device, and a means to identify the consumer (US-02). If not, consumer liability is $0.
3. **Was an access device lost or stolen?**
   - **Yes:** apply the $50 or $500 tier based on timely notice (US-04 to US-06), then the 60-day rule (US-07).
   - **No:** apply the 60-day rule only.
4. **Extensions** for extenuating circumstances (US-08).
5. **Lesser of** the Reg E result and any lower agreement or state-law liability (US-09).
6. **Persist** the evidence, the burden-of-proof record and a versioned calculation (US-10 to US-12).

## 3. Epic: Consumer liability determination

### US-01: Classify a disputed transfer as authorized or unauthorized

**As a** disputes analyst, **I want** the platform to apply the Reg E definition of "unauthorized EFT" to each disputed transaction, **so that** liability is only calculated for transfers that qualify.

**Acceptance criteria**

```gherkin
Feature: Unauthorized EFT classification (12 CFR 1005.2(m))

  Background:
    Given a consumer dispute with one or more disputed EFT transactions

  Scenario Outline: Exclusions from the definition of unauthorized EFT
    Given the investigator records the finding "<finding>" for transaction "T1"
    When the analyst saves the classification
    Then transaction "T1" is classified "<classification>"
    And consumer liability calculation "<calc>"

    Examples:
      | finding                                                                           | classification | calc                |
      | Initiated by a person the consumer furnished the device to; no revocation notice  | Authorized     | is not executed     |
      | Same person, but consumer had notified the bank before the transfer               | Unauthorized   | is executed         |
      | Initiated with fraudulent intent by the consumer or someone acting in concert     | Authorized     | is not executed     |
      | Initiated by the bank or a bank employee                                          | Excluded       | is not executed     |
      | Person obtained the device or credentials from consumer by fraud or robbery       | Unauthorized   | is executed         |
      | Third party with no authority used lost or stolen card                            | Unauthorized   | is executed         |

  Scenario: Classification requires a documented rationale
    When the analyst attempts to save a classification without selecting a finding or entering a rationale
    Then the platform blocks the save
    And displays "A finding and rationale are required"

  Scenario: Transaction-level classification within a multi-transaction dispute
    Given a dispute with transactions "T1" and "T2"
    When "T1" is classified "Unauthorized" and "T2" is classified "Authorized"
    Then only "T1" is included in the liability calculation
```

```gherkin

    And notice was given on "<notice>"
    When the engine evaluates timeliness
    Then the notice is "<result>"
    And the window end date is "<window_end>"

    Examples:
      | learned    | notice     | window_end | result   |
      | 2026-11-16 | 2026-11-17 | 2026-11-18 | Timely   |
      | 2026-11-16 | 2026-11-18 | 2026-11-18 | Timely   |
      | 2026-11-16 | 2026-11-19 | 2026-11-18 | Untimely |
      | 2026-11-20 | 2026-11-24 | 2026-11-24 | Timely   |
      | 2026-11-24 | 2026-11-30 | 2026-11-30 | Untimely |

  Scenario: Thanksgiving holiday is skipped
    Given "2026-11-26" is a bank holiday
    And the consumer learned of the loss on "2026-11-25"
    When the engine computes the window end
    Then the window end date is "2026-11-30"

  Scenario: Knowledge date is required for the access-device tiers
    Given an access device was lost or stolen
    And the learned-of-loss date is blank
    When the engine runs
    Then the case is routed to "Manual Compliance Review"
    And the Tier 2 ($500) amount is not applied automatically
```

*Note: the window end dates in the table follow assumption A1. Weekend and holiday handling is driven by the bank calendar.*

### US-06: Apply the $500 cap when notice is untimely

**As a** liability engine, **I want** to apply the higher cap only when the bank establishes that later transfers would have been prevented by timely notice, **so that** we remain compliant while recovering permissible amounts.

```gherkin
Feature: Untimely notice liability cap (12 CFR 1005.6(b)(2))

  Scenario Outline: Compute liability when notice is untimely
    Given an access device was lost or stolen
    And notice was NOT given within two business days
    And unauthorized transfers within the two-business-day window total "<within>"
    And unauthorized transfers after the window and before notice total "<after>"
    And the bank "<establishes>" that the later transfers would not have occurred with timely notice
    When the engine calculates liability
    Then the consumer liability is "<liability>"

    Examples:
      | within   | after     | establishes         | liability |
      | $20.00   | $100.00   | establishes         | $120.00   |
      | $200.00  | $600.00   | establishes         | $500.00   |
      | $200.00  | $1000.00  | does not establish  | $50.00    |
      | $0.00    | $450.00   | establishes         | $450.00   |
      | $0.00    | $900.00   | establishes         | $500.00   |

  Scenario: The "would not have occurred" showing is mandatory
    Given notice was untimely
    And no causation evidence is attached
    When the analyst attempts to finalize a liability above $50
    Then the platform blocks finalization
    And displays "Evidence that transfers would have been prevented by timely notice is required"

  Scenario: Formula is shown transparently
    When the engine produces a Tier 2 result
    Then the output shows min($500, min($50, within_window) + after_window_established)
```

### US-07: Apply the 60-day periodic statement rule

**As a** liability engine, **I want** to apply the 60-day reporting rule, **so that** consumers are held liable for later transfers only when they failed to report within 60 days of statement transmittal.

```gherkin
Feature: Periodic statement 60-day rule (12 CFR 1005.6(b)(3))

  Scenario: Reported within 60 days
    Given a periodic statement was transmitted on "2026-10-01"
    And it shows an unauthorized transfer of $100 on "2026-10-10"
    And the consumer gave notice on "2026-11-15"
    When the engine evaluates the 60-day rule
    Then the 60th day is "2026-11-30"
    And no additional liability is added under the 60-day rule

  Scenario: Failure to report within 60 days, no access device involved
    Given the unauthorized transfers did not involve an access device
    And a statement transmitted on "2026-10-01" showed an unauthorized transfer of $100
    And a further unauthorized transfer of $250 occurred on "2026-12-05"
    And the consumer gave notice on "2026-12-15"
    And the bank establishes the $250 transfer would not have occurred with timely notice
    When the engine calculates liability
    Then the consumer liability is $250.00
    And the $100 transfer within the 60 days is not charged

  Scenario: Failure to report within 60 days, access device involved
    Given a lost or stolen access device is involved
    And the consumer missed the 60-day deadline
    When the engine calculates liability
    Then liability equals the Tier 1 or Tier 2 amount plus the established post-60-day transfers

  Scenario: Statement transmittal date is the clock start
    Given the system holds both the statement cycle date and the transmittal date
    When the engine calculates the 60-day deadline
    Then the deadline is computed from the transmittal date
```

  Examples:
      | circumstance        | days |
      | Extended travel     | 30   |
      | Hospitalization     | 14   |

## 4. Non-functional requirements

- **Accuracy:** Calculations use decimal arithmetic with no rounding errors, and have automated regression tests covering every example table above.
- **Calendar:** A single maintained bank business-day calendar, with holiday updates under change control.
- **Time zone:** Notice timestamps are stored with the original time zone and evaluated in the bank's official time zone.
- **Retention:** Retain evidence and calculation records at least two years, per §1005.13. Confirm any longer internal retention policy.
- **Security and privacy:** Role-based access, with PII masking for analysts who don't need it.
- **Performance:** The liability calculation returns in under 2 seconds for cases with up to 500 transactions.
