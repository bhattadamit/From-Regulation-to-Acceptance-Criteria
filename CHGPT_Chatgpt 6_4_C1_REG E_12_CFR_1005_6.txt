# **User stories: Regulation E consumer liability for unauthorized electronic fund transfers**

**Business domain:** US Consumer Banking – Disputes & Claims\
**Regulatory scope:** Regulation E, 12 CFR § 1005.6 – Liability of consumer for unauthorized transfers\
**Platform capability:** Unauthorized electronic fund transfer (EFT) dispute intake, investigation, liability determination, provisional credit, and claim resolution.

## **1. Business objective**

Implement automated, consistent, and auditable consumer liability determinations for unauthorized electronic fund transfers in accordance with Regulation E, 12 CFR § 1005.6.

The disputes platform must determine the consumer's potential liability based on:

- Whether the access device was accepted, issued, or otherwise made available to the consumer.
- Whether the consumer provided timely notice of loss or theft of an access device.
- Whether an unauthorized transfer occurred before or after the consumer notified the financial institution.
- Whether the consumer's delay in reporting resulted in additional unauthorized transfers.
- Whether the transfer occurred within the applicable 60-day statement notification period.
- Whether the institution can establish that the consumer's failure to report contributed to subsequent losses.
- Whether special rules apply to transactions involving access devices, account numbers, or recurring EFTs.

**Important regulatory distinction:** The platform must distinguish the consumer's liability calculation under § 1005.6 from the institution's investigation, error-resolution, and provisional-credit obligations under § 1005.11. These are related but separate regulatory requirements.

## **2. Regulatory rules and business interpretation**

The following rules should drive the liability decision engine.

| Scenario                                                                                                             | Consumer liability                                                                                                                            |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Notice received before unauthorized transfer                                                                         | $0                                                                                                                                            |
| Notice received within two business days after learning of loss/theft of an access device                            | Generally, lesser of $50 or qualifying unauthorized transfers before notice                                                                   |
| Notice received more than two business days after learning of loss/theft                                             | Lesser of $500 or the applicable $50 liability plus qualifying transfers occurring after the two-business-day period and before notice        |
| Unauthorized EFTs occurring more than 60 days after transmission of the statement showing the first unauthorized EFT | Potential additional liability for qualifying transfers occurring after the 60-day period and before notice, subject to regulatory conditions |
| Institution establishes that the consumer's delay did not result in additional losses                                | Additional liability based on that delay does not apply                                                                                       |
| Institution fails to provide required periodic statements                                                            | The 60-day statement-based liability provision cannot be applied in the ordinary manner                                                       |

**Key regulatory considerations:**

1. The $50 and $500 limits are maximum liability amounts, not automatic charges.
2. The two-business-day rule applies to notification of loss or theft of an access device after the consumer learns of the loss or theft.
3. The 60-day rule is measured from transmission of the periodic statement reflecting the first unauthorized EFT, not simply the transaction date.
4. The 60-day rule requires consideration of whether subsequent transfers would have been prevented by timely notification.
5. Consumer negligence, including sharing a PIN or password, must not independently increase liability beyond the limits permitted by Regulation E.
6. A consumer may not be held liable for unauthorized transfers occurring after the financial institution receives notification of loss or theft.

## **3. Functional user stories and Gherkin acceptance criteria**

### **Epic 1 – Capture and validate notification details**

**US-01: Capture consumer notification date and time**

As a disputes operations analyst,\
I want the platform to capture when the consumer first notified the bank of an unauthorized transfer or access device loss,\
So that the bank can calculate applicable Regulation E liability periods accurately.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Capture consumer notification details

  Scenario: Consumer reports a lost or stolen access device
    Given a consumer contacts the bank to report a lost or stolen access device
    When the notification is recorded
    Then the platform must capture the date and time of notification
    And capture the notification channel
    And capture the date the consumer first learned of the loss or theft
    And record the access device associated with the notification
    And retain the original notification timestamp for audit purposes

  Scenario: Consumer reports an unauthorized EFT
    Given a consumer reports an unauthorized electronic fund transfer
    When the dispute is created
    Then the platform must capture the date and time the bank received notice
    And identify the date of the first unauthorized EFT
    And associate the reported transactions with the affected account

  Scenario: Consumer reports through an authorized channel
    Given a consumer reports an unauthorized transfer through a bank-supported channel
    When the notification is received
    Then the platform must recognize the notification as received
    And calculate notification timing using the actual receipt date and time
    And not delay recognition solely because the case has not yet been assigned to an analyst
```

**Business rules:**

- Store the original notification timestamp separately from the case creation and case assignment timestamps.
- Support telephone, branch, online banking, mobile banking, and other approved notification channels.
- Record when the consumer first learned of the loss or theft, as this determines the two-business-day reporting period.
- Apply the bank's applicable business-day calendar when calculating the two-business-day period.
- Preserve evidence of the original notification, including call logs or digital notification records.

**US-02: Determine whether the reported transfer qualifies for consumer liability assessment**

As a disputes operations analyst,\
I want the platform to determine whether the transaction falls within the scope of the Regulation E access-device liability rules,\
So that consumer liability is not incorrectly applied to transactions outside the regulatory framework.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Validate eligibility for Regulation E liability assessment

  Scenario: Transfer involves an accepted access device
    Given an unauthorized EFT is reported
    And the bank issued or otherwise made an access device available to the consumer
    And the consumer accepted the access device
    When the platform assesses liability eligibility
    Then the platform must evaluate consumer liability under 12 CFR 1005.6

  Scenario: Access device was not accepted
    Given an unauthorized EFT is reported
    And the consumer did not accept the access device
    When the platform assesses liability eligibility
    Then the platform must not automatically assign consumer liability under Section 1005.6

  Scenario: Transaction involves only an account number
    Given an unauthorized EFT is reported
    And the transaction involved an account number without an access device
    When the platform assesses liability eligibility
    Then the platform must flag the transaction for the applicable regulatory treatment
    And must not automatically apply the access-device liability limits

  Scenario: Transaction is authorized
    Given a transaction is confirmed as authorized by the consumer
    When the platform evaluates liability
    Then the transaction must be excluded from unauthorized EFT liability calculations
    And the platform must record the authorization determination
```

**Business rules:**

- The access-device eligibility determination must be based on § 1005.6(a).
- The platform must distinguish an access device from an account number or other transaction identifier.
- The platform must not assume that possession of valid authentication credentials proves consumer authorization.
- Cases outside § 1005.6 must be routed to the appropriate regulatory or operational treatment.

### **Epic 2 – Calculate liability based on the two-business-day notification rule**

**US-03: Apply the $50 maximum liability rule for timely notification**

As a disputes operations analyst,\
I want the platform to apply the two-business-day notification rule,\
So that consumers who report loss or theft of an access device promptly receive the applicable liability protection.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Apply timely notification liability rules

  Scenario: Consumer reports loss within two business days
    Given the consumer learned of the loss or theft of an access device
    And the consumer notified the bank within two business days
    And unauthorized EFTs occurred before the bank received notification
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the platform must calculate the total qualifying unauthorized EFT amount
    And limit consumer liability to the lesser of $50 or that amount

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfer occurs after notification
    Given the consumer notified the bank of the lost or stolen access device
    When an unauthorized EFT occurs after the bank received notification
    Then consumer liability for that transfer must be $0

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfer occurs before notification
    Given the consumer reports a lost access device within two business days
    And an unauthorized EFT occurred before notification
    When the platform calculates liability
    Then the platform must include the qualifying transfer in the pre-notification liability calculation
    And apply the $50 maximum liability limit

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers total less than $50
    Given the consumer notified the bank within two business days
    And qualifying unauthorized EFTs total $35
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then consumer liability must be $35

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers total more than $50
    Given the consumer notified the bank within two business days
    And qualifying unauthorized EFTs total $250
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then consumer liability must not exceed $50
```

**Calculation logic:**

\\[ L = \min(\\$50,\ T) \\]

Where:

- \\(L\\) = consumer liability.
- \\(T\\) = qualifying unauthorized transfers before notification.

**US-04: Apply the $500 maximum liability rule for delayed notification**

As a disputes operations analyst,\
I want the platform to calculate liability when the consumer fails to notify the bank within two business days of learning about loss or theft,\
So that the bank applies the appropriate statutory liability limit.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Apply delayed notification liability rules

  Scenario: Consumer reports loss after two business days
    Given the consumer learned of the loss or theft of an access device
    And the consumer notified the bank more than two business days later
    And qualifying unauthorized EFTs occurred before notification
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then it must identify qualifying transfers occurring before the end of the two-business-day period
    And calculate the applicable initial liability
    And identify qualifying transfers occurring after that period and before notification
    And determine whether the bank can establish that timely notification would have prevented those additional transfers
    And calculate liability in accordance with Section 1005.6(b)(2)

  Scenario: Additional transfers would have been prevented
    Given the consumer reported loss after two business days
    And the bank establishes that timely notification would have prevented subsequent unauthorized EFTs
    When the platform calculates liability
    Then liability must equal the lesser of $500 or
      the sum of the applicable initial liability and qualifying subsequent transfers

  Scenario: Additional transfers would not have been prevented
    Given the consumer reported loss after two business days
    And the bank cannot establish that timely notification would have prevented additional unauthorized EFTs
    When the platform calculates liability
    Then the platform must exclude those additional transfers from the delayed-notification liability calculation

  Scenario: Total calculated liability exceeds $500
    Given the consumer reported loss after two business days
    And the qualifying liability calculation exceeds $500
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then consumer liability under Section 1005.6(b)(2) must not exceed $500

  Scenario: Total calculated liability is less than $500
    Given the consumer reported loss after two business days
    And the qualifying liability calculation is $320
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then consumer liability must equal $320
```

**Calculation logic:**

\\[ L = \min(\\$500,\ A+B) \\]

Where:

- \\(A\\) = lesser of $50 or qualifying unauthorized transfers occurring before expiration of the two-business-day period.
- \\(B\\) = qualifying unauthorized transfers occurring after expiration of that period and before notification, where the bank establishes that timely notification would have prevented them.

**Important:** The $500 limit applies to this liability calculation. It is not a universal cap on every possible liability scenario under § 1005.6.

### **Epic 3 – Apply the 60-day periodic statement notification rule**

**US-05: Identify unauthorized EFTs not reported within 60 days**

As a disputes operations analyst,\
I want the platform to evaluate whether the consumer reported unauthorized EFTs within 60 days after transmission of the periodic statement,\
So that the bank can identify any additional liability permitted by Regulation E.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Apply 60-day periodic statement notification rule

  Scenario: Consumer reports within 60 days of statement transmission
    Given a periodic statement reflects an unauthorized EFT
    And the consumer notifies the bank within 60 days after statement transmission
    When the platform assesses liability
    Then the platform must not apply additional liability under Section 1005.6(b)(3)
    solely because of the 60-day statement notification rule

  Scenario: Consumer reports after 60 days
    Given a periodic statement reflects an unauthorized EFT
    And more than 60 days have elapsed since statement transmission
    And the consumer has not notified the bank
    When the platform assesses liability
    Then the platform must identify subsequent unauthorized EFTs occurring after expiration of the 60-day period
    And identify whether the bank can establish that timely notification would have prevented those transfers

  Scenario: Bank establishes that timely notification would have prevented subsequent transfers
    Given the consumer failed to notify the bank within 60 days
    And the bank establishes that timely notification would have prevented subsequent unauthorized EFTs
    When the platform calculates liability
    Then it must calculate additional liability for qualifying transfers occurring after expiration of the 60-day period and before notification
    And must not apply the $500 maximum from Section 1005.6(b)(2) as a cap on this additional liability

  Scenario: Bank cannot establish that timely notification would have prevented subsequent transfers
    Given the consumer failed to notify the bank within 60 days
    And the bank cannot establish that timely notification would have prevented subsequent unauthorized EFTs
    When the platform calculates liability
    Then the platform must not impose additional liability under Section 1005.6(b)(3)

  Scenario: No periodic statement was provided
    Given the consumer's account was not provided with a required periodic statement
    When the platform assesses 60-day notification liability
    Then the platform must not apply the 60-day liability rule without establishing that the regulatory requirements have been met
    And route the case for appropriate regulatory review
```

**Business rules:**

- Calculate 60 days from the date the statement was transmitted, not the statement closing date.
- Identify the first periodic statement reflecting the unauthorized EFT.
- Calculate the applicable 60-day period for each relevant statement and transaction.
- Retain evidence supporting statement transmission.
- Do not automatically apply additional liability merely because the consumer reported after 60 days.
- Require an affirmative, documented bank determination that timely notification would have prevented the subsequent transfers.

### **Epic 4 – Prevent impermissible liability determinations**

**US-06: Prevent consumer negligence from independently increasing liability**

As a compliance officer,\
I want the platform to prevent consumer negligence from being used as an independent basis for increasing liability,\
So that liability decisions comply with Regulation E.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Prevent impermissible liability determinations

  Scenario: Consumer shared PIN or password
    Given the consumer shared a PIN or password with another person
    And the consumer disputes an unauthorized EFT
    When the platform evaluates liability
    Then it must not automatically assign liability based solely on the sharing of credentials
    And must apply the applicable Regulation E liability requirements

  Scenario: Consumer failed to safeguard access device
    Given the consumer failed to safeguard an access device
    When the platform evaluates liability
    Then the platform must not increase consumer liability solely because of negligence
    And must apply the applicable statutory liability limits

  Scenario: Consumer disputes an unauthorized EFT
    Given an unauthorized EFT is reported
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the calculation must be based on the applicable statutory criteria
    And not on an internal risk score or fraud classification alone

  Scenario: Liability determination lacks supporting evidence
    Given the bank cannot substantiate the facts required for a higher liability determination
    When the platform evaluates consumer liability
    Then the platform must not assign the higher liability amount
    And must flag the case for review
```

### **Epic 5 – Implement evidence-based liability determinations**

**US-07: Require supporting evidence for additional liability**

As a disputes operations analyst,\
I want the platform to capture evidence supporting additional consumer liability,\
So that the bank can demonstrate compliance with its burden of proof under Regulation E.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Evidence-based liability assessment

  Scenario: Additional liability is proposed under the two-day rule
    Given the consumer reported loss more than two business days after learning of it
    When the platform proposes liability for transfers occurring after the two-day period
    Then the analyst must document why timely notification would have prevented those transfers
    And attach supporting evidence
    And record the decision and decision date

  Scenario: Additional liability is proposed under the 60-day rule
    Given the consumer did not notify the bank within 60 days after statement transmission
    When the platform proposes additional liability
    Then the analyst must document the applicable statement transmission date
    And identify the transfers occurring after the 60-day period
    And document why timely notification would have prevented those transfers
    And attach supporting evidence

  Scenario: Evidence is missing
    Given additional consumer liability is proposed
    And the required supporting evidence is missing
    When the analyst attempts to finalize the liability determination
    Then the platform must prevent unsupported additional liability from being finalized
    And display the missing evidence requirements

  Scenario: Liability decision is overridden
    Given the system has calculated consumer liability
    When an authorized analyst overrides the calculation
    Then the platform must require an override reason
    And record the original calculation
    And record the revised calculation
    And capture the analyst identity and timestamp
```

### **Epic 6 – Recognize effective consumer notification**

**US-08: Recognize when consumer notification becomes effective**

As a consumer banking compliance officer,\
I want the platform to recognize the effective date of consumer notification,\
So that consumers are not held liable for transfers occurring after they have taken the required steps to notify the bank.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Effective consumer notification

  Scenario: Consumer takes reasonable steps to notify the bank
    Given the consumer takes steps reasonably necessary to provide notice
    When the bank receives the notification through an accepted channel
    Then the platform must record the effective notification date
    And use that date when determining consumer liability

  Scenario: Telephone notification requires written confirmation
    Given the bank requires written confirmation of a telephone notification
    When the consumer provides telephone notification
    Then the platform must record the original telephone notification date
    And identify the written confirmation requirement
    And track the applicable 10-business-day confirmation period
    And retain evidence that the consumer was informed of the requirement

  Scenario: Written confirmation is received within the required period
    Given the consumer provided telephone notification
    And written confirmation was required and received within 10 business days
    When the liability calculation is performed
    Then the original telephone notification date must be used

  Scenario: Written confirmation is not received
    Given the consumer provided telephone notification
    And the bank required written confirmation
    And written confirmation was not received within the applicable period
    When the platform assesses the case
    Then it must flag the notification for regulatory review
    And must not automatically replace the original notification date without evaluating the applicable regulatory requirements
```

**Business rules:**

- Apply § 1005.6(d) when determining effective notification.
- The system must distinguish the date of telephone notification from the date written confirmation is received.
- The platform must not automatically treat the written confirmation date as the effective notification date.
- Notification-channel procedures must be consistent with applicable regulatory requirements.

As an internal auditor,\

**Acceptance criteria**

Feature: Regulation E liability audit trail

  Scenario: Liability calculation is completed
    Given a liability calculation is completed
    When the platform saves the determination
    Then it must record the calculation date and time
    And record the applicable regulatory rule
    And record all inputs used in the calculation
    And record the resulting liability amount

  Scenario: Consumer notification information changes
    Given a liability determination has already been completed
    When the consumer notification date or discovery date is updated

    And recalculate liability using the updated information

### **Epic 8 – Integrate liability assessment with dispute resolution**

**US-11: Separate liability determination from provisional credit and error resolution**

As a disputes operations manager,\
I want the platform to maintain separate workflows for consumer liability assessment and Regulation E error resolution,\
So that liability decisions do not incorrectly delay or replace required error-resolution activities.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Separate liability assessment and error resolution

  Scenario: Unauthorized EFT dispute is opened
    Given a consumer reports an unauthorized EFT
    When the dispute is created
    Then the platform must initiate the applicable Regulation E error-resolution workflow
    And initiate the consumer liability assessment separately
    And maintain independent statuses for both workflows

  Scenario: Liability assessment is pending
    Given the unauthorized EFT investigation is in progress
    And the consumer liability determination is pending
    When the applicable provisional credit deadline approaches
    Then the platform must continue tracking the Regulation E error-resolution deadlines
    And must not delay required provisional credit solely because liability assessment is incomplete

  Scenario: Consumer liability is determined
    Given the liability assessment is complete
    When the platform determines the consumer liability amount
    Then it must make the determination available to the error-resolution workflow
    And retain the underlying liability calculation and supporting evidence

  Scenario: Error resolution is completed
    Given the investigation has been completed
    When the dispute is resolved
    Then the platform must retain the final error-resolution outcome
    And retain the consumer liability determination separately
    And ensure the consumer-facing explanation accurately reflects both outcomes
```

**Business rules:**

- Regulation E § 1005.6 governs consumer liability.
- Regulation E § 1005.11 governs error resolution.
- A liability calculation must not be treated as a substitute for investigating the alleged error.
- Provisional credit eligibility and deadlines must be assessed independently under § 1005.11.
- The platform must not use an unresolved liability determination as a reason to miss an applicable regulatory deadline.

## **5. Recommended liability decision engine**

The following decision flow can be used as the basis for technical design.

**Start: Unauthorized EFT reported**

**Does § 1005.6 access-device liability apply?**

**No**

Route to applicable regulatory treatment

**Yes**

Continue liability assessment

**Was notification received within two business days of learning of loss/theft?**

**Yes**

Apply lesser of $50 or qualifying pre-notification transfers.

**No**

Evaluate qualifying transfers and preventability. Apply $500 maximum under § 1005.6(b)(2).

**Evaluate 60-day periodic statement notification rule**

Determine whether additional qualifying transfers occurred after the 60-day period and whether timely notification would have prevented them.

**Final liability determination**

Generate calculation, evidence record, decision rationale, and audit history.

*Design note:* The two-day and 60-day provisions must be evaluated together where applicable. The decision engine must not treat them as mutually exclusive alternatives. Additional liability under the 60-day rule must be calculated separately from the $500 limitation.

### **9. Senior business analyst recommendations**

Before moving these stories into development, I would prioritize five decisions with Compliance, Legal, Operations, and Technology:

1. **Liability versus fraud determination:** Establish that a fraud team's finding does not automatically establish consumer authorization or liability.
2. **Preventability evidence:** Define acceptable evidence and approval authority for applying additional liability under the two-day and 60-day rules.
3. **Statement transmission evidence:** Confirm the source of truth for statement generation and transmission dates, including returned or undelivered statements.
4. **Notification effective date:** Define how telephone, digital, branch, and third-party notifications are recognized and timestamped.
5. **Regulatory exception handling:** Define mandatory manual-review scenarios where the bank cannot substantiate the information needed for a liability determination.

| P1       | US-11        | Error-resolution integration                                           |
| P2       | US-06        | Prevent impermissible liability determinations and strengthen controls |

**Final business recommendation:** Treat the liability engine as a configurable, evidence-driven rules service rather than embedding liability calculations directly into individual dispute workflows. This will make regulatory changes, historical recalculations, audit testing, and consistent treatment across debit card, ATM, ACH, and other covered EFT disputes substantially easier to manage.

The stories above are proposed business requirements, not a substitute for final Legal and Compliance approval of the bank's liability policy.
