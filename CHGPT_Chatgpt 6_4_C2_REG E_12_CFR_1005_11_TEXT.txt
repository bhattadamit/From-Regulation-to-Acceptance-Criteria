Regulation E Error Resolution — User Stories and Gherkin Acceptance Criteria
Regulatory scope: 12 CFR § 1005.11, Procedures for resolving errors
Platform: Card and ACH Disputes Platform
Role: Senior Business Analyst
Objective: Implement the regulatory requirements for receiving, classifying, investigating, resolving, communicating, and tracking consumer-reported electronic fund transfer (EFT) errors.
1. Scope and implementation approach
The following user stories are derived exclusively from the regulatory text provided. They cover the requirements in paragraphs (a) through (e) of § 1005.11.

The stories are organized into these functional capabilities:
Epic	Functional capability
E1	Error classification and inquiry handling
E2	Notice of error intake and eligibility
E3	Oral notice and written confirmation
E4	Investigation deadlines and extensions
E5	Provisional credit management
E6	Investigation outcome and error correction
E7	Consumer notifications and supporting documentation
E8	Provisional credit reversal and consumer protections
E9	Reassertion of previously resolved errors

Important implementation distinction: The regulation establishes requirements and permitted exceptions. The platform should distinguish mandatory obligations ("shall") from optional institutional policies ("may"). Optional capabilities should be configurable rather than treated as universal regulatory mandates.

Epic E1 — Error classification and inquiry handling
US-001: Classify consumer-reported errors
Regulatory reference: § 1005.11(a)(1)

User story

As a dispute operations analyst, I want the platform to classify consumer inquiries against the regulatory definition of an error, so that all covered EFT errors are routed through the appropriate error resolution process.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Classification of Regulation E errors

  Scenario Outline: Identify a covered error type
    Given a consumer submits an inquiry concerning an electronic fund transfer
    When the inquiry is classified
    Then the platform shall identify it as a Regulation E error
    And assign the corresponding error category "<error_type>"

    Examples:
      | error_type |
      | Unauthorized electronic fund transfer |
      | Incorrect electronic fund transfer |
      | Omitted electronic fund transfer from periodic statement |
      | Computational or bookkeeping error |
      | Incorrect amount received from electronic terminal |
      | EFT not identified in accordance with applicable requirements |
      | Request for documentation or clarification concerning an EFT |

  Scenario: Consumer requests information to determine whether an error exists
    Given a consumer requests additional information or clarification concerning an EFT
    When the inquiry is classified
    Then the platform shall identify the inquiry as a covered error

  Scenario: Consumer requests required EFT documentation
    Given a consumer requests documentation required under section 1005.9 or 1005.10(a)
    When the inquiry is classified
    Then the platform shall identify the inquiry as a covered error


US-002: Exclude inquiries that do not constitute errors
Regulatory reference: § 1005.11(a)(2)

User story

As a dispute operations analyst, I want the platform to distinguish routine account inquiries from regulatory errors, so that non-covered inquiries are not incorrectly processed as Regulation E disputes.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Exclusion of non-covered inquiries

  Scenario Outline: Identify an inquiry excluded from the definition of error
    Given a consumer submits an inquiry
    When the inquiry is classified as "<inquiry_type>"
    Then the platform shall not classify it as a Regulation E error

    Examples:
      | inquiry_type |
      | Routine account balance inquiry |
      | Request for tax or other recordkeeping information |
      | Request for duplicate documentation |



Epic E2 — Notice of error intake and eligibility
US-003: Capture oral and written notices of error
Regulatory reference: § 1005.11(b)(1)

User story

As a consumer, I want to submit a notice of error orally or in writing, so that the financial institution can initiate the required error resolution process.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Notice of error intake

  Scenario Outline: Accept a notice of error
    Given a consumer submits a notice of error through "<channel>"
    When the institution receives the notice
    Then the platform shall record the notice
    And capture the date and time of receipt
    And identify the submission channel

    Examples:
      | channel |
      | Oral |
      | Written |

  Scenario: Identify the consumer
    Given a notice of error has been received
    When the consumer provides their identifying information
    Then the platform shall capture the consumer's name
    And capture the account number

  Scenario: Capture alleged error details
    Given a consumer submits a notice of error
    When the notice concerns an alleged EFT error
    Then the platform shall capture why the consumer believes an error exists
    And capture the error type to the extent possible
    And capture the transaction date to the extent possible
    And capture the alleged error amount to the extent possible

  Scenario: Exempt documentation and clarification requests from error detail requirements
    Given a consumer submits a notice based on a request described under section 1005.11(a)(1)(vii)
    When the platform validates the notice contents
    Then the platform shall not require the consumer to provide the error type, date, and amount


US-004: Validate the 60-calendar-day notification deadline
Regulatory reference: § 1005.11(b)(1)(i)

User story

As a dispute operations analyst, I want the platform to calculate whether a notice of error was received within the regulatory notification period, so that the institution can determine whether the notice meets the timing requirement.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Initial notice timeliness

  Scenario: Notice received within 60 days
    Given a periodic statement or passbook documentation has been sent or provided
    And the statement or documentation first reflects the alleged error
    When the institution receives the notice of error no later than 60 days after that date
    Then the platform shall identify the notice as timely

  Scenario: Notice received after 60 days
    Given a periodic statement or passbook documentation has been sent or provided
    And the statement or documentation first reflects the alleged error
    When the institution receives the notice of error more than 60 days after that date
    Then the platform shall identify that the notice falls outside the specified 60-day period

  Scenario: Calculate the notification period using the first statement reflecting the error
    Given an alleged error appears on multiple periodic statements
    When the platform calculates the initial notice deadline
    Then it shall use the date the institution sent the periodic statement or provided the passbook documentation on which the alleged error is first reflected


US-005: Apply the alternative notification deadline for documentation requests
Regulatory reference: § 1005.11(b)(3)

User story

As a dispute operations analyst, I want the platform to calculate the notification deadline from the date requested information is sent when the consumer's notice is based on previously requested documentation or clarification, so that the correct regulatory timeframe is applied.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Alternative notice timeliness

  Scenario: Notice received within 60 days after requested information is sent
    Given a consumer previously requested documentation or clarification under section 1005.11(a)(1)(vii)
    And the institution sent the requested information
    When the consumer submits a notice of error no later than 60 days after the information was sent
    Then the platform shall identify the notice as timely

  Scenario: Notice received more than 60 days after requested information is sent
    Given a consumer previously requested documentation or clarification
    And the institution sent the requested information
    When the consumer submits a notice of error more than 60 days after the information was sent
    Then the platform shall identify that the notice falls outside the alternative 60-day period



Epic E3 — Oral notice and written confirmation
US-006: Support written confirmation requirements for oral notices
Regulatory reference: § 1005.11(b)(2)

User story

As a dispute operations analyst, I want the platform to support an institutional requirement for written confirmation following oral notice, so that the consumer is informed of the requirement and the institution can track receipt within the applicable period.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Written confirmation of oral notice

  Scenario: Institution requires written confirmation
    Given a consumer provides an oral notice of error
    And the institution requires written confirmation
    When the oral notification is recorded
    Then the platform shall record that written confirmation is required
    And provide notice of the written confirmation requirement
    And provide the address where the written confirmation must be sent

  Scenario: Track the written confirmation deadline
    Given written confirmation is required following an oral notice
    When the oral notice is received
    Then the platform shall establish a deadline of 10 business days from receipt

  Scenario: Written confirmation received within 10 business days
    Given written confirmation is required
    When the institution receives the written confirmation within 10 business days of the oral notice
    Then the platform shall record the confirmation as received

  Scenario: Written confirmation not received within 10 business days
    Given written confirmation is required
    And the institution has not received written confirmation within 10 business days of the oral notice
    When the platform evaluates provisional credit eligibility
    Then the platform shall identify the account as eligible for the written-confirmation exception to provisional credit



Epic E4 — Investigation deadlines and extensions
US-007: Establish the standard 10-business-day investigation deadline
Regulatory reference: § 1005.11(c)(1)

User story

As a dispute operations analyst, I want the platform to calculate the standard investigation deadline, so that the institution determines whether an error occurred within the required timeframe.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Standard investigation timeframe

  Scenario: Establish standard investigation deadline
    Given the institution receives a notice of error
    When the investigation is initiated
    Then the platform shall establish an investigation deadline of 10 business days after receipt

  Scenario: Determine an error within 10 business days
    Given a notice of error has been received
    When the institution determines whether an error occurred within 10 business days
    Then the platform shall record the investigation determination date

  Scenario: Report results within three business days
    Given the institution completes its investigation
    When the investigation results are recorded
    Then the platform shall establish a consumer reporting deadline of three business days after investigation completion

  Scenario: Correct an identified error within one business day
    Given the institution determines that an error occurred
    When the determination is recorded
    Then the platform shall establish an error correction deadline of one business day after the determination


US-008: Support extended investigations up to 45 days
Regulatory reference: § 1005.11(c)(2)

User story

As a dispute operations analyst, I want the platform to support an extended investigation timeframe when the institution cannot complete an investigation within 10 business days, so that the investigation can be completed within the applicable regulatory deadline while satisfying provisional credit and notification requirements.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Extended investigation timeframe

  Scenario: Extend investigation when unable to complete within 10 business days
    Given the institution receives a notice of error
    And the institution is unable to complete its investigation within 10 business days
    When the institution elects to use the extended investigation period
    Then the platform shall establish an investigation deadline of 45 days from receipt
    And require applicable provisional credit processing within 10 business days

  Scenario: Complete extended investigation within 45 days
    Given the institution has elected the extended investigation period
    When the institution determines whether an error occurred within 45 days of receiving the notice
    Then the platform shall record the investigation determination

  Scenario: Report extended investigation results
    Given an extended investigation has been completed
    When the institution records the investigation results
    Then the platform shall establish a consumer reporting deadline of three business days after completion

  Scenario: Correct an error following an extended investigation
    Given an extended investigation determines that an error occurred
    When the institution records the determination
    Then the platform shall establish a correction deadline of one business day after the determination


US-009: Apply the 20-business-day initial investigation period
Regulatory reference: § 1005.11(c)(3)(i)

User story

As a dispute operations analyst, I want the platform to identify EFT errors occurring within 30 days after the first deposit to an account, so that the applicable 20-business-day period is used instead of 10 business days.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: New account investigation extension

  Scenario: Apply 20-business-day investigation period
    Given the notice of error involves an EFT to or from an account
    And the EFT occurred within 30 days after the first deposit to the account was made
    When the platform calculates the applicable investigation period
    Then it shall establish a 20-business-day period in place of 10 business days

  Scenario: Do not apply the new account extension
    Given the notice of error involves an EFT
    And the EFT did not occur within 30 days after the first deposit to the account was made
    When the platform calculates the applicable investigation period
    Then it shall not apply the 20-business-day extension under section 1005.11(c)(3)(i)


US-010: Apply the 90-day extended investigation period
Regulatory reference: § 1005.11(c)(3)(ii)

User story

As a dispute operations analyst, I want the platform to identify transactions eligible for the 90-day extended investigation period, so that the institution applies the correct maximum investigation timeframe.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Special extended investigation period

  Scenario Outline: Apply 90-day investigation period
    Given the institution receives a notice of error
    And the EFT meets the condition "<condition>"
    And the institution is unable to complete the investigation within the applicable initial period
    When the institution elects to use the extended investigation period
    Then the platform shall establish a maximum investigation period of 90 days from receipt

    Examples:
      | condition |
      | EFT was not initiated within a state |
      | EFT resulted from a point-of-sale debit card transaction |
      | EFT occurred within 30 days after the first deposit to the account was made |

  Scenario: Apply standard extended period when no special condition exists
    Given the institution receives a notice of error
    And none of the conditions for the 90-day period applies
    When the institution elects an extended investigation
    Then the platform shall establish a maximum investigation period of 45 days from receipt



Epic E5 — Provisional credit management
US-011: Provisionally credit the consumer's account
Regulatory reference: § 1005.11(c)(2)(i)

User story

As a dispute operations analyst, I want the platform to calculate and process provisional credit for eligible error claims, so that the consumer receives access to the alleged error amount while the extended investigation is pending.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Provisional credit processing

  Scenario: Issue provisional credit for an extended investigation
    Given the institution is unable to complete an investigation within the applicable initial period
    And the institution elects to use the extended investigation period
    And no provisional credit exception applies
    When the institution processes provisional credit
    Then the platform shall credit the consumer's account within the applicable 10 or 20 business days
    And the credit amount shall equal the alleged error amount
    And include interest where applicable

  Scenario: Allow withholding up to $50 for qualifying unauthorized EFTs
    Given the alleged error concerns an unauthorized electronic fund transfer
    And the institution has a reasonable basis for believing that an unauthorized EFT occurred
    And the institution has satisfied the requirements of section 1005.6(a)
    When the institution calculates provisional credit
    Then the platform may permit withholding an amount not exceeding $50
    And shall not permit withholding more than $50 under this provision

  Scenario: Do not require provisional credit when written confirmation is not received
    Given the institution requires written confirmation following oral notice
    And written confirmation is not received within 10 business days of the oral notice
    When the platform evaluates provisional credit eligibility
    Then the platform shall identify the written-confirmation exception

  Scenario: Do not require provisional credit for Regulation T accounts
    Given the alleged error involves an account subject to Regulation T
    When the platform evaluates provisional credit eligibility
    Then the platform shall identify the Regulation T exception


US-012: Notify the consumer of provisional credit
Regulatory reference: § 1005.11(c)(2)(ii)

User story

As a consumer, I want to be informed of provisional credit applied to my account, including the credit amount and date, so that I understand the funds available during the investigation.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Provisional credit notification

  Scenario: Notify consumer within two business days
    Given provisional credit has been applied to the consumer's account
    When the credit is posted
    Then the platform shall establish a notification deadline of two business days after crediting

  Scenario: Include credit amount and date
    Given provisional credit has been applied
    When the consumer notification is generated
    Then the notification shall include the provisional credit amount
    And the date of provisional crediting

  Scenario: Provide full use of provisionally credited funds
    Given provisional credit has been applied
    When the investigation remains pending
    Then the platform shall provide the consumer full use of the provisionally credited funds

US-014: Record no-error and different-error determinations
Regulatory reference: § 1005.11(d)

User story

As a dispute operations analyst, I want the platform to record when an investigation identifies no error or an error different in manner or amount from the consumer's allegation, so that the appropriate consumer explanation and documentation rights are communicated.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Investigation outcome classification

  Scenario: Determine no error occurred
    Given the institution has completed its investigation
    When the institution determines that no error occurred
    Then the platform shall record the outcome as no error

  Scenario: Determine a different error occurred
    Given the institution has completed its investigation
    When the institution determines that an error occurred in a different manner or amount
    Then the platform shall record the actual error determination
    And identify the difference from the consumer's allegation

  Scenario: Require additional written explanation
    Given the institution determines that no error occurred
    Or the institution determines that an error occurred differently from the consumer's allegation
    When the investigation results are prepared
    Then the report shall include a written explanation of the institution's findings
    And note the consumer's right to request the documents relied upon in making the determination

Epic E8 — Provisional credit reversal and consumer protections
US-017: Notify consumers when provisional credit is debited
Regulatory reference: § 1005.11(d)(2)

User story

As a consumer, I want to receive notification when provisional credit is debited from my account, so that I understand the amount removed and the protections available for qualifying payments after the debit.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Provisional credit debit notification

  Scenario: Notify consumer of provisional credit debit
    Given provisional credit has been applied to the consumer's account
    And the institution subsequently debits the provisionally credited amount
    When the debit occurs
    Then the platform shall notify the consumer of the debit date
    And notify the consumer of the debit amount

  Scenario: Notify consumer of five-business-day payment protections
    Given provisional credit has been debited
    When the debit notification is generated
    Then the notification shall inform the consumer that the institution will honor qualifying checks, drafts, or similar instruments payable to third parties
    And inform the consumer that the institution will honor qualifying preauthorized transfers
    And specify that these protections apply for five business days after notification
    And inform the consumer that the institution will not charge the consumer as a result of an overdraft for these protected items

  Scenario: Honor qualifying payment items during protection period
    Given the institution has notified the consumer of a provisional credit debit
    And five business days have not elapsed after notification
    When a check, draft, similar instrument payable to a third party, or preauthorized transfer is presented
    Then the institution shall honor the item if it would have been paid had the provisionally credited funds not been debited

  Scenario: Do not extend protection beyond qualifying items
    Given provisional credit has been debited
    When a payment item is presented during the five-business-day protection period
    Then the institution shall only be required to honor the item if it would have been paid had the provisionally credited funds not been debited



Epic E9 — Reassertion of previously resolved errors
US-018: Handle reassertion of previously resolved errors
Regulatory reference: § 1005.11(e)

User story

As a dispute operations analyst, I want the platform to identify when a consumer reasserts an error that has already been fully resolved under Regulation E, so that the institution can distinguish repeat claims from claims that require further action.

Acceptance criteria
Feature: Reassertion of previously resolved errors

  Scenario: Identify reassertion of a previously resolved error
    Given the institution has fully complied with the error resolution requirements for an alleged error
    When the consumer reasserts the same error
    Then the platform shall identify the claim as a reassertion of a previously resolved error

  Scenario: No further responsibility for previously resolved error
    Given the institution has fully complied with the error resolution requirements
    And the consumer reasserts the same error
    And the error was not asserted following receipt of information provided under section 1005.11(a)(1)(vii)
    When the platform evaluates the reasserted claim
    Then the platform shall identify that no further responsibilities arise under section 1005.11

  Scenario: Apply exception for errors asserted following requested information
    Given the institution previously provided information requested under section 1005.11(a)(1)(vii)
    When the consumer asserts an error following receipt of that information
    Then the platform shall not automatically apply the general reassertion exception

4. Recommended case lifecycle
The following lifecycle translates the regulatory requirements into a platform workflow.
Regulation E error resolution workflow
1. Notice received
   
   Capture oral or written notice, consumer identity, account number, alleged error, and receipt date.
2. Error classification and timeliness
   
   Determine whether the inquiry is covered and assess the applicable 60-day notice period.
3. Written confirmation, if required
   
   Notify the consumer of the requirement and confirmation address; track the 10-business-day period.
4. Investigation timeframe selection
   
   Establish the applicable 10/20-business-day initial period and 45/90-day extended period, as appropriate.
5. Investigation and provisional credit
   
   If using the extended period, assess provisional credit requirements and issue required notifications.
6. Investigation determination
   
   Record whether an error occurred, no error occurred, or a different error occurred.
7. Correction and consumer reporting
   
   Correct identified errors within one business day and report investigation results within three business days.
8. Final credit treatment
   
   Make applicable provisional credit final or debit provisional credit, including required notifications and consumer protections.
9. Reassertion handling
   
   Evaluate repeat claims against the prior resolution and the information-request exception.

5. Business analysis considerations and regulatory interpretation boundaries
The following are important implementation considerations. They identify areas where the supplied regulatory text does not provide enough detail to prescribe a specific technical solution.
Area	Business analysis consideration
Card and ACH coverage	The platform should classify disputes based on whether they concern an EFT and whether they meet the supplied definition of error.
Calendar versus business days	The 60-day notice period and 45/90-day investigation periods are expressed in days; other specified deadlines are expressed in business days.
Deadline configuration	The regulatory text does not define the institution's business-day calendar or holiday treatment. These require separate operational/legal confirmation.
Initial versus extended period	The 45/90-day period is an alternative when the institution cannot complete the investigation within the applicable initial period.
Provisional credit	Provisional credit is tied to use of the extended investigation period, subject to the stated exceptions.
$50 withholding	The withholding provision is conditional, not a general authorization to deduct $50 from every provisional credit.
Different error	The platform must support findings that differ in both manner and amount from the consumer's allegation.
Documentation requests	The obligation to provide copies is triggered upon consumer request and must be fulfilled promptly.
Reassertions	The exception concerning an error asserted following receipt of requested information must be distinguished from ordinary repeat claims.

Important regulatory boundary: The supplied text references other provisions, including §§ 1005.6(a), 1005.9, 1005.10(a), and 1005.14, but does not provide their contents. These user stories do not independently define requirements from those sections.

The supplied text also does not establish specific requirements for fraud investigation techniques, evidence standards, transaction-network integrations, chargeback processing, consumer authentication, or internal approval hierarchies. Those should not be presented as requirements derived from § 1005.11.

Priority is a proposed delivery sequencing recommendation, not a regulatory classification.
7. Recommended definition of done
A story should be considered complete when:
- The applicable regulatory scenario is supported.
- The relevant date, amount, account, and case information is captured.
- The applicable deadline is calculated and retained.
- Required consumer communications can be generated and their delivery recorded.
- Required financial adjustments can be processed and recorded.
- Applicable exceptions are supported.
- The resulting case status and investigation determination are retained.
- Positive, negative, boundary-date, and exception scenarios pass their Gherkin tests.
Final recommendation: Build the Regulation E rules as a centralized, configurable error-resolution capability shared by the card and ACH dispute workflows, rather than implementing separate deadline and provisional-credit logic in each product. This supports consistent application of the same regulatory requirements across both channels while preserving the distinct transaction characteristics needed to determine the applicable investigation period.
