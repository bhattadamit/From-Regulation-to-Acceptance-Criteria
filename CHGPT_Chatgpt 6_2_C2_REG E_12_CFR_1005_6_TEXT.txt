# **Regulation E – Consumer Liability for Unauthorized Transfers**

**Business Requirements and User Stories | 12 CFR 1005.6**

## **1. Objective**

Implement consumer liability determination within the bank's disputes platform in accordance with 12 CFR 1005.6.

The platform must calculate the consumer's maximum permissible liability for unauthorized electronic fund transfers (EFTs), taking into account:

- Whether required Regulation E disclosures were provided.
- Whether an access device was involved and met the acceptance and identification requirements.
- When the consumer learned of the loss or theft.
- When the consumer notified the financial institution.
- Unauthorized transfer dates and amounts.
- Periodic statement transmission dates and reporting deadlines.
- Extenuating circumstances.
- Applicable state law or contractual provisions imposing lower liability.

**Scope limitation:** The acceptance criteria below are based exclusively on the regulatory text supplied in the request. They do not introduce additional regulatory interpretations, operational requirements, or assumptions about other Regulation E provisions.

## **2. Epic: Determine Consumer Liability for Unauthorized Transfers**

**Epic description**

As a disputes platform, I need to determine the maximum consumer liability for unauthorized EFTs based on the conditions and limitations in 12 CFR 1005.6, so that consumer liability is assessed within the limits established by the regulation.

## **3. User stories and Gherkin acceptance criteria**

### **US-01: Validate conditions for consumer liability**

**User story**

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to validate the regulatory conditions for consumer liability, so that liability is not assigned unless the financial institution has satisfied the required disclosure and access device conditions.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.6(a)

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Validate conditions for consumer liability

  Scenario: Required disclosures have been provided
    Given an unauthorized electronic fund transfer involves a consumer's account
    And the financial institution provided the disclosures required by
      Section 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    When the platform evaluates consumer liability
    Then the platform may proceed to evaluate the applicable
      liability limitations

  Scenario: Required disclosures have not been provided
    Given an unauthorized electronic fund transfer involves a consumer's account
    And the financial institution did not provide one or more of the
      disclosures required by Section 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    When the platform evaluates consumer liability
    Then the consumer must not be held liable under Section 1005.6

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfer involves an access device
    Given an unauthorized electronic fund transfer involves an access device
    And the access device is an accepted access device
    And the financial institution provided a means to identify
      the consumer to whom the access device was issued
    When the platform evaluates consumer liability
    Then the platform may proceed to evaluate the applicable
      liability limitations

  Scenario: Access device is not an accepted access device
    Given an unauthorized electronic fund transfer involves an access device
    And the access device is not an accepted access device
    When the platform evaluates consumer liability
    Then the consumer must not be held liable under Section 1005.6

  Scenario: No means of identifying the consumer
    Given an unauthorized electronic fund transfer involves an access device
    And the access device is an accepted access device
    And the financial institution did not provide a means to identify
      the consumer to whom it was issued
    When the platform evaluates consumer liability
    Then the consumer must not be held liable under Section 1005.6
```

### **US-02: Calculate liability when timely notice is provided**

**User story**

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to calculate consumer liability when the consumer reports the loss or theft of an access device within two business days of learning about it, so that liability does not exceed the regulatory limit.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.6(b)(1)

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Calculate liability when timely notice is provided

  Scenario: Consumer provides notice within two business days
    Given the consumer learned of the loss or theft of an access device
    And the consumer notified the financial institution within
      two business days after learning of the loss or theft
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the maximum consumer liability must not exceed
      the lesser of $50 or the amount of unauthorized transfers
      occurring before notice to the financial institution

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers before notice are less than $50
    Given the consumer notified the financial institution within
      two business days after learning of the loss or theft
    And unauthorized transfers before notice total $30
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the maximum consumer liability must be $30

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers before notice exceed $50
    Given the consumer notified the financial institution within
      two business days after learning of the loss or theft
    And unauthorized transfers before notice total $100
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the maximum consumer liability must be $50
```

### **US-03: Calculate liability when notice is not provided within two business days**

**User story**

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to calculate consumer liability when the consumer does not report the loss or theft of an access device within two business days, so that the liability calculation reflects the applicable $500 cap and qualifying transfers.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.6(b)(2)

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Calculate liability when timely notice is not given

  Scenario: Consumer does not notify within two business days
    Given the consumer learned of the loss or theft of an access device
    And the consumer failed to notify the financial institution
      within two business days
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the maximum consumer liability must not exceed
      the lesser of $500 or the sum of:
      | Liability component |
      | Lesser of $50 or unauthorized transfers within two business days |
      | Qualifying unauthorized transfers after two business days
        and before notice |

  Scenario: Transfers within two business days are less than $50
    Given the consumer failed to notify the financial institution
      within two business days
    And unauthorized transfers within the two business days total $30
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the first liability component must be $30

  Scenario: Transfers within two business days exceed $50
    Given the consumer failed to notify the financial institution
      within two business days
    And unauthorized transfers within the two business days total $100
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the first liability component must be $50

  Scenario: Institution establishes that later transfers would have
    been prevented by timely notice
    Given the consumer failed to notify the financial institution
      within two business days
    And unauthorized transfers occurred after the close of
      two business days and before notice
    And the financial institution establishes that those transfers
      would not have occurred if the consumer had notified it
      within two business days
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then those qualifying transfers must be included
      in the second liability component

  Scenario: Institution does not establish that later transfers
    would have been prevented by timely notice
    Given the consumer failed to notify the financial institution
      within two business days
    And unauthorized transfers occurred after the close of
      two business days and before notice
    And the financial institution does not establish that those
      transfers would have been prevented by timely notice
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then those transfers must not be included
      in the second liability component

  Scenario: Total liability components exceed $500
    Given the consumer failed to notify the financial institution
      within two business days
    And the sum of the applicable liability components exceeds $500
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the maximum consumer liability must be $500

  Scenario: Total liability components are below $500
    Given the consumer failed to notify the financial institution
      within two business days
    And the sum of the applicable liability components is $300
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the maximum consumer liability must be $300
```

### **US-04: Apply the 60-day periodic statement reporting requirement**

**User story**

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to identify unauthorized transfers reported within 60 days of periodic statement transmittal, so that the consumer is not held liable for subsequent transfers under the conditions specified in the regulation.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.6(b)(3)

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Apply periodic statement reporting requirement

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfer reported within 60 days
    Given an unauthorized electronic fund transfer appears
      on a periodic statement
    And the consumer reports the unauthorized transfer within
      60 days of the financial institution's transmittal
      of the statement
    When the platform evaluates liability for subsequent transfers
    Then the consumer must not be held liable for subsequent transfers
      under the failure-to-report provision of Section 1005.6(b)(3)

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfer not reported within 60 days
    Given an unauthorized electronic fund transfer appears
      on a periodic statement
    And the consumer fails to report the transfer within 60 days
      of the financial institution's transmittal of the statement
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the maximum liability under the 60-day provision
      must not exceed the amount of unauthorized transfers
      occurring after the close of the 60 days and before notice
    And those transfers must be established by the institution
      as transfers that would not have occurred had the consumer
      notified the institution within the 60-day period

  Scenario: Institution establishes that subsequent transfers
    would have been prevented by notice within 60 days
    Given the consumer failed to report an unauthorized transfer
      within 60 days of statement transmittal
    And unauthorized transfers occurred after the close
      of the 60-day period and before notice
    And the financial institution establishes that those transfers
      would not have occurred if the consumer had notified
      the institution within the 60-day period
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the qualifying subsequent transfers must be included
      in the liability calculation

  Scenario: Institution does not establish that subsequent transfers
    would have been prevented by notice within 60 days
    Given the consumer failed to report an unauthorized transfer
      within 60 days of statement transmittal
    And unauthorized transfers occurred after the close
      of the 60-day period and before notice
    And the financial institution does not establish that those
      transfers would have been prevented by timely notice
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then those transfers must not be included
      in the liability calculation under Section 1005.6(b)(3)

  Scenario: Access device is involved in the unauthorized transfer
    Given an unauthorized transfer appearing on a periodic statement
      involves an access device
    And the consumer failed to report the transfer within 60 days
      of statement transmittal
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the platform must also evaluate liability under
      Section 1005.6(b)(1) or Section 1005.6(b)(2), as applicable
    And the consumer may be liable for other amounts
      permitted under the applicable paragraph
```

**User story**

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to account for extenuating circumstances that caused a consumer's delay in notification, so that applicable notification time limits are extended to a reasonable period.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.6(b)(4)

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Extend notification time limits for extenuating circumstances

  Scenario: Extenuating circumstances caused delayed notification
    Given the consumer delayed notifying the financial institution
    And the delay was due to extenuating circumstances
    When the platform evaluates the applicable notification deadline
    Then the institution must extend the applicable time limit
      to a reasonable period

  Scenario: No extenuating circumstances caused the delay
    Given the consumer delayed notifying the financial institution
    And the delay was not due to extenuating circumstances

  Scenario: Extended reasonable period is met

```

### **US-06: Recognize valid methods and timing of consumer notice**

**User story**

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to recognize notice based on the consumer's reasonable steps and permitted communication methods, so that notice is not dependent on actual receipt by a particular employee or agent.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.6(b)(5)(i)–(iii)

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Recognize valid consumer notice

  Scenario: Consumer takes steps reasonably necessary to provide notice
    Given the consumer takes steps reasonably necessary
      to provide the financial institution with pertinent information
    When the platform evaluates the notice date
    Then notice must be considered given
    And notice must not depend on whether a particular employee
      or agent actually receives the information

  Scenario: Consumer provides notice in person
    Given the consumer provides pertinent information
      to the financial institution in person
    When the platform evaluates the notice
    Then the notice method must be recognized as permitted

  Scenario: Consumer provides notice by telephone
    Given the consumer provides pertinent information
      to the financial institution by telephone
    When the platform evaluates the notice
    Then the notice method must be recognized as permitted

  Scenario: Consumer provides notice in writing
    Given the consumer provides written notice to the institution
    When the platform evaluates the notice
    Then the notice method must be recognized as permitted

  Scenario: Written notice is mailed
    Given the consumer mails written notice to the financial institution
    When the platform determines the notice date
    Then the notice date must be the date the consumer mailed the notice

  Scenario: Written notice is delivered through another usual means
    Given the consumer delivers written notice for transmission
      to the financial institution through another usual means
    When the platform determines the notice date
    Then the notice date must be the date the consumer delivered
      the notice for transmission

  Scenario: Institution becomes aware of circumstances indicating
    a possible unauthorized transfer
    Given the financial institution becomes aware of circumstances
      leading to a reasonable belief that an unauthorized transfer

```

### **US-07: Apply lower liability limits imposed by state law or agreement**

**User story**

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to apply a lower consumer liability amount when state law or an agreement between the consumer and the financial institution imposes less liability than Regulation E, so that the consumer is not charged more than the applicable lower amount.

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.6(b)(6)

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Apply lower liability limits under state law or agreement

  Scenario: State law imposes lower consumer liability
    Given the consumer's liability has been calculated
      under Section 1005.6
    And applicable state law imposes less liability
      than Section 1005.6
    When the platform determines the final consumer liability
    Then the consumer's liability must not exceed
      the amount imposed under state law

  Scenario: Consumer agreement imposes lower liability
    Given the consumer's liability has been calculated
      under Section 1005.6
    And an agreement between the consumer and the financial institution
      imposes less liability than Section 1005.6
    When the platform determines the final consumer liability
    Then the consumer's liability must not exceed
      the amount imposed under the agreement

  Scenario: State law or agreement does not impose lower liability
    Given the consumer's liability has been calculated

```

## **4. Business calculation rules**

The following calculation rules summarize the mathematical requirements expressed in § 1005.6(b).

### **BR-01: Timely notice — two-business-day rule**

When the consumer notifies the financial institution within two business days after learning of the loss or theft of the access device:

\\[ L_1 = \min(50,\ U\_{\text{before notice}}) \\]

Where:

- \\(L_1\\) = maximum consumer liability.
- \\(U\_{\text{before notice}}\\) = unauthorized transfers occurring before notice.

### **BR-02: Notice after two business days**

When the consumer fails to notify the institution within two business days:

\\[ L_2 = \min(500,\ A+B) \\]

Where:

\\[ A = \min(50,\ U\_{\text{within 2 days}}) \\]

\\[ B = U\_{\text{after 2 days and before notice, qualifying}} \\]

A transfer qualifies for component B only when the financial institution establishes that it would not have occurred had the consumer notified the institution within the two-business-day period.

### **BR-03: Periodic statement — 60-day rule**

When the consumer fails to report an unauthorized transfer appearing on a periodic statement within 60 days of statement transmittal:

\\[ L_3 = U\_{\text{after 60 days and before notice, qualifying}} \\]

A transfer qualifies only when the financial institution establishes that it would not have occurred had the consumer notified the institution within the 60-day period.

When an access device is involved, the consumer may also be liable for amounts under BR-01 or BR-02, as applicable.

### **BR-04: Extenuating circumstances**

Where the delay in notification is due to extenuating circumstances:

- The applicable time limit must be extended.
- The extension must be to a reasonable period.

### **BR-05: Lower liability under state law or agreement**

The final liability must not exceed the amount imposed by state law or the consumer agreement when either imposes less liability than § 1005.6.

## **7. Items requiring business clarification before implementation**

The supplied regulatory text establishes the liability rules but does not specify every operational detail needed for a production implementation. These items should be resolved through business and technology design rather than assumed as regulatory requirements.

| Topic                     | Clarification required                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Business-day calendar     | Which business-day calendar and holiday schedule will the platform use?                                                                                     |
| Learning date             | How will the platform capture the date the consumer learned of the loss or theft?                                                                           |
| Notice timestamp          | How will the platform record notice dates for in-person, telephone, and written communications?                                                             |
| Constructive notice       | What circumstances and evidence will support a reasonable belief that an unauthorized transfer has been or may be made?                                     |
| Institution's burden      | How will the platform capture the institution's determination and supporting evidence that subsequent transfers would have been prevented by timely notice? |
| Extenuating circumstances | Who determines whether circumstances are extenuating and what constitutes a reasonable extension?                                                           |
| Statement transmittal     | What source establishes the periodic statement's transmittal date?                                                                                          |
| State law and agreements  | How will applicable lower liability limits be identified and maintained?                                                                                    |
| Related transfers         | How will the platform group a series of related unauthorized transfers for liability calculations?                                                          |

**Important implementation distinction:** The regulation states that the institution must establish that certain later transfers would not have occurred following timely notice. The platform should not treat the mere existence of later unauthorized transfers as sufficient to include them in consumer liability.
