## Overview & Regulatory Scope

Under **Regulation E (12 CFR § 1005.6)**, consumer liability for unauthorized electronic fund transfers (EFTs) depends on two primary factors: **whether an access device (such as a debit card or PIN) was lost or stolen**, and **how quickly the consumer notifies the financial institution** after learning of the loss/theft or receiving a periodic statement showing the unauthorized transfer.

The user stories below define the automated liability calculation engine and case-worker workflow for the bank's disputes platform.

---

## Epic: Automated Regulation E Consumer Liability Engine (12 CFR § 1005.6)

### Story 1: Lost or Stolen Access Device — 2-Business-Day Notification Window

**As a** Dispute Operations Specialist,

**I want** the disputes platform to cap consumer liability at **$50** (or the amount of unauthorized transfers, whichever is less) when a consumer reports a lost or stolen access device within **2 business days** of learning of the loss or theft,

**So that** the bank complies with 12 CFR § 1005.6(b)(1) and accurately calculates provisional credit and final liability.

**Acceptance Criteria:**

```gherkin
Feature: Reg E Liability - Lost or Stolen Access Device (Within 2 Business Days)

  Background:
    Given a dispute case is created for an unauthorized Electronic Fund Transfer (EFT)
    And the unauthorized transfer involved an access device (e.g., debit card)
    And the consumer reported the access device as lost or stolen

  Scenario: Consumer reports loss/theft within 2 business days and unauthorized transfers exceed $50
    Given the consumer learned of the loss or theft on "2026-11-02" (Monday)
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-11-04" (Wednesday, within 2 business days)
    And unauthorized transfers totaling "$420.00" occurred before notification
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the maximum consumer liability shall be set to "$50.00"
    And the bank responsibility (reimbursable amount) shall be calculated as "$370.00"

  Scenario: Consumer reports loss/theft within 2 business days and unauthorized transfers are less than $50
    Given the consumer learned of the loss or theft on "2026-11-02"
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-11-03" (within 2 business days)
    And unauthorized transfers totaling "$32.50" occurred before notification
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the maximum consumer liability shall be set to "$32.50"
    And the bank responsibility shall be calculated as "$0.00"

  Scenario: Zero liability for transfers occurring after notification to the bank
    Given the consumer notified the bank of the lost card on "2026-11-03" at "14:00 EST"
    And an unauthorized transfer of "$150.00" occurred on "2026-11-03" at "16:30 EST"
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the consumer liability for the post-notification transfer shall be "$0.00"

```

### Story 2: Lost or Stolen Access Device — Delayed Notification (> 2 Business Days, Within 60 Days of Statement)

**As a** Dispute Operations Specialist,

**I want** the system to apply the **$500 liability tier** when a consumer fails to report a lost or stolen access device within **2 business days** of learning of the loss/theft,

**So that** the system enforces 12 CFR § 1005.6(b)(2) by calculating the sum of the first 2 business days' losses (up to $50) plus subsequent preventable losses up to the $500 statutory cap.

**Acceptance Criteria:**

```gherkin
Feature: Reg E Liability - Lost or Stolen Access Device (After 2 Business Days)

  Background:
    Given a dispute case involves a lost or stolen access device
    And the bank establishes that the later transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified the bank within 2 business days

  Scenario: Consumer reports loss/theft after 2 business days with high-value unauthorized transfers
    Given the consumer learned of the theft on "2026-11-02" (Monday)
    And the 2-business-day deadline expired at the end of "2026-11-04" (Wednesday)
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-11-09" (Monday)
    And "$40.00" in unauthorized transfers occurred between "2026-11-02" and "2026-11-04"
    And "$600.00" in unauthorized transfers occurred between "2026-11-05" and "2026-11-09"
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the Tier 1 liability (first 2 business days) shall be "$40.00" (lesser of $50 or $40.00)
    And the Tier 2 liability (after 2 business days) shall be "$460.00" ($500.00 cap minus $40.00)
    And the total maximum consumer liability shall be capped at "$500.00"
    And the bank responsibility shall be calculated as "$140.00" ($640.00 total minus $500.00)

  Scenario: Consumer reports loss/theft after 2 business days with low-value transfers under $500 total
    Given the consumer learned of the loss on "2026-11-02"
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-11-10"
    And "$100.00" in unauthorized transfers occurred on "2026-11-03" (within 2 business days)
    And "$180.00" in unauthorized transfers occurred on "2026-11-07" (after 2 business days)
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the Tier 1 liability shall be "$50.00"
    And the Tier 2 liability shall be "$180.00"
    And the total consumer liability shall be "$230.00"
    And the bank responsibility shall be "$50.00"

```

### Story 3: Unauthorized Transfers Without Loss or Theft of an Access Device (60-Day Statement Rule)

**As a** Dispute Operations Specialist,

**I want** the platform to assign **$0 consumer liability** for unauthorized EFTs not involving a lost or stolen access device (e.g., unauthorized ACH debits, card-not-present skimming/cloning while the physical card remains in the consumer's possession) provided the consumer reports them within **60 calendar days** of statement transmittal,

**So that** the bank complies with 12 CFR § 1005.6(b)(3).

**Acceptance Criteria:**

```gherkin
Feature: Reg E Liability - No Lost or Stolen Access Device (60-Day Statement Window)

  Scenario: Unauthorized ACH or cloned card reported within 60 days of periodic statement
    Given an unauthorized EFT of "$1,250.00" occurred on "2026-10-10"
    And the access device was NOT lost or stolen
    And the periodic statement reflecting the transfer was transmitted on "2026-10-31"
    And the consumer reported the unauthorized transfer on "2026-12-15" (45 calendar days after statement transmittal)
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the consumer liability shall be "$0.00"
    And the bank responsibility shall be "$1,250.00"

  Scenario: Unauthorized EFT without lost/stolen device reported AFTER 60 calendar days
    Given the periodic statement reflecting an initial unauthorized ACH of "$300.00" was transmitted on "2026-09-01"
    And the 60-calendar-day reporting window expired on "2026-10-31"
    And a subsequent unauthorized ACH of "$900.00" occurred on "2026-11-05"
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-11-12"
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the consumer liability for the "2026-09-01" statement transfer ("$300.00") shall be "$0.00"
    And the consumer liability for the "2026-11-05" transfer (after day 60) shall be "$900.00" (unlimited liability for post-60-day preventable transfers)

```

### Story 4: Unlimited Liability After 60 Calendar Days (Lost or Stolen Access Device)

**As a** Dispute Operations Specialist,

**I want** the system to partition unauthorized transfers occurring **before** and **after** the 60-calendar-day statement deadline when a lost or stolen access device goes unreported,

**So that** the consumer's liability is capped at $500 for transfers within the 60-day window and becomes unlimited for preventable transfers occurring after day 60 (12 CFR § 1005.6(b)(3)).

**Acceptance Criteria:**

```gherkin
Feature: Reg E Liability - Lost/Stolen Device Reported After 60 Calendar Days

  Scenario: Partitioning liability across the 2-business-day, 60-calendar-day, and post-60-day windows
    Given the consumer lost their debit card on "2026-08-10"
    And "$200.00" in unauthorized transfers occurred between "2026-08-10" and "2026-08-12" (within 2 business days)
    And "$600.00" in unauthorized transfers occurred between "2026-08-13" and "2026-10-29"
    And the periodic statement showing the August transfers was transmitted on "2026-08-31"
    And the 60-calendar-day window closed on "2026-10-30"
    And "$1,100.00" in unauthorized transfers occurred on "2026-11-04" (after the 60-day window)
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-11-06"
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the liability for transfers prior to "2026-10-30" shall be capped at "$500.00"
    And the liability for the "2026-11-04" transfer shall be "$1,100.00"
    And the total consumer liability shall be "$1,600.00"
    And the bank responsibility shall be "$300.00" ($1,900.00 total minus $1,600.00)

```

### Story 5: Extenuating Circumstances Extension Override

**As a** Dispute Operations Supervisor,

**I want** the ability to apply an **Extenuating Circumstances Override** (such as extended travel or hospitalization) with documented justification,

**So that** the platform extends the 2-business-day and/or 60-calendar-day notification windows to a reasonable period in compliance with 12 CFR § 1005.6(b)(4).

**Acceptance Criteria:**

```gherkin
Feature: Reg E Extenuating Circumstances Extension

  Scenario: Supervisor applies extenuating circumstances for hospitalization
    Given a dispute case has calculated "$500.00" liability due to reporting a stolen card on Day 5
    And the consumer provides documentation of inpatient hospitalization during Days 1 through 4
    When a user with role "Dispute_Supervisor" selects "Apply Extenuating Circumstances Extension"
    And selects reason code "HOSPITALIZATION"
    And enters an adjusted effective reporting deadline of Day 6
    Then the system shall recalculate liability using the within-2-business-days rule ("$50.00" cap)
    And the system shall log the supervisor's user ID, timestamp, reason code, and previous/new liability amounts in the audit trail

  Scenario: Standard agent cannot apply extenuating circumstances override without approval
    Given a user is logged in with role "Dispute_Agent"
    When the user views the liability calculation screen
    Then the "Apply Extenuating Circumstances Extension" action shall be disabled or hidden

```

### Story 6: Card Network Zero-Liability Policy Hierarchy

**As a** Product Compliance Manager,

**I want** the disputes platform to compare the statutory Regulation E liability against applicable card network Zero-Liability rules (e.g., Visa/Mastercard Zero Liability for signature/PIN debit) and apply the **lesser liability to the consumer**,

**So that** the bank honors both federal law (12 CFR § 1005.6(b)(6)) and card brand agreements without overcharging customers.

**Acceptance Criteria:**

```gherkin
Feature: Statutory vs. Card Network Agreement Liability Comparison

  Scenario: Card network Zero-Liability policy overrides $50 Reg E statutory liability
    Given a dispute involves an unauthorized Visa debit card transaction of "$300.00"
    And the statutory Reg E calculation determines consumer liability is "$50.00"
    And the account product is eligible for "Card Network Zero Liability ($0.00)"
    When the final liability determination runs
    Then the system shall record Statutory Reg E Liability as "$50.00"
    And the system shall record Contractual/Network Liability as "$0.00"
    And the applied consumer liability shall be set to "$0.00" (the lesser amount)

```

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
