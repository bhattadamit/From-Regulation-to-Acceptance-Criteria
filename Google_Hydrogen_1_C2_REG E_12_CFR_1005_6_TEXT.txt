### Epic: Regulation E Consumer Liability Engine (12 CFR § 1005.6)

---

### Story 1: Validate Prerequisites for Consumer Liability

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Engine

**I want to** verify whether the bank met mandatory initial disclosure and access device prerequisites before assigning any liability to a consumer

**So that** consumers are held liable for unauthorized Electronic Fund Transfers (EFTs) only when legally permissible under 12 CFR § 1005.6(a).

#### Acceptance Criteria

```gherkin
Feature: Conditions for Consumer Liability (§ 1005.6(a))

  Background:
    Given an unauthorized EFT claim has been submitted on a consumer account

  Scenario: Initial § 1005.7(b)(1)-(3) disclosures were not provided
    Given the bank has not provided the disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the consumer's maximum liability shall be set to $0.00
    And the engine shall bypass further liability tier calculations

  Scenario: Transfer involves an unaccepted access device
    Given the bank provided the disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    And the unauthorized EFT involved an access device
    And the access device is not an accepted access device
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the consumer's maximum liability shall be set to $0.00

  Scenario: Bank failed to provide consumer identification means for the access device
    Given the bank provided the disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    And the unauthorized EFT involved an accepted access device
    And the bank did not provide a means to identify the consumer to whom the device was issued
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the consumer's maximum liability shall be set to $0.00

  Scenario: All prerequisites met for an access device transfer
    Given the bank provided the disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    And the unauthorized EFT involved an accepted access device
    And the bank provided a means to identify the consumer to whom the device was issued
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the claim shall proceed to liability limitation calculations under § 1005.6(b)

  Scenario: All prerequisites met for a non-access device transfer (e.g., ACH)
    Given the bank provided the disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    And the unauthorized EFT did not involve an access device
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the claim shall proceed to liability limitation calculations under § 1005.6(b)

```

      | channel      | submission_timestamp |
      | in person    | 2026-11-10T10:15:00Z |

  Scenario: Consumer provides written notice by mail or usual transmission

  Scenario: Bank becomes aware of circumstances indicating potential unauthorized transfers (Constructive Notice)

### Story 4: Calculate Tier 2 Liability for Lost or Stolen Access Device (Late Notice > 2 Business Days)

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Engine

**I want to** calculate consumer liability up to $500 when the consumer fails to notify the bank within two business days of learning of a lost or stolen access device

**So that** the platform accurately applies the two-part formula and preventability test under 12 CFR § 1005.6(b)(2).

#### Acceptance Criteria

```gherkin
Feature: Access Device Liability - Late Notice After 2 Business Days (§ 1005.6(b)(2))

  Background:
    Given all § 1005.6(a) conditions for liability are met
    And the unauthorized EFTs involved the loss or theft of an accepted access device
    And the consumer failed to notify the bank within 2 business days of learning of the loss or theft

  Scenario: Combined liability exceeds $500 cap
    Given unauthorized transfers within the first 2 business days total $100.00
    And unauthorized transfers after the close of 2 business days and before notice total $600.00
    And the bank establishes that the $600.00 in post-2-day transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified within 2 business days
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the Tier 1 component shall be $50.00 (lesser of $50 or $100.00)
    And the Tier 2 component shall be $600.00
    And the consumer's maximum liability shall be capped at $500.00

  Scenario: Combined liability is below $500 cap with sub-$50 transfers in first 2 business days
    Given unauthorized transfers within the first 2 business days total $30.00
    And unauthorized transfers after the close of 2 business days and before notice total $200.00
    And the bank establishes that the $200.00 in post-2-day transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified within 2 business days
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the Tier 1 component shall be $30.00 (lesser of $50 or $30.00)
    And the Tier 2 component shall be $200.00
    And the consumer's maximum liability shall be $230.00

  Scenario: Bank fails to establish that post-2-day transfers were preventable
    Given unauthorized transfers within the first 2 business days total $80.00
    And unauthorized transfers after the close of 2 business days and before notice total $400.00
    And the bank does not establish that the $400.00 in transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified within 2 business days
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the Tier 1 component shall be $50.00
    And the Tier 2 component shall be $0.00
    And the consumer's maximum liability shall be $50.00

```

### Story 6: Extend Reporting Deadlines for Extenuating Circumstances

**As a** Disputes Specialist

**I want to** record extenuating circumstances for a consumer's delayed reporting so that the system extends the 2-business-day and 60-day deadlines to a reasonable period

**So that** consumers are not penalized for delays beyond their control under 12 CFR § 1005.6(b)(4).

#### Acceptance Criteria

```gherkin
Feature: Extension of Time Limits for Extenuating Circumstances (§ 1005.6(b)(4))

  Scenario: Extenuating circumstances extend the 2-business-day deadline
    Given the consumer learned of a lost or stolen access device on "2026-11-02"
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-11-10"
    And a dispute specialist flags that the delay was due to documented extenuating circumstances
    And the extended reasonable deadline is set to "2026-11-12"
    When the liability calculation engine re-evaluates the claim
    Then the notice shall be treated as timely within the extended period
    And the consumer's liability shall be capped under § 1005.6(b)(1) (lesser of $50 or pre-notice transfers)

  Scenario: Extenuating circumstances extend the 60-day periodic statement deadline
    Given a periodic statement was transmitted on "2026-09-01"
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-11-15" (75 days after transmittal)
    And a dispute specialist flags that the delay was due to documented extenuating circumstances
    And the extended reasonable deadline is set to "2026-11-20"
    When the liability calculation engine re-evaluates the claim
    Then the notice shall be treated as timely within the extended 60-day period
    And no post-60-day liability under § 1005.6(b)(3) shall be assessed

```

### Story 8: Apply Liability Across a Series of Related Unauthorized Transfers

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Engine

**I want to** group related unauthorized EFTs into a single series when calculating liability caps

**So that** the $50 and $500 statutory limits apply once per series rather than per individual transaction, in accordance with 12 CFR § 1005.6(b).

#### Acceptance Criteria

```gherkin
Feature: Series of Related Unauthorized Transfers (§ 1005.6(b))

  Scenario: Multiple unauthorized transfers from a single lost access device are grouped as one series
    Given all § 1005.6(a) conditions for liability are met
    And a consumer lost an accepted debit card and notified the bank within 2 business days
    And 4 separate unauthorized transactions of $40.00 each ($160.00 total) occurred prior to notice
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the 4 transactions shall be evaluated together as a single series of related unauthorized transfers
    And the consumer's total maximum liability across all 4 transactions shall be $50.00 (not $40.00 x 4)

```
