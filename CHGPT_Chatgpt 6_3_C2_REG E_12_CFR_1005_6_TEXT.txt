# **Regulation E – Consumer Liability for Unauthorized Transfers**

**Business Requirements and User Stories for Disputes Platform**

**Regulatory reference:** 12 CFR § 1005.6 – Liability of consumer for unauthorized transfers

**Role:** Senior Business Analyst\
**Scope:** Implementation of consumer liability determination for unauthorized electronic fund transfers (EFTs) within the disputes platform.

## **1. Business objective**

Implement automated consumer liability calculations for unauthorized EFT disputes in accordance with 12 CFR § 1005.6.

The platform must determine whether consumer liability may be imposed, calculate applicable liability limits, evaluate notification timing, account for periodic statement reporting, support extensions for extenuating circumstances, recognize valid notification methods, and apply lower liability limits established by state law or agreement.

The platform must maintain sufficient case information to support the liability determination, including the applicable calculation inputs and institutional findings.

## **2. Functional scope and business rules**

| Rule ID | Regulatory requirement | Platform behavior                                                                                                                          |
| ------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| RE-01   | § 1005.6(a)            | Liability eligibility depends on required disclosures and, when applicable, accepted access device and consumer identification capability. |
| RE-02   | § 1005.6(b)(1)         | Timely notification within two business days limits liability to the lesser of $50 or unauthorized transfers before notice.                |
| RE-03   | § 1005.6(b)(2)         | Late notification applies the lesser of $500 or the sum of the applicable two-day amount and qualifying subsequent transfers.              |
| RE-04   | § 1005.6(b)(3)         | Failure to report statement transactions within 60 days can result in liability for qualifying subsequent transfers.                       |
| RE-05   | § 1005.6(b)(4)         | Extenuating circumstances require extension of notification time limits to a reasonable period.                                            |
| RE-06   | § 1005.6(b)(5)         | Notification can be provided in person, by telephone, or in writing, subject to the regulatory notice standards.                           |
| RE-07   | § 1005.6(b)(6)         | Lower liability under state law or agreement supersedes the liability otherwise determined under § 1005.6.                                 |

# **3. User stories and Gherkin acceptance criteria**

## **US-01: Validate eligibility to impose consumer liability**

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.6(a)

**User story**

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to validate the regulatory conditions for imposing consumer liability so that liability is only assessed when the required conditions are satisfied.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Validate consumer liability eligibility

  Scenario: Required disclosures have been provided
    Given an unauthorized electronic fund transfer is reported
    And the financial institution provided the disclosures
      required by 12 CFR 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    When the platform evaluates consumer liability eligibility
    Then the disclosure condition is satisfied

  Scenario: Required disclosures have not been provided
    Given an unauthorized electronic fund transfer is reported
    And the financial institution did not provide one or more
      of the disclosures required by 12 CFR 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    When the platform evaluates consumer liability eligibility
    Then the consumer may not be held liable under Section 1005.6

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfer does not involve an access device
    Given an unauthorized electronic fund transfer is reported
    And the transfer did not involve an access device
    And the required disclosures were provided
    When the platform evaluates consumer liability eligibility
    Then the access device conditions do not apply

  Scenario: Access device is not accepted
    Given an unauthorized electronic fund transfer is reported
    And the transfer involved an access device
    And the access device was not accepted
    When the platform evaluates consumer liability eligibility
    Then the consumer may not be held liable under Section 1005.6

  Scenario: Institution did not provide a means of identifying
            the consumer to whom the access device was issued
    Given an unauthorized electronic fund transfer is reported
    And the transfer involved an accepted access device
    And the financial institution did not provide a means
      to identify the consumer to whom the device was issued
    When the platform evaluates consumer liability eligibility
    Then the consumer may not be held liable under Section 1005.6
```

**Implementation note:** The platform should distinguish between transfers involving an access device and transfers that do not involve an access device.

## **US-02: Calculate liability when the consumer provides timely notice**

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.6(b)(1)

**User story**

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to calculate consumer liability when notification occurs within two business days after the consumer learns of the loss or theft of an access device so that the applicable regulatory liability cap is applied.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Calculate liability for timely notice

  Scenario: Consumer reports loss or theft within two business days
    Given the consumer has reported the loss or theft
      of an access device
    And the consumer notified the financial institution
      within two business days after learning of the loss or theft
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then liability must not exceed the lesser of:
      | Liability limit |
      | $50 |
      | Unauthorized transfers occurring before notice |

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers before notice are less than $50
    Given the consumer provided timely notice
    And unauthorized transfers before notice total $30
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then maximum consumer liability is $30

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers before notice exceed $50
    Given the consumer provided timely notice
    And unauthorized transfers before notice total $200
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then maximum consumer liability is $50

  Scenario: No unauthorized transfers occurred before notice
    Given the consumer provided timely notice
    And no unauthorized transfers occurred before notice
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then maximum consumer liability is $0
```

**Business calculation:**

\\[ L = \min(50,\ U\_{\text{before notice}}) \\]

Where:

- \\(L\\) = maximum consumer liability.
- \\(U\_{\text{before notice}}\\) = unauthorized transfers before notice.

## **US-03: Calculate liability when the consumer does not provide timely notice**

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.6(b)(2)

**User story**

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to calculate liability when the consumer fails to notify the institution within two business days after learning of the loss or theft of an access device so that the applicable $500 cap and qualifying subsequent transfers are considered.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Calculate liability for untimely notice

  Scenario: Consumer fails to notify within two business days
    Given the consumer learned of the loss or theft of an access device
    And the consumer did not notify the financial institution
      within two business days
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then liability must not exceed the lesser of $500 or the sum of:
      | Component |
      | The lesser of $50 or unauthorized transfers
        occurring within the two business days |
      | Unauthorized transfers occurring after the close
        of two business days and before notice
        that the institution establishes would not
        have occurred had timely notice been given |

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers within two business days
    Given the consumer failed to provide timely notice
    And unauthorized transfers within the two business days total $30
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the first liability component is $30

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers within two business days exceed $50
    Given the consumer failed to provide timely notice
    And unauthorized transfers within the two business days total $100
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the first liability component is $50

  Scenario: Institution establishes subsequent transfers
    Given the consumer failed to provide timely notice
    And unauthorized transfers within the two business days total $40
    And subsequent unauthorized transfers before notice total $300
    And the institution establishes that those subsequent transfers
      would not have occurred had the consumer notified it
      within the two-business-day period
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the liability calculation includes $40
    And the liability calculation includes $300
    And maximum consumer liability is $340

  Scenario: Institution does not establish subsequent transfers
    Given the consumer failed to provide timely notice
    And unauthorized transfers within the two business days total $40
    And subsequent unauthorized transfers before notice total $300
    And the institution does not establish that those subsequent
      transfers would not have occurred had timely notice been given
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the liability calculation includes $40
    And the liability calculation excludes $300

  Scenario: Total calculated liability exceeds $500
    Given the consumer failed to provide timely notice
    And the applicable liability components total $600
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then maximum consumer liability is $500
```

**Business calculation:**

\\[ L = \min(500,\ A+B) \\]

Where:

\\[ A = \min(50,\ U\_{\text{within 2 days}}) \\]

\\[ B = U\_{\text{after 2 days and before notice, qualifying}} \\]

The institution must establish that transfers included in component B would not have occurred had the consumer provided notice within two business days.

## **US-04: Calculate liability related to periodic statement reporting**

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.6(b)(3)

**User story**

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to evaluate whether an unauthorized transfer appearing on a periodic statement was reported within 60 days of statement transmittal so that liability for subsequent unauthorized transfers can be determined.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Evaluate periodic statement reporting

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfer reported within 60 days
    Given an unauthorized electronic fund transfer appears
      on a periodic statement
    And the consumer reports the unauthorized transfer
      within 60 days of the financial institution's transmittal
      of the statement
    When the platform evaluates periodic statement liability
    Then the consumer avoids liability for subsequent transfers
      under Section 1005.6(b)(3)

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfer not reported within 60 days
    Given an unauthorized electronic fund transfer appears
      on a periodic statement
    And the consumer does not report the unauthorized transfer
      within 60 days of the financial institution's transmittal
      of the statement
    When the platform evaluates periodic statement liability
    Then the platform identifies potential liability for
      subsequent unauthorized transfers

  Scenario: Subsequent transfers are established by the institution
    Given the consumer failed to report the unauthorized transfer
      within 60 days of statement transmittal
    And subsequent unauthorized transfers occurred after
      the close of the 60-day period and before notice
    And the institution establishes that those transfers
      would not have occurred had the consumer notified
      the institution within the 60-day period
    When the platform calculates periodic statement liability
    Then liability must not exceed the amount of those
      qualifying subsequent unauthorized transfers

  Scenario: Institution does not establish subsequent transfers
    Given the consumer failed to report the unauthorized transfer
      within 60 days of statement transmittal
    And subsequent unauthorized transfers occurred after
      the close of the 60-day period and before notice
    And the institution does not establish that those transfers
      would not have occurred had timely notice been given
    When the platform calculates periodic statement liability
    Then those subsequent transfers are excluded
      from periodic statement liability

  Scenario: Access device is involved in the unauthorized transfer
    Given the unauthorized transfer involved an access device
    And the consumer failed to report the transfer
      within 60 days of statement transmittal
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the platform also considers liability under
      Section 1005.6(b)(1) or Section 1005.6(b)(2)
      as applicable
```

**Business calculation:**

\\[ L\_{60} = U\_{\text{after 60 days and before notice, qualifying}} \\]

This amount is subject to the requirement that the institution establish that the subsequent transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified the institution within the 60-day period.

**Important:** For access-device transactions, the platform must also consider the applicable two-business-day liability provisions. The periodic statement liability provision does not replace those provisions.

## **US-05: Extend notification time limits for extenuating circumstances**

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.6(b)(4)

**User story**

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to support reasonable extensions of notification deadlines when the consumer's delay was caused by extenuating circumstances so that the liability determination reflects the required regulatory extension.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Extend notification deadlines

  Scenario: Consumer delay is due to extenuating circumstances
    Given the consumer delayed notifying the financial institution
    And the delay was due to extenuating circumstances
    When the platform evaluates the notification deadline
    Then the applicable notification time limits must be extended
    And the extension must be to a reasonable period

  Scenario: Consumer delay is not due to extenuating circumstances
    Given the consumer delayed notifying the financial institution
    And the delay was not due to extenuating circumstances
    When the platform evaluates the notification deadline
    Then the standard notification time limits apply

  Scenario: Extended notification period is satisfied
    Given the consumer's notification deadline was extended
      due to extenuating circumstances
    And the consumer notified the financial institution
      within the reasonable extended period
    When the platform evaluates consumer liability
    Then the extended notification period must be applied
      in the liability determination
```

**Implementation note:** The regulation requires an extension to a reasonable period but does not prescribe a specific number of additional days. The platform should support a configurable reasonable extension rather than hardcoding an extension period into the regulatory calculation.

## **US-06: Recognize valid consumer notification methods and timing**

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.6(b)(5)

**User story**

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to recognize consumer notification based on the regulatory definition of notice so that liability deadlines are calculated using the appropriate notification date and method.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Recognize consumer notification

  Scenario: Consumer provides notice in person
    Given the consumer takes steps reasonably necessary
      to provide pertinent information to the institution
    And notification is provided in person
    When the platform records the notification
    Then the notification method is recorded as in person

  Scenario: Consumer provides notice by telephone
    Given the consumer takes steps reasonably necessary
      to provide pertinent information to the institution
    And notification is provided by telephone
    When the platform records the notification
    Then the notification method is recorded as telephone

  Scenario: Consumer provides written notice
    Given the consumer takes steps reasonably necessary
      to provide pertinent information to the institution
    And written notice is mailed to the institution
    When the platform records the notification
    Then the notification date is the date the consumer mailed the notice

  Scenario: Consumer delivers written notice through another usual means
    Given the consumer takes steps reasonably necessary
      to provide pertinent information to the institution
    And written notice is delivered for transmission
      to the institution through another usual means
    When the platform records the notification
    Then the notification date is the date the consumer
      delivered the notice for transmission

  Scenario: Employee does not actually receive the notification
    Given the consumer takes steps reasonably necessary
      to provide pertinent information to the institution
    And notice is given in accordance with Section 1005.6(b)(5)
    And a particular employee or agent does not actually
      receive the information
    When the platform evaluates whether notice was given
    Then the notification is recognized as given

  Scenario: Institution becomes aware of circumstances indicating
            a possible unauthorized transfer
    Given the institution becomes aware of circumstances
      leading to a reasonable belief that an unauthorized transfer
      to or from the consumer's account has been or may be made
    When the platform evaluates whether notice was given
    Then the platform supports recognizing constructive notice

  Scenario: Consumer takes no reasonably necessary steps
    Given the consumer has not taken steps reasonably necessary
      to provide the institution with pertinent information
    When the platform evaluates whether notice was given
    Then notice is not recognized solely on that basis
```

**Implementation note:** The platform should maintain separate fields for:

- Consumer notification date.
- Notification method.
- Date notice was mailed or delivered for transmission, when applicable.
- Constructive notice date, when applicable.
- Pertinent information provided.
- Evidence supporting the notification date.

The actual receipt date by an employee or agent must not automatically override the regulatory notice date.

## **US-07: Apply lower liability limits under state law or agreement**

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.6(b)(6)

**User story**

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to apply a lower consumer liability amount when state law or an agreement between the consumer and financial institution imposes less liability than Regulation E so that the consumer is not assessed an amount exceeding the lower applicable limit.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Apply lower liability under state law or agreement

  Scenario: State law imposes lower consumer liability
    Given the platform has calculated consumer liability
      under Section 1005.6
    And applicable state law imposes a lower liability amount
    When the platform determines final consumer liability
    Then final consumer liability must not exceed
      the amount imposed under state law

  Scenario: Consumer agreement imposes lower liability
    Given the platform has calculated consumer liability
      under Section 1005.6
    And an agreement between the consumer and financial institution
      imposes a lower liability amount
    When the platform determines final consumer liability
    Then final consumer liability must not exceed
      the amount imposed under the agreement

  Scenario: State law and agreement do not impose lower liability
    Given the platform has calculated consumer liability
      under Section 1005.6
    And neither applicable state law nor the agreement
      imposes a lower liability amount
    When the platform determines final consumer liability
    Then the liability calculated under Section 1005.6 applies
```

**Business calculation:**

\\[ L\_{\text{final}} = \min(L\_{\text{Reg E}}, L\_{\text{lower applicable}}) \\]

Where the lower applicable liability amount is determined by state law or agreement, if applicable.

## **US-08: Determine final consumer liability across applicable provisions**

**Regulatory reference:** § 1005.6(a) and (b)(1)–(6)

**User story**

As a disputes analyst, I want the platform to apply the applicable Regulation E liability provisions and any lower liability imposed by state law or agreement so that the final consumer liability amount reflects the applicable regulatory limitations.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Determine final consumer liability

  Scenario: Consumer is not eligible for liability
    Given the financial institution did not satisfy
      the required disclosure conditions
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then consumer liability under Section 1005.6 is $0

  Scenario: Consumer is eligible for liability
    Given all applicable conditions under Section 1005.6(a)
      have been satisfied
    When the platform evaluates consumer liability
    Then the applicable liability provisions under
      Section 1005.6(b) are evaluated

  Scenario: Timely notice applies
    Given the consumer notified the institution
      within two business days after learning of loss or theft
      of the access device
    When the platform determines consumer liability
    Then the two-business-day timely notice limitation applies

  Scenario: Untimely notice applies
    Given the consumer failed to notify the institution
      within two business days after learning of loss or theft
      of the access device
    When the platform determines consumer liability
    Then the two-business-day untimely notice limitation applies

  Scenario: Periodic statement liability also applies
    Given an unauthorized transfer appeared on a periodic statement
    And the consumer failed to report it within 60 days
      of statement transmittal
    When the platform determines consumer liability
    Then the platform evaluates liability for qualifying
      subsequent transfers under Section 1005.6(b)(3)

  Scenario: Lower liability limit applies
    Given consumer liability has been calculated
      under the applicable Regulation E provisions
    And state law or an agreement imposes a lower liability amount
    When the platform determines final consumer liability
    Then final liability does not exceed the lower amount

  Scenario: Extenuating circumstances apply
    Given the consumer's notification delay was due
      to extenuating circumstances
    When the platform determines consumer liability
    Then the applicable notification time limits are extended
      to a reasonable period
    And the liability determination uses the extended period
```

# **6. Business rules and calculation examples**

The following examples demonstrate the calculations defined in § 1005.6. They assume the eligibility conditions are satisfied and no lower state-law or contractual liability limit applies.

| Scenario                                                                          | Calculation                     | Maximum liability |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Timely notice; $30 before notice                                                  | Lesser of $50 or $30            | $30               |
| Timely notice; $200 before notice                                                 | Lesser of $50 or $200           | $50               |
| Late notice; $40 within two days; $300 qualifying subsequent                      | Lesser of $500 or ($40 + $300)  | $340              |
| Late notice; $100 within two days; $600 qualifying subsequent                     | Lesser of $500 or ($50 + $600)  | $500              |
| Late notice; $40 within two days; $300 subsequent, not established by institution | Lesser of $500 or $40           | $40               |
| 60-day failure; $250 qualifying subsequent transfers                              | Qualifying subsequent transfers | $250              |

## **7. Key business analyst considerations and regulatory boundaries**

To keep the implementation aligned with the supplied regulatory text, the following distinctions are important.

1. **Two-business-day and 60-day periods are different clocks.** The two-business-day period begins when the consumer learns of the loss or theft of the access device. The 60-day period begins with the financial institution's transmittal of the periodic statement.
2. **Institution-established causation is a required condition.** For subsequent transfers under paragraphs (b)(2)(ii) and (b)(3), the institution must establish that the transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified the institution within the applicable period.
3. **The $500 cap applies to paragraph (b)(2).** It should not automatically be applied to paragraph (b)(3), which specifies a different liability limitation.
4. **Access-device involvement matters.** Paragraph (b)(3) expressly preserves the possibility of liability under paragraphs (b)(1) or (b)(2), as applicable.
5. **Notice is not dependent solely on actual employee receipt.** The platform must recognize notice when the consumer takes steps reasonably necessary to provide pertinent information, consistent with paragraph (b)(5).
6. **Extenuating circumstances require an extension.** The institution shall extend the applicable time limits to a reasonable period. The supplied text does not define a fixed extension duration.
7. **Lower liability takes precedence.** State law or an agreement imposing less liability limits the consumer's liability accordingly.
8. **Related unauthorized transfers must be considered.** Paragraph (b) applies to an unauthorized EFT or a series of related unauthorized transfers.
9. **Do not infer additional regulatory requirements.** Evidence retention periods, specific approval workflows, dispute resolution deadlines, provisional credit, investigation timelines, and customer communication requirements are outside the supplied text and should be specified separately if needed.

## **9. Definition of done**

The implementation is complete when:

- All eight user stories have passed their Gherkin acceptance criteria.
- The platform correctly determines eligibility under § 1005.6(a).
- The platform correctly calculates the applicable two-business-day liability limitations.
- The platform correctly calculates periodic statement liability.
- Institution-established causation is considered for subsequent transfers.
- All notification methods and applicable notice dates are supported.
- Reasonable extensions for extenuating circumstances are supported.
- Lower liability limits under state law or agreement are applied.
- The final liability calculation is reproducible from the underlying case data.
- Boundary-value scenarios, including $0, $50, $500, and amounts exceeding the applicable caps, have been tested.

**BA recommendation:** Implement the liability calculation as a centralized, configurable rules engine rather than embedding separate calculations in individual dispute workflows. This will promote consistent liability determinations across dispute channels and make future regulatory changes easier to implement.
