### Epic: Regulation E Unauthorized Electronic Fund Transfer (EFT) Liability Engine

---

### Story 1: Validate Prerequisites for Holding a Consumer Liable (§ 1005.6(a))

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Engine

**I want to** verify whether the regulatory prerequisites for consumer liability have been met

**So that** the bank does not hold a consumer liable for unauthorized EFTs when required disclosures or access device conditions are missing.

```gherkin
Feature: Prerequisites for Consumer Liability (§ 1005.6(a))

  Scenario: Required § 1005.7(b)(1)-(3) disclosures were not provided
    Given an unauthorized EFT claim has been submitted on a consumer account
    And the bank has not provided the disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the consumer's maximum liability must be set to $0.00
    And the liability evaluation must terminate without applying § 1005.6(b) tiers

  Scenario: Access device was not an accepted access device
    Given an unauthorized EFT claim involves an access device
    And the bank has provided the disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    And the access device is not an accepted access device
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the consumer's maximum liability must be set to $0.00

  Scenario: Bank failed to provide a means to identify the consumer
    Given an unauthorized EFT claim involves an accepted access device
    And the bank has provided the disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    And the bank did not provide a means to identify the consumer to whom the access device was issued
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the consumer's maximum liability must be set to $0.00

  Scenario: All prerequisites are met for an access device transfer
    Given an unauthorized EFT claim involves an accepted access device
    And the bank has provided the disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    And the bank provided a means to identify the consumer to whom the access device was issued
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the claim proceeds to liability tier calculation under § 1005.6(b)

  Scenario: All prerequisites are met for a non-access device transfer
    Given an unauthorized EFT claim does not involve an access device
    And the bank has provided the disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3)
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates the claim
    Then the claim proceeds to periodic statement liability evaluation under § 1005.6(b)(3)

```

### Story 2: Determine Effective Notice Timestamp and Channel (§ 1005.6(b)(5))

**As a** Disputes Intake & Case Management Module

**I want to** capture and normalize the consumer's notice timestamp based on the communication channel or constructive notice triggers

**So that** reporting deadlines (2-business-day and 60-day windows) are measured accurately against the legally effective notice time.

```gherkin
Feature: Notice Receipt and Effective Timestamp (§ 1005.6(b)(5))

  Scenario: Consumer provides notice in person or by telephone
    Given a consumer takes steps reasonably necessary to provide pertinent information regarding an unauthorized transfer or lost/stolen access device
    And the notice is delivered in person or by telephone
    When the dispute case is logged
    Then the effective notice timestamp is recorded as the time the consumer provided the information, regardless of whether a specific employee or agent actually received it

  Scenario: Consumer provides notice in writing via mail or usual transmission
    Given a consumer sends written notice with pertinent information regarding an unauthorized transfer or lost/stolen access device
    When the dispute case is logged
    Then the effective notice timestamp is recorded as the time the consumer mailed the notice or delivered it for transmission by any other usual means

  Scenario: Bank receives constructive notice of potential unauthorized activity
    Given the bank becomes aware of circumstances leading to a reasonable belief that an unauthorized transfer to or from the consumer's account has been or may be made
    And the consumer has not yet contacted the bank
    When the bank's monitoring or operational systems log this awareness
    Then the effective notice timestamp may be recorded as the time the bank became aware of those circumstances

```

### Story 3: Apply Extenuating Circumstances Time Extensions (§ 1005.6(b)(4))

**As a** Disputes Specialist

**I want to** record extenuating circumstances that delayed a consumer's notice and extend the regulatory reporting windows to a reasonable period

**So that** consumers are not penalized for reporting delays outside their control.

```gherkin
Feature: Extension of Time Limits for Extenuating Circumstances (§ 1005.6(b)(4))

  Scenario: Consumer delay is due to extenuating circumstances
    Given a consumer reported a lost/stolen access device after 2 business days or an unauthorized transfer on a periodic statement after 60 days
    And the Disputes Specialist documents that the delay was due to extenuating circumstances
    When the specialist specifies an extended reasonable deadline date
    Then the system must evaluate timeliness against the extended reasonable period instead of the standard 2-business-day or 60-day cutoff
    And if the effective notice timestamp falls within the extended reasonable period, the notice must be treated as timely

  Scenario: No extenuating circumstances exist
    Given a consumer reported a lost/stolen access device or unauthorized transfer
    And no extenuating circumstances are documented
    When the liability calculation engine evaluates timeliness
    Then the system must enforce the strict 2-business-day and 60-day statutory windows

```

### Story 4: Calculate Liability for Access Device Loss/Theft with Timely Notice (§ 1005.6(b)(1))

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Engine

**I want to** cap consumer liability at the lesser of $50 or the pre-notice unauthorized transfer amount when notice is given within 2 business days

**So that** timely reporters receive the statutory $50 liability cap.

```gherkin
Feature: Timely Notice of Lost or Stolen Access Device (§ 1005.6(b)(1))

  Scenario: Timely notice given and unauthorized transfers exceed $50
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability prerequisites are met for an access device
    And the consumer notifies the bank within 2 business days (or extended reasonable period) after learning of the loss or theft of the access device
    And unauthorized transfers occurring before the effective notice timestamp total $120.00
    When the liability calculation engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer's liability must be $50.00

  Scenario: Timely notice given and unauthorized transfers are less than $50
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability prerequisites are met for an access device
    And the consumer notifies the bank within 2 business days (or extended reasonable period) after learning of the loss or theft of the access device
    And unauthorized transfers occurring before the effective notice timestamp total $35.00
    When the liability calculation engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer's liability must be $35.00

  Scenario: Timely notice given before any unauthorized transfers occur
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability prerequisites are met for an access device
    And the consumer notifies the bank within 2 business days (or extended reasonable period) after learning of the loss or theft
    And $0.00 in unauthorized transfers occurred before the effective notice timestamp
    When the liability calculation engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer's liability must be $0.00

```

### Story 5: Calculate Liability for Access Device Loss/Theft Without Timely Notice (§ 1005.6(b)(2))

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Engine

**I want to** calculate consumer liability up to a $500 cap when a consumer fails to notify the bank within 2 business days of learning of a lost or stolen access device

**So that** the tiered liability formula and bank preventability burden are accurately applied.

```gherkin
Feature: Untimely Notice of Lost or Stolen Access Device (§ 1005.6(b)(2))

  Scenario: Untimely notice with transfers in both windows and bank establishes preventability (capped at $500)
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability prerequisites are met for an access device
    And the consumer fails to notify the bank within 2 business days (and no extenuating circumstances extend the window) after learning of the loss or theft
    And unauthorized transfers within the first 2 business days total $80.00
    And unauthorized transfers after the close of 2 business days and before effective notice total $600.00
    And the bank establishes that the $600.00 in transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified the bank within the 2-business-day period
    When the liability calculation engine calculates consumer liability
    Then Tier 1 liability (first 2 business days) is calculated as the lesser of $50.00 or $80.00 ($50.00)
    And Tier 2 liability (after 2 business days, before notice) is $600.00
    And total combined liability ($650.00) is capped at the lesser of $500.00 or $650.00, resulting in $500.00 consumer liability

  Scenario: Untimely notice with total calculated liability under $500
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability prerequisites are met for an access device
    And the consumer fails to notify the bank within 2 business days after learning of the loss or theft
    And unauthorized transfers within the first 2 business days total $30.00
    And unauthorized transfers after the close of 2 business days and before effective notice total $200.00
    And the bank establishes that the $200.00 in transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified the bank within the 2-business-day period
    When the liability calculation engine calculates consumer liability
    Then Tier 1 liability is $30.00 (lesser of $50.00 or $30.00)
    And Tier 2 liability is $200.00
    And total consumer liability is $230.00

  Scenario: Bank fails to establish that post-2-day transfers would have been prevented
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability prerequisites are met for an access device
    And the consumer fails to notify the bank within 2 business days after learning of the loss or theft
    And unauthorized transfers within the first 2 business days total $100.00
    And unauthorized transfers after the close of 2 business days and before effective notice total $400.00
    And the bank does not establish that the $400.00 in transfers would have been prevented had timely notice been given
    When the liability calculation engine calculates consumer liability
    Then Tier 2 liability is $0.00
    And total consumer liability is $50.00 (Tier 1 lesser of $50.00 or $100.00)

```

### Story 6: Calculate Liability for Failure to Report Unauthorized Transfers on a Periodic Statement (§ 1005.6(b)(3))

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Engine

**I want to** assess unlimited liability for preventable transfers occurring more than 60 days after periodic statement transmittal (plus any applicable access device liability)

**So that** the 60-day periodic statement rule is enforced for both access-device and non-access-device transfers.

```gherkin
Feature: Periodic Statement 60-Day Reporting Window (§ 1005.6(b)(3))

  Scenario: Consumer reports unauthorized transfer within 60 days of statement transmittal (no access device)
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability prerequisites are met
    And an unauthorized EFT not involving an access device appears on a transmitted periodic statement
    And the consumer notifies the bank within 60 days (or extended reasonable period) of the statement transmittal date
    When the liability calculation engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer's liability must be $0.00

  Scenario: Consumer fails to report within 60 days and bank establishes preventability (no access device)
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability prerequisites are met
    And an unauthorized EFT of $150.00 not involving an access device appears on a transmitted periodic statement
    And the consumer notifies the bank 75 days after statement transmittal (with no extenuating circumstances)
    And additional unauthorized transfers totaling $1,200.00 occurred after the close of the 60-day period and before effective notice
    And the bank establishes that the $1,200.00 in transfers would not have occurred had the consumer notified the bank within the 60-day period
    When the liability calculation engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer's liability for the initial $150.00 transfer is $0.00
    And the consumer's liability for the post-60-day preventable transfers is $1,200.00
    And total consumer liability is $1,200.00

  Scenario: Consumer fails to report within 60 days but bank cannot establish preventability
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability prerequisites are met
    And an unauthorized EFT not involving an access device appears on a transmitted periodic statement
    And the consumer notifies the bank after 60 days of statement transmittal
    And additional unauthorized transfers totaling $900.00 occurred after the close of the 60-day period and before effective notice
    And the bank does not establish that the $900.00 in transfers would not have occurred had timely notice been given
    When the liability calculation engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer's liability must be $0.00

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers involve both a lost/stolen access device and failure to report within 60 days
    Given all § 1005.6(a) liability prerequisites are met for an access device
    And the consumer fails to report a lost/stolen access device within 2 business days of learning of the loss/theft
    And the consumer also fails to report an unauthorized transfer appearing on a periodic statement within 60 days of transmittal
    And $500.00 in preventable unauthorized transfers occur before the close of the 60-day period (subject to § 1005.6(b)(2))
    And $800.00 in preventable unauthorized transfers occur after the close of the 60-day period and before effective notice
    When the liability calculation engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the consumer's liability includes the § 1005.6(b)(2) amount ($500.00) plus the § 1005.6(b)(3) post-60-day preventable amount ($800.00)
    And total consumer liability is $1,300.00

```

### Story 7: Override Statutory Liability with Lower State Law or Account Agreement Limits (§ 1005.6(b)(6))

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Engine

**I want to** compare the calculated Regulation E liability against applicable state law and account agreement liability limits and apply the lowest amount

**So that** the bank honors stricter consumer protections required by state law or contract (e.g., Zero Liability cardholder agreements).

```gherkin
Feature: State Law or Account Agreement Liability Override (§ 1005.6(b)(6))

  Scenario: Account agreement imposes lower liability than Regulation E (e.g., $0 Zero Liability)
    Given the Regulation E calculation under § 1005.6(b)(1)-(3) yields a consumer liability of $50.00
    And the consumer's account agreement imposes a maximum liability of $0.00 for the unauthorized transfer
    And applicable state law imposes a maximum liability of $50.00
    When the final liability determination runs
    Then the consumer's final liability must be capped at $0.00

  Scenario: State law imposes lower liability than Regulation E and account agreement
    Given the Regulation E calculation under § 1005.6(b)(1)-(3) yields a consumer liability of $500.00
    And the consumer's account agreement imposes a maximum liability of $500.00
    And applicable state law imposes a maximum liability of $50.00
    When the final liability determination runs
    Then the consumer's final liability must be capped at $50.00

  Scenario: Regulation E imposes lower liability than state law and account agreement
    Given the Regulation E calculation under § 1005.6(b)(1)-(3) yields a consumer liability of $50.00
    And applicable state law imposes a maximum liability of $100.00
    And the consumer's account agreement imposes a maximum liability of $500.00
    When the final liability determination runs
    Then the consumer's final liability remains $50.00

```
