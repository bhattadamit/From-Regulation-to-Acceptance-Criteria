To implement Regulation E consumer liability tiers for unauthorized electronic fund transfers (12 CFR § 1005.6) in the disputes platform, the calculation engine must evaluate two independent branches: **Lost or Stolen Access Devices** (which triggers the 2-business-day and 60-day tiers) and **Unauthorized Transfers Not Involving a Lost/Stolen Access Device** (e.g., compromised credentials, skimming, or unauthorized ACH/debit pulls, which triggers only the 60-day statement reporting rule).

### Epic: Regulation E Consumer Liability Calculation Engine (12 CFR 1005.6)

#### Story 1: Evaluate Timeliness of Notification for Lost or Stolen Access Devices

**As a** Disputes Adjudication Engine,

**I want** to calculate the consumer's maximum liability when a physical access device (debit card) is lost or stolen based on the elapsed business days between learning of the loss/theft and notifying the bank,

**So that** the bank accurately enforces the $50 and $500 liability caps under 12 CFR § 1005.6(b)(1)–(2).

**Acceptance Criteria:**

```gherkin
Feature: Lost or Stolen Access Device Liability Tiers (2-Business-Day Rule)

  Background:
    Given a dispute claim is filed for unauthorized Electronic Fund Transfers (EFTs)
    And the claim involves a lost or stolen physical access device
    And the periodic statement 60-day reporting window has not been breached

  Scenario: Consumer notifies the bank within 2 business days of learning of loss or theft
    Given the consumer learned of the loss or theft on "2026-11-02" (Monday)
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-11-04" (Wednesday)
    And total unauthorized EFTs prior to notification equal "$850.00"
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the elapsed time is evaluated as 2 business days
    And the maximum consumer liability is capped at the lesser of "$50.00" or the amount of unauthorized transfers
    And the calculated consumer liability equals "$50.00"
    And the bank provisional/final credit obligation equals "$800.00"

  Scenario: Consumer notifies the bank after 2 business days (within 60 days of statement)
    Given the consumer learned of the loss or theft on "2026-11-02" (Monday)
    And the consumer notified the bank on "2026-11-06" (Friday)
    And unauthorized transfers occurring within the first 2 business days equal "$40.00"
    And unauthorized transfers occurring after the 2nd business day and before notice equal "$600.00"
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the elapsed time is evaluated as 4 business days
    And the Tier 1 liability (first 2 business days) is capped at "$40.00" (lesser of $50 or actual transfers)
    And the remaining available cap up to "$500.00" is "$460.00"
    And the Tier 2 liability (after 2 business days) is capped at "$460.00"
    And the total calculated consumer liability equals "$500.00"
    And the bank credit obligation equals "$140.00"

  Scenario: Total unauthorized transfers are less than the $500 cap after 2 business days
    Given the consumer notified the bank 5 business days after learning of the theft
    And total unauthorized transfers prior to notification equal "$320.00"
    When the liability engine calculates consumer liability
    Then the calculated consumer liability equals "$320.00"
    And the bank credit obligation equals "$0.00"

```

### Implementation & Edge-Case Notes for Engineering

* **Business Day Calendar:** The 2-business-day calculation in Story 1 must exclude weekends and Federal Reserve holidays, and cut off at the bank's established business-day close (or midnight if no cutoff is specified in disclosures).
* **Card Network Zero Liability vs. Reg E:** Many US banks apply Visa/Mastercard Zero Liability policies (`$0` consumer liability) as a commercial overlay. Ensure the engine calculates and stores the **Statutory Reg E Liability** separately from the **Bank Policy Waiver** so compliance audit reports accurately reflect statutory adherence while customer-facing notices reflect the `$0` network benefit when applicable.
* **Burden of Proof (12 CFR § 1005.6(b)(5)(ii)):** Notice to the bank is effective when the consumer takes steps reasonably necessary to provide the bank with pertinent information, even if a specific bank employee fails to log it immediately—ensure the intake UI captures a manual `"Date/Time Consumer First Attempted Notice"` field distinct from `"Case Creation Timestamp"`.
