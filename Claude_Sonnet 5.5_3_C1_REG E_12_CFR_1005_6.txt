# Reg E Consumer Liability (12 CFR 1005.6): User Stories and Gherkin Acceptance Criteria

**Prepared by:** Business Analysis | **Status:** Draft for Compliance/Legal review | **Date:** Nov 20, 2026

## 1. Scope and regulatory basis

**In scope:** The liability determination for EFTs already classified as unauthorized on consumer accounts (debit, ACH, online/mobile, and prepaid if hosted on this platform).

**Out of scope, but a dependency:**
- Error-resolution timelines and provisional credit (1005.11)
- The authorized vs. unauthorized classification (1005.2(m))
- Credit card disputes (Reg Z)

**Rules the stories implement** (paraphrased from the regulation and CFPB commentary):
- Liability has three tiers: up to $50, up to $500, or unlimited, depending on when the unauthorized EFT occurs.
- The bank may impose liability only if it provided the required 1005.7(b) disclosures.
- To avoid liability for later transfers, the consumer must report an unauthorized EFT that appears on a statement within 60 days of the bank's transmittal of that statement.
- The first two tiers do not apply to unauthorized transfers made without an access device.
- Consumer negligence cannot be used to impose greater liability than Reg E allows.
- If state law or the customer agreement imposes less liability, the lesser amount governs.
- An unauthorized EFT includes a transfer initiated by someone who obtained the access device from the consumer through fraud or robbery.
- For prepaid accounts, the 60-day period runs from the earlier of the statement being sent or the first electronic access/history reflecting the transfer. (This comes from 1005.18(c)(3), not 1005.6.)

**Assumptions (confirm with Compliance before sprint planning):**
1. "Business day" follows the bank's own definition under 1005.2(d), meaning days it is open for substantially all business functions. This must be a configurable calendar, not a hard-coded Mon–Fri.
2. The two-business-day clock starts the day after the consumer learns of the loss or theft. CFPB's example has a consumer learning on Wednesday with a deadline at midnight Friday.
3. The "learned date" is captured from the consumer. It is never inferred from the statement date, because statement receipt is evidence but not conclusive proof of knowledge.
4. Liability amounts are *maximums*. The bank may choose to charge less or nothing (see US-07).

### US-01: Verify conditions precedent to liability (1005.6(a))
**As a** Disputes Analyst
**I want** the platform to confirm the bank is legally allowed to impose any liability
**So that** we never charge a consumer when disclosure or access-device conditions weren't met.

```gherkin
Feature: Conditions for imposing consumer liability

  Background:
    Given an EFT case is classified "Unauthorized"
    And the account is a consumer account subject to Regulation E

  Scenario: All conditions met
    Given the required 1005.7(b) disclosures were delivered before the transfer
    And the case involves an access device
    And the access device was an accepted access device
    And the bank provided a means to identify the consumer (PIN, signature, biometric, or similar)
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the case is marked "Liability permitted"
    And processing continues to tier calculation

  Scenario Outline: A failed condition caps liability at zero
    Given the case involves "<transfer_type>"
    And the condition "<failed_condition>" is not satisfied
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the consumer liability amount is $0.00
    And the reason "<reason_code>" is recorded
    And the case cannot be manually overridden to a non-zero amount

    Examples:
      | transfer_type | failed_condition                    | reason_code        |
      | access device | disclosures not provided            | NO_DISCLOSURE      |
      | non-device    | disclosures not provided            | NO_DISCLOSURE      |
      | access device | device not accepted by consumer     | DEVICE_NOT_ACCEPTED|
      | access device | no means to identify the consumer   | NO_IDENTIFICATION  |

  Scenario: Missing disclosure evidence
    Given the platform cannot retrieve a disclosure delivery record for the account
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the case is routed to "Compliance Review"
    And no liability amount is calculated until the record is confirmed or waived
```

### US-02: Record notice date and channel (1005.6(b)(5))
**As a** Disputes Analyst
**I want** to capture when and how the consumer notified the bank
**So that** the notice date used in liability calculations is accurate and defensible.

```gherkin
Feature: Notice of loss, theft, or unauthorized transfer

  Scenario Outline: Notice accepted through any reasonable channel
    When the consumer reports an unauthorized transfer via "<channel>"
    Then the platform records the notice timestamp as the time the consumer took reasonable steps to give the information
    And the notice is valid whether or not a specific employee received it

    Examples:
      | channel                              |
      | in person at a branch                |
      | telephone to the contact center      |
      | written letter                       |
      | secure message or chat               |
      | call to a number other than the designated line, if reasonable |

  Scenario: Written notice date
    Given the consumer mailed a written notice on "2026-11-10"
    And the bank received it on "2026-11-16"
    When the notice date is recorded
    Then the notice date is "2026-11-10" (date mailed or delivered for transmission)

  Scenario: Notice without account or card number
    Given the consumer cannot supply an account or card number
    But provides name and other information sufficient to identify the account
    When the notice is logged
    Then the notice is treated as valid for limiting liability

  Scenario: Constructive notice
    Given the bank's fraud system flags suspected unauthorized activity on the account on "2026-11-12"
    And the consumer has not yet contacted the bank
    When the liability determination runs
    Then the notice date is the earlier of the consumer's contact date and "2026-11-12"
    And the basis "CONSTRUCTIVE_NOTICE" is recorded

  Scenario: Learned date is captured separately from statement date
    Given a statement showing the unauthorized transfer was transmitted on "2026-10-01"
    When the analyst records the consumer's learned date
    Then the field requires explicit consumer-provided input
    And the system does not default it to the statement date
```

### US-03: Timely notice, $50 maximum liability (1005.6(b)(1))
**As a** Compliance Officer
**I want** the platform to cap liability at the lesser of $50 or the unauthorized amount when the consumer reports within two business days of learning
**So that** we meet the baseline Reg E limit.

```gherkin
Feature: Tier 1 liability, timely notice

  Background:
    Given the bank's business days are Monday through Friday with no holidays in the period
    And the case involves a lost or stolen accepted access device
    And all conditions precedent in US-01 are met
    And no state-law or agreement limit applies

  Scenario Outline: Lesser of $50 or amount of unauthorized transfers before notice
    Given the consumer learned of the loss on Monday
    And the consumer gave notice on Tuesday
    And unauthorized transfers before notice total <unauth_total>
    When liability is calculated
    Then the consumer liability is <liability>
    And the tier "TIER_1_TIMELY" is recorded

    Examples:
      | unauth_total | liability |
      | $30.00       | $30.00    |
      | $50.00       | $50.00    |
      | $200.00      | $50.00    |
      | $2,000.00    | $50.00    |

  Scenario: Notice on the last day of the window
    Given the consumer learned of the theft on Wednesday
    And the consumer gave notice at 11:55 PM on Friday
    When liability is calculated
    Then timely notice is determined as met
    And the tier "TIER_1_TIMELY" applies
```

### US-04: Untimely notice, $500 maximum (1005.6(b)(2))
**As a** Compliance Officer
**I want** the platform to compute the second tier only when the bank can establish later transfers would have been prevented by timely notice
**So that** we don't over-assess consumers.

```gherkin
Feature: Tier 2 liability, notice after two business days

  Background:
    Given the bank's business days are Monday through Friday
    And the case involves a lost or stolen accepted access device
    And all conditions precedent in US-01 are met

  Scenario: Cap applies, matches CFPB commentary example
    Given the consumer learned of the theft on Monday
    And the consumer gave notice on Friday
    And an unauthorized transfer of $100.00 occurred on Tuesday
    And an unauthorized transfer of $600.00 occurred on Thursday
    And the analyst confirms the Thursday transfer would not have occurred with timely notice
    When liability is calculated
    Then component A (lesser of $50 or transfers within two business days) is $50.00
    And component B (transfers after two business days and before notice) is $600.00
    And the sum $650.00 exceeds the cap
    And the consumer liability is $500.00
    And the tier "TIER_2_UNTIMELY" is recorded

  Scenario: Sum below the cap
    Given the consumer learned of the theft on Monday
    And the consumer gave notice on Friday
    And an unauthorized transfer of $20.00 occurred on Tuesday
    And an unauthorized transfer of $100.00 occurred on Thursday
    And the analyst confirms the Thursday transfer would not have occurred with timely notice
    When liability is calculated
    Then the consumer liability is $120.00

  Scenario: Bank cannot establish causation
    Given the consumer gave notice after the two-business-day window
    And a later transfer of $600.00 occurred after the window and before notice
    And the analyst selects "Cannot establish transfer would have been prevented"
    When liability is calculated
    Then component B is $0.00
    And the consumer liability is limited to component A
    And the analyst's rationale is mandatory and stored with the case

  Scenario: Causation cannot be left blank
    Given the case has transfers after the two-business-day window
    When the analyst attempts to finalize without a causation determination
    Then the system blocks finalization
    And displays "Causation determination required for post-window transfers"
```

### US-05: Periodic statement rule and unlimited liability (1005.6(b)(3))
**As a** Compliance Officer
**I want** the 60-day statement window evaluated for both device and non-device transfers
**So that** unlimited liability is imposed only where Reg E allows it.

```gherkin
Feature: Tier 3 liability, 60-day periodic statement window

  Background:
    Given all conditions precedent in US-01 are met

  Scenario: Consumer reports within 60 days of statement transmittal
    Given a statement showing an unauthorized transfer was transmitted on "2026-09-01"
    And the consumer gave notice on "2026-10-15"
    When liability is calculated for a transfer made without an access device
    Then the consumer liability is $0.00

  Scenario: Non-device transfer, notice after 60 days (CFPB example pattern)
    Given an unauthorized ACH debit of $200.00 appeared on a statement transmitted on "2026-01-31"
    And a further unauthorized debit of $400.00 posted on day 61 after transmittal
    And the consumer gave notice on day 62
    And the analyst confirms the $400.00 debit would not have occurred had notice been given within 60 days
    When liability is calculated
    Then the consumer liability is $400.00
    And the $200.00 debit is not charged to the consumer
    And the tier "TIER_3_UNLIMITED" is recorded

  Scenario: Tiers 1 and 2 never apply to non-device transfers
    Given the unauthorized transfer was made without an access device
    When liability is calculated
    Then no amount is computed under Tier 1 or Tier 2
    And liability can arise only under the 60-day rule

  Scenario: Device-involved case combines tiers
    Given a stolen debit card was used for transfers before and after day 60 following statement transmittal
    When liability is calculated
    Then transfers within the 60-day window are assessed under Tier 1 or Tier 2 per notice timing
    And transfers after the 60-day window and before notice are assessed under Tier 3 if causation is established
    And the total is the sum of the components
    And a per-component breakdown is stored

  Scenario: Post-60-day transfer, causation not established
    Given a transfer occurred after the 60-day window
    And the analyst selects "Cannot establish transfer would have been prevented"
    When liability is calculated
    Then that transfer contributes $0.00 to consumer liability
```

### US-06: Extension for extenuating circumstances (1005.6(b)(4))
**As a** Disputes Analyst
**I want** to apply a reasonable extension to notice deadlines when the consumer was delayed by extenuating circumstances
**So that** the consumer is not penalized for events like hospitalization or extended travel.

```gherkin
Feature: Extension of notice deadlines

  Scenario Outline: Extenuating circumstance extends the applicable deadline
    Given the consumer's notice was late for the "<deadline>"
    And the consumer reports the circumstance "<circumstance>"
    When the analyst records the circumstance with supporting details
    Then the platform extends the deadline by a reasonable period entered by the analyst
    And liability is recalculated using the extended deadline
    And the extension reason and duration are stored in the audit trail

    Examples:
      | deadline            | circumstance                         |
      | two-business-day    | extended travel                      |
      | 60-day statement    | hospitalization                      |

  Scenario: Extension requires a documented reason
    When the analyst applies an extension without selecting a circumstance
    Then the system blocks the extension
    And displays "Extenuating circumstance and rationale required"

  Scenario: Extension applied to Compliance-required review
    Given an extension exceeds the configured review threshold of 30 days
    When the analyst saves the extension
    Then the case is routed to a Team Lead for approval
```

### US-07: Apply lesser liability under state law or customer agreement (1005.6(b)(6))
**As a** Compliance Officer
**I want** the platform to automatically use the lowest applicable liability limit
**So that** state-law protections and our own cardholder agreement terms are honored.

```gherkin
Feature: Lesser-liability override

  Scenario: Agreement imposes lower liability
    Given the account's customer agreement provides zero liability for unauthorized card transactions
    And the Reg E calculation yields $50.00
    When the final liability is determined
    Then the final consumer liability is $0.00
    And the basis "AGREEMENT_ZERO_LIABILITY" is recorded

  Scenario: State law imposes a lower cap
    Given the consumer's state of residence has a statutory cap of $25.00 for the transfer type
    And the Reg E calculation yields $50.00
    When the final liability is determined
    Then the final consumer liability is $25.00
    And the basis "STATE_LAW_CAP" is recorded with the statute reference

  Scenario: Reg E is lower than the agreement
    Given the customer agreement states a higher limit than Reg E allows
    When the final liability is determined
    Then the Reg E amount is used
    And the system never applies a limit above the Reg E result

  Scenario: Liability matrix is maintained under change control
    When Compliance updates a state or agreement limit
    Then the change requires approval by a second authorized user
    And takes effect on a specified effective date
    And prior determinations are not retroactively changed
```

### US-08: Aggregate related transfers as a series (1005.6(b))
**As a** Disputes Analyst
**I want** related unauthorized transfers grouped into one series
**So that** liability caps apply once per series, not once per transaction.

```gherkin
Feature: Series of related unauthorized transfers

  Scenario: Multiple transfers from one loss event
    Given three unauthorized transfers resulted from the same stolen card
    When the analyst groups them into one series
    Then the liability caps are applied once to the series
    And the calculation uses the combined amounts by time bucket

  Scenario: Unrelated events are not combined
    Given two unauthorized transfers resulted from separate events (a stolen card and a compromised account number)
    When the analyst saves them as separate series
    Then each series has its own notice date, tier calculation, and liability amount

  Scenario: Regrouping triggers recalculation
    Given a finalized liability determination exists
    When the analyst moves a transfer from one series to another
    Then both series are recalculated
    And the earlier result is retained as a superseded version
```

## 3. Non-functional requirements
- **Auditability:** Every calculation is reproducible from stored inputs and the stored rule-set version.
- **Determinism:** Identical inputs always give identical output (a regression suite built from the Gherkin examples above).
- **Access control:** Only Compliance can edit parameters, calendars, and the state-law matrix.
- **Retention:** Decision records follow the bank's Reg E record retention policy (at least 2 years under 1005.13, or longer by policy).
- **Performance:** The calculation returns in under 2 seconds for a series of up to 500 transfers.

## 4. Open questions for Compliance/Legal
1. **Day-of-learning treatment:** How should transfers on the learned date, or before the consumer learned of the loss, be bucketed in the two-day component? This design counts them in Tier 1's pool, which needs sign-off.
2. **Scam and fraudulently induced payments:** Confirm how the bank classifies transfers the consumer was tricked into authorizing, including P2P. These are a classification question under 1005.2(m) and are currently excluded from this engine.
3. **Network zero-liability programs:** Confirm which products are subject to them, as they feed US-07.
4. **Business-day definition:** Confirm the official branch-hours basis for the business-day calendar, including weekend and holiday handling.
5. **Prepaid products:** Confirm they are in scope, since they add the 1005.18 rules in US-09.
6. **Regulatory currency:** The cited CFR text on eCFR was current as of June 2026. Legal should confirm there have been no amendments or new CFPB guidance since then before build.
