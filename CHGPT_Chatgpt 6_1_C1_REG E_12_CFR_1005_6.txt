# **Business Requirements: Regulation E Consumer Liability for Unauthorized Transfers**

**Product:** Consumer Disputes Platform\
**Regulation:** Regulation E — 12 CFR § 1005.6, Consumer Liability\
**Business domain:** Deposit accounts / Electronic Funds Transfers (EFTs)\
**Primary users:** Disputes analysts, operations associates, compliance officers, and consumers\
**Objective:** Implement configurable, explainable, and auditable consumer liability calculations for unauthorized electronic fund transfers in accordance with Regulation E.

## **1. Business objective**

The disputes platform must determine the maximum consumer liability for unauthorized electronic fund transfers based on:

1. When the consumer became aware that an access device was lost or stolen.
2. When the consumer notified the financial institution.
3. When unauthorized transfers occurred.
4. When the financial institution transmitted the periodic statement showing the unauthorized transfer.
5. Whether the consumer notified the institution within the applicable regulatory timeframe.
6. Whether the financial institution can establish that transfers occurring after a notification deadline would have been prevented by timely notification.

The platform must apply the appropriate regulatory liability limits, calculate the consumer's maximum potential liability, and retain sufficient evidence to support the determination.

**Important implementation principle:** Consumer liability is not automatically equal to the disputed amount. The platform must calculate liability under the applicable provisions of § 1005.6 and must not increase liability based solely on consumer negligence.

## **2. Regulatory rules and calculation logic**

The following rules form the foundation of the implementation.

| Regulatory provision | Business rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| § 1005.6(a)          | Consumer liability is limited by the conditions and limits in § 1005.6(b).                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| § 1005.6(b)(1)       | If the consumer notifies the institution within two business days after learning of loss or theft of an access device, maximum liability is generally the lesser of $50 or unauthorized transfers occurring before notification.                                      |
| § 1005.6(b)(2)       | If notification occurs after two business days, maximum liability is generally the lesser of $500 or the sum of the applicable initial $50 liability and qualifying transfers occurring after the two-business-day period but before notification.                    |
| § 1005.6(b)(3)       | If the consumer fails to report an unauthorized transfer within 60 days after the institution transmits the statement showing the transfer, the consumer may be liable for qualifying subsequent transfers that would not have occurred had notification been timely. |
| § 1005.6(b)(4)       | Special rules apply to certain circumstances involving transfers initiated by means other than an access device.                                                                                                                                                      |
| § 1005.6(b)(5)       | The financial institution bears the burden of establishing the consumer's liability, including the consumer's failure to provide timely notification.                                                                                                                 |
| § 1005.6(c)          | Notification can be provided by telephone or in writing. The institution may require written confirmation following telephone notification, subject to regulatory conditions.                                                                                         |

### **2.1 Liability calculation scenarios**

**Scenario A — Notification within two business days**

\\[ L = \min(\\$50, T\_{pre}) \\]

Where:

- \\(L\\) = maximum consumer liability.
- \\(T\_{pre}\\) = qualifying unauthorized transfers before notification.

**Scenario B — Notification after two business days**

\\[ L = \min(\\$500, \min(\\$50,T\_{pre}) + T\_{late}) \\]

Where:

\- \\(T\_{late}\\) = qualifying unauthorized transfers occurring after the two-business-day period and before notification that would not have occurred if notification had been provided within two business days.

**Scenario C — Failure to notify within 60 days**

The platform must separately evaluate transfers occurring after expiration of the 60-day period and before notification.

Potential additional liability is limited to transfers that the institution establishes would not have occurred had the consumer notified the institution within the applicable 60-day period.

The calculation must not simply add all post-deadline transfers without considering causation and the institution's burden of proof.

## **3. User stories and Gherkin acceptance criteria**

### **EPIC 1 — Establish regulatory eligibility and liability assessment**

#### **US-001: Identify whether Regulation E liability rules apply**

**As a** disputes analyst,\
**I want** the platform to determine whether a disputed transaction is subject to Regulation E consumer liability rules,\
**So that** liability calculations are only performed when the regulatory requirements are applicable.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Regulation E applicability assessment

  Scenario: Eligible unauthorized EFT involving an access device
    Given a consumer disputes an electronic fund transfer
    And the transfer is classified as unauthorized
    And the transfer involves an accepted access device
    When the dispute is submitted
    Then the platform shall identify Regulation E
      as the applicable regulatory framework
    And the platform shall initiate a consumer liability assessment

  Scenario: Transaction is not classified as unauthorized
    Given a consumer disputes an electronic fund transfer
    And the transaction is determined to be authorized
    When the dispute is assessed
    Then the platform shall not calculate unauthorized
      transfer liability under Section 1005.6

  Scenario: Regulation E applicability cannot be established
    Given a consumer disputes an electronic fund transfer
    And required transaction or access device information is missing
    When the dispute is assessed
    Then the platform shall flag the assessment for review
    And the platform shall not assume the consumer is liable
```

**Business rules / data requirements**

- Transaction classification.
- EFT transaction date and amount.
- Access device identifier and type.
- Whether the access device was accepted by the institution.
- Whether the transaction is unauthorized.
- Regulatory applicability decision and reason code.

#### **US-002: Capture the date the consumer learned of loss or theft**

**As a** disputes analyst,\
**I want** the platform to capture when the consumer first learned that an access device was lost or stolen,\
**So that** the two-business-day notification deadline can be calculated accurately.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Capture consumer awareness date

  Scenario: Consumer provides date of discovery
    Given the consumer reports a lost or stolen access device
    When the analyst records the date the consumer
      first learned of the loss or theft
    Then the platform shall save the awareness date
    And the platform shall use this date as the starting
      point for the two-business-day calculation

  Scenario: Consumer does not know the discovery date
    Given the consumer reports an unauthorized transfer
    And the consumer cannot establish when the access device
      was lost or stolen
    When the analyst submits the dispute
    Then the platform shall flag the awareness date as unknown
    And the platform shall not automatically classify
      the notification as late

  Scenario: Awareness date is in the future
    Given the analyst enters a consumer awareness date
    When the date is later than the current business date
    Then the platform shall reject the date
    And the platform shall request correction
```

**Required data fields**

- Consumer awareness date.
- Awareness date source.
- Awareness date confidence/status.
- Analyst override indicator.
- Override reason and audit timestamp.

#### **US-003: Calculate the two-business-day notification deadline**

**As a** disputes analyst,\
**I want** the platform to calculate the two-business-day notification deadline using the institution's business calendar,\
**So that** the consumer's notification timeliness is assessed consistently.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Calculate two-business-day notification deadline

  Scenario: Awareness occurs on a Monday
    Given the consumer learns of the loss or theft on Monday
    And Tuesday and Wednesday are business days
    When the platform calculates the notification deadline
    Then the platform shall identify Wednesday
      as the second business day

  Scenario: Awareness occurs before a weekend
    Given the consumer learns of the loss or theft on Friday
    And Saturday and Sunday are non-business days
    And Monday and Tuesday are business days
    When the platform calculates the notification deadline
    Then the platform shall identify Tuesday
      as the second business day

  Scenario: Awareness occurs before a bank holiday
    Given the consumer learns of the loss or theft
      before a recognized bank holiday
    When the platform calculates the notification deadline
    Then the platform shall exclude non-business days
    And the platform shall calculate the deadline
      using the institution's approved business calendar

  Scenario: Notification occurs on the second business day
    Given the consumer learns of the loss or theft on Monday
    And Tuesday and Wednesday are business days
    When the consumer notifies the institution on Wednesday
    Then the notification shall be classified as timely
```

**Implementation considerations**

- Use the financial institution's applicable business-day calendar.
- Exclude weekends and recognized non-business days.
- Maintain the original awareness date and calculated deadline.
- Do not use elapsed calendar hours as a substitute for business-day calculations.
- Ensure that the regulatory deadline is not confused with an internal processing SLA.

### **EPIC 2 — Determine consumer liability based on notification timing**

#### **US-004: Apply the $50 liability limit for timely notification**

**As a** disputes analyst,\
**I want** the platform to cap consumer liability at $50 when notification is provided within two business days,\
**So that** the institution applies the regulatory limitation required by § 1005.6(b)(1).

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Apply $50 liability limit

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers exceed $50
    Given the consumer's access device was lost or stolen
    And the consumer notified the institution within
      two business days after learning of the loss
    And unauthorized transfers before notification total $800
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then maximum consumer liability shall be $50

  Scenario: Unauthorized transfers are less than $50
    Given the consumer notified the institution within
      two business days after learning of the loss
    And unauthorized transfers before notification total $30
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then maximum consumer liability shall be $30

  Scenario: No unauthorized transfers occurred before notification
    Given the consumer notified the institution within
      two business days after learning of the loss
    And no unauthorized transfers occurred before notification
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then maximum consumer liability shall be $0

  Scenario: Notification occurs exactly on the deadline
    Given the consumer notification occurs on the second
      business day after learning of the loss
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the notification shall be treated as timely
    And the maximum liability shall not exceed $50
```

**Calculation rule**

\\[ L = \min(50,T\_{pre}) \\]

#### **US-005: Apply the $500 liability limit for late notification**

**As a** disputes analyst,\
**I want** the platform to calculate consumer liability when notification occurs more than two business days after the consumer learns of the loss or theft,\
**So that** the appropriate limitation under § 1005.6(b)(2) is applied.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Apply $500 liability limit

  Scenario: Late notification with qualifying additional transfers
    Given the consumer learns of the loss on Monday
    And Wednesday is the second business day
    And the consumer notifies the institution on Friday
    And unauthorized transfers before notification total $700
    And $400 of those transfers occurred after Wednesday
    And the institution establishes that those $400
      would not have occurred with timely notification
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the initial liability component shall be $50
    And the additional liability component shall be $400
    And maximum consumer liability shall be $450

  Scenario: Late notification reaches the $500 maximum
    Given the consumer notified the institution
      more than two business days after learning of the loss
    And the initial liability component is $50
    And qualifying additional transfers total $900
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then maximum consumer liability shall be $500

  Scenario: Late notification with no qualifying additional transfers
    Given the consumer notified the institution
      more than two business days after learning of the loss
    And unauthorized transfers total $600
    And no transfers qualify for additional liability
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then maximum consumer liability shall be $50

  Scenario: Additional transfers cannot be linked to delayed notification
    Given the consumer notified the institution late
    And unauthorized transfers occurred after the
      two-business-day period
    And the institution cannot establish that those transfers
      would not have occurred with timely notification
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then those transfers shall not be included
      in the additional liability component
    And the platform shall flag the causation assessment
      for review
```

**Calculation rule**

\\[ L = \min(500,\min(50,T\_{pre})+T\_{late}) \\]

Where \\(T\_{late}\\) includes only qualifying transfers supported by the institution's causation determination.

**Important:** The system must distinguish between transfers occurring after the two-business-day deadline and transfers that the institution can establish would not have occurred had the consumer notified it within that period. These are not automatically equivalent.

**As a** disputes analyst,\
**I want** the platform to track the 60-day notification period after transmission of a periodic statement containing an unauthorized transfer,\

  Scenario: Consumer reports unauthorized transfer within 60 days

      showing an unauthorized transfer
    And the consumer notified the institution within
      60 days after statement transmission

    And the platform shall not apply additional liability

  Scenario: Consumer reports unauthorized transfer after 60 days
    Given the institution transmitted a periodic statement
      showing an unauthorized transfer
    And the consumer did not notify the institution
      within 60 days after statement transmission
    When the platform evaluates notification timeliness
    Then the platform shall flag a potential
      60-day notification violation
    And the platform shall initiate a subsequent
      transfer liability assessment

  Scenario: Consumer reports unauthorized transfer
    And no qualifying periodic statement has been transmitted
    When the platform evaluates the 60-day requirement
    Then the platform shall not start the 60-day period
    And the platform shall flag the statement information
      as incomplete

  Scenario: Consumer notifies institution before the 60-day
    deadline expires
    Given a qualifying statement was transmitted
    And notification was received on the 60th day
    When the platform evaluates notification timeliness

```

**Required data fields**

- Statement transmission date.
- Statement period.
- Unauthorized transaction appearing on statement.
- Statement delivery/transmission evidence.
- 60-day notification deadline.
- Consumer notification date.
- Notification timeliness determination.

#### **US-007: Calculate additional liability for transfers occurring after the 60-day period**

**As a** disputes analyst,\
**I want** the platform to identify subsequent unauthorized transfers occurring after the 60-day notification deadline,\
**So that** additional consumer liability is assessed only where the institution can establish the required causal relationship.

**Acceptance criteria**

```
Feature: Assess additional liability after 60 days

  Scenario: Subsequent transfers qualify for additional liability
    Given the institution transmitted a statement
      showing an unauthorized transfer
    And the consumer failed to notify the institution
      within 60 days
    And additional unauthorized transfers occurred
      after the 60-day deadline
    And the institution establishes that those transfers
      would not have occurred with timely notification
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then the qualifying subsequent transfers shall be
      identified as potential additional consumer liability
    And the platform shall retain the supporting evidence

  Scenario: Subsequent transfers cannot be linked to
    failure to notify
    Given the consumer failed to notify the institution
      within 60 days
    And additional unauthorized transfers occurred
    But the institution cannot establish that timely
      notification would have prevented those transfers
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then those transfers shall not be included
      in additional consumer liability

  Scenario: No transfers occurred after the deadline
    Given the consumer failed to notify the institution
      within 60 days
    And no additional unauthorized transfers occurred
      after the deadline
    When the platform calculates consumer liability
    Then no additional liability shall be assessed
      for subsequent transfers

  Scenario: Subsequent transfers are identified
    as requiring evidence
    Given the consumer failed to notify the institution
      within 60 days
    And subsequent unauthorized transfers are identified
    But causation evidence is unavailable
    When the platform performs the assessment
    Then the case shall be routed for manual review
    And the platform shall not automatically assign
      additional consumer liability
```

**Implementation note:** The 60-day provision is a separate assessment from the initial $50/$500 liability calculation. The platform must support a combined liability assessment without double-counting transfers or applying an unsupported cap or formula to the subsequent-transfer component.

      statement transmission dates
    And calculate the notification period for each
      applicable statement

  Scenario: Duplicate transactions are identified
    Given a disputed transaction appears more than once
      in the case data
    When the platform performs the liability calculation
    Then duplicate records shall not inflate
      the liability amount
    And the platform shall retain the duplicate identification
```

## **7. Recommended end-to-end process flow**

**1. Dispute received**

Capture disputed EFT and consumer notification

**2. Regulation E applicability**

Validate unauthorized EFT and access device conditions

**3. Establish notification timelines**

Determine awareness date, two-business-day deadline and statement-based 60-day deadline

**4. Evaluate transaction history**

Classify transactions before and after applicable deadlines

**5. Validate evidence and causation**

Establish the institution's burden of proof

**6. Calculate consumer liability**

Apply $50 / $500 limits and evaluate additional qualifying liability

**7A. Determination complete**

Save liability result and supporting rationale

**7B. Manual review**

Resolve missing evidence or complex conditions

## **9. Important regulatory interpretation items before development sign-off**

As the business analyst, I would explicitly raise the following items with Legal, Compliance, and Deposit Operations before finalizing the requirements.

1. **Access device applicability:** Confirm how the platform handles unauthorized EFTs involving an access device versus transfers not involving an access device. Do not assume the $50/$500 framework applies universally.
2. **Business-day calculation:** Confirm the institution's interpretation and configuration of business days, including applicable holidays and notification receipt cutoffs.
3. **60-day calculations:** Confirm statement transmission evidence, multiple statement periods, and transaction attribution.
4. **Causation requirements:** Define what evidence is sufficient to establish that a transfer would not have occurred had the consumer notified the institution on time.
5. **Combined liability:** Validate the interaction between the initial liability limitations and subsequent liability under § 1005.6(b)(3), particularly where both provisions are implicated.
6. **Written confirmation:** Confirm operational procedures under § 1005.6(c) and their interaction with § 1005.11.
7. **Other liability exceptions:** Confirm whether additional regulatory provisions or account-specific arrangements affect the determination.

**Primary regulatory references:**

- [12 CFR § 1005.6 — Consumer liability](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1005/section-1005.6?utm_source=chatgpt.com).
- [12 CFR § 1005.11 — Procedures for resolving errors](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1005/section-1005.11?utm_source=chatgpt.com).
- [CFPB Regulation E § 1005.6 and official commentary](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/6/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

## **10. Recommended business acceptance criteria for the overall epic**

The epic should not be considered complete until the following conditions are satisfied.

- The platform correctly determines Regulation E applicability.
- The two-business-day deadline is accurately calculated.
- The $50 liability limit is correctly enforced.
- The $500 liability limit is correctly enforced.
- The 60-day statement notification requirement is supported.
- Subsequent transfers are evaluated using the institution's burden of proof.
- Unsupported liability is not automatically assigned.
- Telephone and written notification channels are supported.
- Written confirmation requirements are properly tracked.
- Transaction-level calculations prevent duplicate counting.
- Manual overrides require justification and authorization.
- All determinations are auditable and reproducible.
- Compliance and Legal have approved the regulatory interpretation.

**My recommendation:** Treat this as a dedicated Regulation E liability decisioning capability rather than embedding the calculations directly into individual dispute workflows. A centralized, version-controlled rules engine will make it easier to maintain regulatory consistency across debit card, ATM, ACH, and other applicable EFT disputes.

*Regulatory qualification: This is a business-analysis requirements baseline, not a legal opinion. The precise treatment of access-device conditions, the interaction of § 1005.6(b)(1)–(3), and the special circumstances under § 1005.6(b)(4) should be validated against the current regulation and official commentary by bank counsel before implementation.*
